\section{Qupit Clifford Circuits and the Main Theorems}\label{sec:background}

Fix an odd prime $p$ and $\omega = e^{2\pi i/p}$.  On the basis
$\ket{0},\dots,\ket{p-1}$ indexed by $\Zp$, $X\ket{j} = \ket{j+1}$ and
$Z\ket{j} = \omega^{j}\ket{j}$ generate, with the phases $\omega^t$, the
Pauli group $\Pauli$, and $H\colon \ket{j} \mapsto
\tfrac{\delta_p}{\sqrt p}\sum_k \omega^{jk}\ket{k}$, $S\colon \ket{j}
\mapsto \omega^{j(j-1)/2}\ket{j}$, $CZ\colon \ket{j}\ket{l} \mapsto
\omega^{jl}\ket{j}\ket{l}$ generate the Clifford group $\Cliff$ ($\delta_p$
makes the Clifford phases exactly the powers of
$\omega$)~\cite{bian2026qudit}.  Modulo scalars, $\PCliff =
\Cliff/\langle\omega\rangle$ and $\PPauli \cong \Zp^{2n}$ $\Cliff/\Pauli \cong \PCliff/\PPauli \cong \Sp$, and $\PCliff \cong
\PPauli \rtimes \Sp$.  These ``glue facts'' are the two not formalised:
our semantic groups are \emph{defined} as the right-hand sides.

$\aid{Circuit}\;n = \aid{Word}\;(\aid{Gen}\;n)$ is the free monoid on
wire-indexed generators over the gates $H$, $S$ (unary) and $CZ$
(binary), and $\aid{{\_}↑}$ shifts a circuit up one wire.  The other
usual gates are \emph{words} ($Z = HHSHHS^{-1}$, $X = HSHHS^{-1}H$, the
swap $\mathit{Ex} = (CZ \cdot H{\downarrow} \cdot H{\uparrow})^3$), as
is the \emph{multiplier} $M_x\ket{j} = \ket{xj}$, $x \in \Zp^{*}$,
spelled by the paper over $S$ with a Pauli prefix (its Lemma~2.14) and
by us mostly as $R^{x}HR^{x^{-1}}HR^{x}H$ with $R = S \cdot Z^{1/2}$.  Our primary rule set --- Figure~1 modulo scalars,
multiplier over $R$, $(SH)^3 = \varepsilon$ kept as an axiom --- is one
inductive family \aid{{\_}CRel,{\_}==={\_}} with sixteen constructors,
six one-qupit (orders, the multiplier calculus \aid{M-power} and \aid{semi-MR}, \aid{comm-HHSHHS}), seven two-qupit (orders of $CZ$ and $\mathit{Ex}$, commutations, the swap rules, \aid{blake-c12}) and three three-qupit (Yang--Baxter, \aid{cz-slide}, \aid{semi-CX↑-CZ↓}) (Figure~\ref{fig:rules}), plus the
three \emph{structural} rules every circuit theory gets free
(congruence under $\uparrow$; gates on disjoint wires commute); $XZ =
ZX$ modulo scalars, $\mathit{Ex}$ commuting with $CZ$ and both rules
pushing a Pauli through $CZ$ are derived.  Figure~1 has fifteen
non-scalar rules; under the $R$-spelling $(SH)^3 = \varepsilon$ must
stay an axiom, and \S\ref{sec:composing} verifies the paper's fifteen
in its $S$-spelling, where it is a theorem.
