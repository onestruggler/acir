\subsection{What constructivity costs}\label{sec:why-agda}

Agda under \texttt{-{}-safe} is constructive. There is no excluded
middle, no axiom of choice and no propositional extensionality, no
quotient types in the plain language, and the standard library has no
real numbers. Lean has choice, propositional extensionality and the
soundness of quotients as axioms~\cite{moura2021lean4}, from which
excluded middle follows, and Mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020}, on which the
proof of Fermat's Last Theorem is built, uses them throughout. The
developments here assume none of this, and \texttt{-{}-safe} would
reject a postulate.

That makes the work harder. A case split must be on a decidable
question, so ``either the entry is odd or it is even'' is a decision
procedure on $\Z[\sqrt2]$, and ``the matrix determines the normal
form'' is a function that reads the normal form off the matrix. Two
functions with the same values are not equal, so operators on bit
vectors are compared pointwise, and every lemma about them carries the
congruence proofs that the standard library's setoid bundles ask for.
And the intended semantics of several papers is $\mathbb{R}$ or
$\mathbb{C}$, which do not exist here; those theorems are proved over
any commutative ring with the properties the paper uses, and
instantiated at the rings the paper computes in. In return, every
proof is a program: with soundness, completeness and decidable
equality of matrices, the word problem of a theory is decided, a
normal-form witness is a normalizer that runs, and the proofs by
evaluation of Section~\ref{sec:eval} are run by the typechecker
itself, with no oracle outside it to trust.

\subsection{What makes Agda slow}\label{sec:slow}

Most of what we learned about Agda in this work is about what makes it
slow, or makes it run out of memory, and none of it is specific to
these papers: it comes from proving things about large explicit
objects, matrices and long words, that Agda can also compute with.
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-agda} describes the six problems we met and
what we did about each: the conversion checker compares terms by
unfolding them, so readings of circuits and the ring operations are
\aid{abstract} or \aid{opaque}; unification cannot invert $2^n$,
products or powers, so such arguments are given explicitly; a
\aid{with} normalizes its goal, so decisions about matrices and levels
are taken in helper functions; a rewrite loop of the ported tactics
was exponential; memory is kept across modules, so evaluation-heavy
modules are split; and generated code needs the same care.
