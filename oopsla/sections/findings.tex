\subsection{A gap in the completeness proof of Hadamard-\texorpdfstring{$\Pi$}{Pi}}\label{sec:gap}

Hadamard-$\Pi$~\cite{fang2026hadamard} proves the completeness of its
Figure~6 (Theorem~4.14) by the method of Section~\ref{sec:by-levels},
with the level $(j,k,\ell)$ of a matrix its pivot column, the least
denominator exponent of that column, and the number of odd entries of
$\sqrt2^k$ times it; the odd entries come in two residue classes
modulo 2, $1$ and $1+\sqrt2$, each of even size, and Algorithm~1
applies $H$ to the first two odd entries of one class. The Main Lemma
(Lemma~A.7) is split into Main Cases by the generator $G$ and the
syllable $N$. Main Case~9, where $G=H_{[1,2]}$ and $N=H_{[1,i_2]}$, takes
nine pages in the extended version, and in one of its subcases it
applies Lemma~A.19, which gives a path between two states that stays
below a promised level; in the case $\ell=4$, sub-case (2) of that
lemma, items (c) and (d) rely on Lemmas~A.16 and A.17.

These two lemmas are false. Lemma~A.16 takes a state at level $(j,k,4)$
with $k\ge2$ whose first four entries $v$ of column $j$ satisfy
$\sqrt2^k v \equiv (1+\sqrt2,1,1+\sqrt2,1)$ or
$(1,1+\sqrt2,1,1+\sqrt2) \pmod 2$, and says that if one word of five
$H$'s brings $\sqrt2^{k-1}$ times the column into $\Z[\sqrt2]$, then
so does a second. The unit column
\[
\sqrt2^4\cdot M_{\cdot,6} = (-1-\sqrt2,\; 1,\; -1-\sqrt2,\; 1,\; \sqrt2,\; -2+\sqrt2),
\]
at level $(6,4,4)$, is a counterexample: the first word does and the
second does not. The proof compares the $\sqrt2$-parts of the norm
equation and drops a $+1$ from the bracket it reduces modulo~2, and
with the $+1$ the case it rules out is possible. Lemma~A.17 has the
same hypotheses with $H_{[3,4]}$ in place of $H_{[2,4]}$, ends its
proof by appeal to the argument of Lemma~A.16, and fails in the same
way, on the column $(1+\sqrt2,-1,1+\sqrt2,1,-\sqrt2,2-\sqrt2)/\sqrt2^4$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:gap-details} states both lemmas in full and checks
both columns.

A false lemma does not make the Main Lemma false, since Lemma~A.19 only
needs some path, and there might be one. So we looked for a state on
which the proof itself fails, by sampling random states and following
the case analysis of the Main Lemma on each
(Appendix~\ref{sec:gap-details}). One is the state $s$ with
\[
\sqrt2^7\cdot s_{\cdot,9} = (1+3\sqrt2,\; 1,\; -3\sqrt2,\; 1+3\sqrt2,\; 2,\; 5,\; -2+\sqrt2,\; -4+2\sqrt2,\; -2-2\sqrt2)
\]
at level $(9,7,4)$. Algorithm~1 takes $N=H_{[1,4]}$, and the proof of
Main Case~9 goes to Lemma~A.19 with $t_2=H_{[2,6]}H_{[1,4]}s$ at level
$(9,6,4)$ and $(a,b,c,d)=(1,2,4,6)$, in its sub-case (2), where only
item (d) of Lemma~A.15 holds. Main Case~9 needs a path from $t_2$ to
$t_3$ strictly below $(9,7,4)$, and every one of the eight diagrams of
Lemma~A.19, started at $t_2$ with these labels, reaches $(9,7,4)$ or
$(9,8,4)$. So the proof of the Main Lemma does not cover this state,
and the proof of Theorem~4.14 is incomplete.

What is left open is the edge given by $H$ on two odd entries of
different classes, when exactly four entries of the pivot column are
odd. Cl\'ement's 2019 thesis~\cite{clement2019thesis} presents the same
group with the same generators by its own relations (1)--(21), with a
completeness proof by the same method, and his Case~3.4 is exactly
this edge. We did two things with it. First, we proved that his
relations and those of Figure~6 generate the same congruence in every
dimension, his (20) and (21) on four and six indices being equivalent
to (d3) and (d4) given the others, by generated chains of rewrites on
the first indices carried to any indices by an increasing embedding.
Second, we formalized his Case~3.4 and used it for the open edge. On the state $s$ above, his
diagram~(32), on rows 1, 2, 4, 6 and the further rows 3 and 5, gives a
path that never rises above the level of $s$. The general argument is a
case analysis over the residues of four entries, extended in some
sub-cases by up to three further rows of given depth and class, and
closed by his diagrams~(20) and~(30); we wrote it as a decision tree
that Agda checks by evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:trees}). With it, the
relations of Figure~6 present $O_n(\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2}])$ in every
dimension.

After that we formalized the rest of the thesis: his version of the
algorithm, in which every syllable is a single generator, his
Lemma~4.4 for the basic generators in all its cases, and his
Theorem~4.1, supplying two steps the thesis leaves implicit, the
reduction from all generators to the basic ones and the induction
itself. So the theorem now has two machine-checked proofs, one along
Hadamard-$\Pi$ with Cl\'ement's case, and one along Cl\'ement.
Cl\'ement's 2026 paper on real Clifford+$CH$
circuits~\cite{clement2026realcliffordch} imports Theorem~4.14 of
Hadamard-$\Pi$ as its own Theorem~4.4, for circuits on two qubits and,
through its Theorem~4.10, on three and more; Section~\ref{sec:encoding}
explains how the two developments are connected so that its
completeness is proved with no imported statement. The two
counterexamples and the state $s$ are small enough to check by hand;
the errata are provided as supplementary material.

\subsection{Our own errors}\label{sec:own-errors}

The formalization found errors in our transcriptions as well, and some
of them are instructive.

\paragraph{A false hypothesis.} The real Clifford+$CH$ development was
first written with Theorem~4.4 as a module parameter, stated as
completeness of the relations of Figure~7 for every word over the
generators $(-1)_{[a]}$, $X_{[a,b]}$, $H_{[a,b]}$ with indices in
$\mathsf{Fin}\,N$. That statement is false. The type of generators
admits $X_{[a,a]}$, which denotes the identity, but every rule has
distinct indices, so the number of such letters is invariant under the
congruence and $X_{[a,a]}$ is not related to the empty word. Every
theorem proved from the hypothesis was vacuous. We noticed only when we
tried to discharge it. The fix restricts completeness to proper words,
whose letters have distinct indices, as the paper intends; the encoding
was then shown to produce only proper words. A hypothesis that nobody
discharges is not checked by anything.

\paragraph{A bug invisible at small sizes.} Our transcription of the
swap networks of Cl\'ement's Definition~8.2 took two products over the
wires between $i$ and $j$ in the wrong order. On three qubits there is
at most one wire in between, so the two orders agree and the decision
procedure that checks Figure~8 there could not see the difference; on
four qubits the transcribed (36) and (37) are unsound. We found it when
proving (65) at every width, which needs exactly this permutation. And
a control dot of equation (261), misread at screen resolution, cost a
proof of a true but harder statement before the one the paper means.
