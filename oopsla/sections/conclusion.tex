\section{What Is Not Formalized, and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The theorems of Table~\ref{tab:corpus} are proved as stated there. Some
of the papers prove more, and some of our statements are weaker than
the papers' in ways a reader should know.

\begin{itemize}
\item Greylyn's presentation of $U_4(\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2},i])$ is proved
  for $n\le4$, as in the thesis, and not for larger $n$.
\item The three-qubit Clifford+$CS$ result says that the circuit
  relations present the group of matrices that the circuits generate,
  inside $U_8(\Z[\frac12,i])$. The paper also uses that this group has
  index 2 in $U_8(\Z[\frac12,i])$, which it takes
  from~\cite{amy2020numbertheoretic}. We do not formalize the index.
  The coset table with two cosets enters our proof only through the
  hypotheses of the Reidemeister--Schreier theorem, which give
  injectivity and say nothing about the index.
\item The qudit Clifford theorems are stated against an algebraic
  model of the Clifford group (Section~\ref{sec:qupit}). The Weyl
  representation, which identifies the model with the group of unitary
  matrices, is not formalized.
\item Blake's simpler presentations~\cite{blake2026simpler} are
  formalized for soundness and minimality. Their completeness, by
  translation from the earlier presentations, is not.
\item For Hadamard-$\Pi$ we did not repair Lemma~A.19 of the paper. We
  replaced the case that uses it by Cl\'ement's argument. Whether the
  diagrams of Lemma~A.19 can be fixed in place is open.
\item Some semantic statements are proved over abstract rings with
  stated properties, because Agda has no real numbers. The count of
  real stabilizer operators (Corollary~4.16 of~\cite{makary2021real}) is
  proved over any nontrivial commutative ring with an element $s$ such
  that $2s^2=1$, in which $1$ and $-1$ are the only square roots of $1$.
  The paper means $\mathbb{R}$. We instantiate the corollary only at
  $\mathbb{F}_7$, with $s=2$, which shows that the hypotheses are
  consistent. The presentation theorems themselves are instantiated at
  the rings of the papers.
\end{itemize}

The trust base is Agda~2.8, the standard library and the modules of
EucDomain we import, all checked under \texttt{-{}-safe} with no
postulates; everything is constructive (Section~\ref{sec:why-agda}).
What Agda cannot check is that our definitions mean what the papers
mean. We wrote the semantics of each development separately, from the
paper, and except in the qudit Clifford development checked it against
the paper's matrices by computation. In three places two developments
are connected, and the connection is a check on both: the real
stabilizer group inside Hadamard-$\Pi$'s orthogonal group
(Section~\ref{sec:theorem-form}), Bian and Selinger's ports against the
two-level presentations, and Cl\'ement's real Clifford+$CH$ paper
against Hadamard-$\Pi$ (Section~\ref{sec:encoding}). The other
developments are checked only against their papers.

We checked fourteen papers on the completeness and minimality of
equations for quantum circuits and related matrix groups. One proof
had a gap. We closed it with an argument from another source, and so
discharged the import of the later paper that relied on it. The other
papers were correct up to misprints, though some of the misprints were
in statements that later proofs use. Most of the work was engineering:
turning case analyses and derivations into data that Agda can check,
and keeping Agda within time and memory. An agent wrote most of the
code under our direction, and Agda checked it. What was left for people
was to check the scaffold once and a short statement per paper; what
an author of a new theory writes is the mathematics of the paper's
route.

We think that long computational proofs of this kind can now come with
a machine-checked version, and that the cost has moved from writing
proofs to checking statements. Scaffolds made by people, on which
language models build, are one way to keep that cost small, for the
reader who checks a statement and for the author who writes the next
one.
