\section{What Is Not Formalized, and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The theorems of Table~\ref{tab:corpus} are proved as stated there, and
some are weaker than the papers' in ways a reader should know.
Greylyn's presentation of $U_4(\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2},i])$ is proved for
$n\le4$, as in the thesis. The three-qubit Clifford+$CS$ result says
that the circuit relations present the group that the circuits
generate inside $U_8(\Z[\frac12,i])$; that this group has index 2
there, which the paper takes from~\cite{amy2020numbertheoretic}, is
not formalized. The qudit Clifford theorems are stated against an
algebraic model of the Clifford group (Section~\ref{sec:qupit}), the
Weyl representation being left out. Blake's simpler
presentations~\cite{blake2026simpler} are formalized for soundness,
minimality and necessity, and for completeness in the Clifford and
CNOT-dihedral fragments only. For Hadamard-$\Pi$ we
replaced the case that uses Lemma~A.19 by Cl\'ement's argument;
whether its diagrams can be fixed in place is open. And some semantic
statements are proved over abstract rings with stated properties,
because Agda has no real numbers: the count of real stabilizer
operators (Corollary~4.16 of~\cite{makary2021real}) is proved over any
nontrivial commutative ring with $2s^2=1$ whose only square roots of
$1$ are $\pm1$, and instantiated only at $\mathbb{F}_7$; the
presentation theorems are instantiated at the rings of the papers.

The trust base is Agda~2.8, the standard library and the modules of
EucDomain we import, all checked under \texttt{-{}-safe} with no
postulates; everything is constructive (Section~\ref{sec:why-agda}).
What Agda cannot check is that our definitions mean what the papers
mean. We wrote the semantics of each development separately, from the
paper, and except in the qudit Clifford development checked it against
the paper's matrices by computation. Where two developments are
connected, the connection checks both: the real stabilizer group
inside Hadamard-$\Pi$'s orthogonal group, the ports against the
two-level presentations, and real Clifford+$CH$ against
Hadamard-$\Pi$.

We checked fourteen papers on the completeness and minimality of
equations for quantum circuits and related matrix groups. One proof
had a gap, which we closed with an argument from another source, and
so discharged the import of the later paper that relied on it; the
others were correct up to misprints. Most of the work was engineering:
turning case analyses and derivations into data that Agda can check,
and keeping Agda within time and memory. An agent wrote most of the
code under our direction, and Agda checked it. What was left for people
was to check the scaffold once and a short statement per paper, and
for an author of a new theory to write the mathematics of its route.
Long computational proofs of this kind can now come with a
machine-checked version; the cost has moved from writing proofs to
checking statements, and scaffolds made by people, on which language
models build, are one way to keep it small.
