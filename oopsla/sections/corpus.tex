group; a \emph{sub-presentation} means that they present the subgroup,

\subsection{Two-level matrix groups}

Four of the sources present groups of two-level matrices by exact
synthesis, and the fifth (Hadamard-$\Pi$) does too. We formalized each
of them with the same skeleton: the ring and its operations, the action
of a generator on a column, the algorithm and its correctness, the
level and the proof that a syllable lowers it, and the Main Lemma as a
function that returns the path of every edge out of a state at a level
$L$ that does not go up, given the edges below $L$
(Section~\ref{sec:levels}).

Greylyn's thesis~\cite{greylyn2014} presents $U_4(\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2},i])$,
the group of two-qubit Clifford+$T$ operators. Our development states the
relations for every $n$ and proves completeness for $n\le4$, as the
thesis does. Bian and Selinger~\cite{bian2021un} present
$U_n(\Z[\frac12,i])$, the group behind Clifford+$CS$, for every $n$.
Li, Ross and Selinger~\cite{li2021on} present $O_n(\Z[\frac12])$ with the
generators $(-1)_{[a]}$, $X_{[a,b]}$ and $K_{[a,b,c,d]}=H\otimes H$, and,
in their Section~5, the group $L_n$ of scaled dyadic orthogonal
matrices, which adds $I\otimes H$. We formalized both. Their normal
syllables carry four signs. Ours carry one, which is enough for $K$ to
reduce the four entries it acts on and removes most sign cases from the
Main Lemma; a separate module relates the two kinds of syllable.

Hadamard-$\Pi$~\cite{fang2026hadamard} presents
$O_n(\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2}])$ by the generators $Z_{[a]}$, $X_{[a,b]}$ and
$H_{[a,b]}$ and the relations (a1)--(d4) of its Figure~6. We formalized
generation, Algorithm~1 and its correctness, soundness, and the Main
Lemma up to one case, which the paper's proof does not cover. The
theorem takes that case as a hypothesis. Cl\'ement's thesis of
2019~\cite{clement2019thesis} presents the same group with relations
(1)--(21). We proved that the two relation sets generate the same
congruence in every dimension, proved the missing case after his
Case~3.4, and then formalized his whole proof over his own version of
the algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:gap} tells this story.

\subsection{Circuits}

Selinger's Clifford circuits~\cite{selinger2015clifford} and Makary,
Ross and Selinger's real stabilizer circuits~\cite{makary2021real} are
proved complete by normal forms. Both papers come with a supplement of
derivations, which we followed line by line
(Section~\ref{sec:search}). Both also count their normal forms, and so
the order of the group: $8\prod_{i=1}^n 2(4^i-1)4^i$ for Clifford and
$2\prod_{i=1}^n (4^i+2^i-2)(2\cdot4^{i-1})$ for real stabilizers. We
proved these counts as bijections between the normal forms and
$\mathsf{Fin}$ of the order, and, for the real case, that every
orthogonal matrix that normalizes the Pauli group is the matrix of a
circuit, over any ring whose only square roots of $1$ are $\pm1$.

Amy, Chen and Ross~\cite{amy2018cnotdihedral} present the CNOT-dihedral
operators, generated by $\omega$, $X$, $T$ and $CNOT$. Blake's
phase-affine circuits~\cite{blake2026phaseaffine} form four levels in
each prime dimension $d$: affine circuits, then phases, then $S$, then
$T$, the last two for odd $d$ and $d>3$. Both proofs go by induction on
the number of wires, splitting a circuit into a diagonal part, a layer
of $X$ gates and a linear part (Section~\ref{sec:width}). The proof
of~\cite{amy2018cnotdihedral} builds on known presentations of linear
reversible circuits; our development proves that part too, by a coset
tower in the style of Lafont~\cite{lafont2003boolean}.

Bian and Selinger's papers on two-qubit Clifford+$T$ and three-qubit
Clifford+$CS$ circuits~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2,bian2023cliffordcs3}
came with Agda code. That code proves only that the circuit relations
and a set of two-level relations generate the same congruence; it
does not mention matrices. We ported it to our library, which took a
change in its rewriting tactic for Agda~2.8
(Section~\ref{sec:engineering}), and connected it to the two-level
presentations above at $n=4$ and $n=8$. The result is the semantic
theorem: the circuit relations present the image of the circuits in
$U_4$ and $U_8$.

Cl\'ement's theory of real Clifford+$CH$
circuits~\cite{clement2026realcliffordch} is proved complete width by
width. On one qubit it uses a normal form; on two qubits it encodes
circuits as words over two-level matrices and back, and uses
completeness of those (its Theorem~4.4, imported from
Hadamard-$\Pi$); on three or more qubits it encodes them in a group $P$
of pairs of two-level matrices, whose relations (its Figure~8) are
proved complete in an appendix by the Reidemeister--Schreier method
(its Theorem~4.10). The paper numbers its equations up to (361), and
most of them are auxiliary equations for the decoding, proved by
induction on the width with completeness one width down as a black
box. All of it is formalized, and Section~\ref{sec:encoding} describes how the
imported theorem is discharged.

\subsection{Minimality and independence}\label{sec:structural}

Blake~\cite{blake2026simpler} gives smaller presentations for six
fragments of near-Clifford circuits and proves them minimal: no rule
follows from the others. The proofs use separating interpretations, a
model in which all rules but one hold. We formalized the six fragments
with their matrix semantics, soundness at every width, and all the
separating interpretations of the minimality theorem. The completeness
part of that paper, a translation from the earlier presentations, is
not formalized.

We used the same method on our own circuit layer. Its four structural
rules (Section~\ref{sec:presentations}) are independent: for each rule
there is a family of monoids, one per width, in which the other three
hold and that one fails. The models record how many times each gate
has been shifted up. A fifth rule that the library used to have, that a scalar
commutes with every gate, is derivable from the four when scalars
commute with each other. With two scalars it is not, and a fifth model
shows it.

\subsection{What the theorems look like}

Every development ends in a module that states its theorem in the
library's terms, without hypotheses. For example, the real stabilizer
development concludes
\begin{agdacode}
presentation : {n : ℕ} → (n VRel,_===_) IsSubPresentationOf U (2 ^ n)
\end{agdacode}
where \aid{U} is the unitary group over any nontrivial
commutative ring with an involution and an element $s$ with $s s + s s
= 1$ fixed by it. A second module instantiates this at
$\D[\sqrt2]=\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2}]$ and $s=\frac1{\sqrt2}$, which puts the
real stabilizer group inside the orthogonal group of
Hadamard-$\Pi$. Because that group is presented by Figure~6, every real
Clifford circuit translates to a two-level word with the same matrix,
and two circuits are equal by the circuit relations exactly when their
translations are equal by Figure~6. The two developments, written
separately, thus check each other.
