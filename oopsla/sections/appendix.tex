\section{Misprints and Slips in Detail}\label{sec:appendix-errata}

This appendix describes the errors of Table~\ref{tab:findings} other
than the gap, which Section~\ref{sec:gap} describes. Each is a
statement that is false as printed and easy to correct once a
computation shows it is false, or a wrong step or citation inside a
proof whose conclusion is right. We list them because several are in
statements that a later proof uses, and a reader who builds on the
printed version would build on something false.

\subsection{Misprinted statements}\label{sec:misprints}

\paragraph{Real stabilizer circuits.} The normal form of Makary, Ross and
Selinger~\cite{makary2021real} is pushed through by 137 \emph{typed
relations}, listed in their Appendix~B, one for each gate and each kind
of normal-form wire. Eleven of them are false as printed: both sides
have different matrices, and do not differ only by a sign. Six have a
wrong right-hand side; for example the paper prints $X;C_1 = C_1;Z$
where $X;C_1=C_2$ holds, and $H;A_3 = A_2;H$ where $A_3$ is meant on the
right. The other five lack a Pauli gate at the end, such as $Z_t;B_2 =
B_2;X_t$ for $Z_t;B_2 = B_2;X_t;Z_b$. Six derivations in the supplement
reach one of these false conclusions. The formalization proves the
corrected relations, and the normal form, the uniqueness argument and
the count of Corollary~4.16 go through with them. Two smaller problems
in the paper are an equation printed without its ``='' in Figure~10,
and Definition~5.3 of the normal forms, which does not allow a $CZ$
whose top wire is a single wire labelled 2, although the left-hand
sides of rules 5.1--5.4 have exactly such a gate.

\paragraph{The group $O_n(\Z[\frac12])$.} Table~2 of Li, Ross and
Selinger~\cite{li2021on} lists the relations of the group $L_n$ that
mention $I\otimes H$. In relation (7d) the right-hand sides for odd and
for even $a$ are exchanged: for $a$ odd (counting from 1) the right
side is $(-1)_{[a+1]}$, and for $a$ even it is
$X_{[a,a+1]}K_{[a-1,a,a+1,a+2]}$. We found this when the relation
failed to check numerically, and our development uses the corrected
form.

\paragraph{Simpler presentations.} Figure~4 of Blake's
paper~\cite{blake2026simpler} gives the scalar of the qutrit rule (E) as
$\omega_{12}^7$. With the semantics of its Section~3.2 the rule is then
unsound; with $\omega_{12}$ it is sound. Separately, one of the
separating interpretations used for the minimality of the Clifford
rule (CZ) does not satisfy the three-wire rule (I). The minimality
claim survives, because a derivation of a two-wire equation never needs
a rule on three wires (the paper's Remark~14); we prove that remark and
use it.

\paragraph{Real Clifford+$CH$ circuits.} The equations (36) and (37) of
Cl\'ement's paper, which write a pair of Hadamards through a network of
swaps, hold with the negations $\mathcal{E}^x$ of the controls, not
with $\mathcal{E}^{1-x}$ as printed. The rules (30), (34) and (42) of its
Figure~8 need distinctness conditions on their indices that the paper
assumes by convention but does not state. We found these by deciding
every rule of Figure~8 on three qubits, at every admissible choice of
indices, before formalizing it.

\subsection{Slips in derivations}\label{sec:slips}

Some derivations cite the wrong rule for a step whose conclusion is
right. In the supplement of Selinger's Clifford
paper~\cite{selinger2015clifford}, which has 105 pages, the proof of
Lemma~1.3 says that property (19) is relation (C10) where it is (C12),
and two steps in the proof of (21) are labelled (11), a rule that cannot
reorder two $CZ$ gates, where they use (19). These are the only three
wrong citations among all the derivations of its Lemmas 1.1--1.3 and
equations (3.1)--(3.101), and the paper's figures all check. The
supplement of the real stabilizer paper has five misprinted
intermediate rows, four wrong axiom labels, and forty citations of
typed relations by an older figure numbering. In Hadamard-$\Pi$, the
proof of Lemma A.19(d) cites Propositions A.28 and A.29 where it needs
a relation stated nowhere in the paper and the relation of A.28;
Proposition A.29 repeats A.22. The missing relation holds, and follows
from Proposition A.26 as A.28 follows from A.27.

A rewriter that follows the paper's own steps (Section~\ref{sec:search})
fails at exactly such a step, and the failure points at the line. This
is how the wrong citations in the two supplements were found.

\section{What Makes Agda Slow, in Detail}\label{sec:appendix-agda}

Section~\ref{sec:slow} summarizes the six problems below.

\paragraph{The evaluator compares by unfolding.} When Agda checks that
two terms are equal and they are not syntactically equal, it unfolds
definitions until they are. For two readings of a 40-gate circuit as
operators, unfolding means computing a sum over every intermediate
basis vector, which is exponential in the circuit. This happens for
reasons that are invisible in the source, such as an implicit width
elaborated as \aid{3} in one place and \aid{₃₊ 0} in another. The fix in
the real Clifford+$CH$ development was to make the reading of a circuit
\aid{abstract}, with its defining equations exported as lemmas, so that
two readings are compared by comparing the circuits. The ring
operations of $\Z[\frac1{\sqrt2}]$ are records of dyadic fractions, and
comparing two expressions over them unfolds the arithmetic on stuck
terms; we made them \aid{opaque} and do arithmetic with a ring solver
over an abstract commutative ring, unfolding them only inside
\aid{opaque unfolding} blocks where a concrete value is needed.

\paragraph{Unification cannot invert.} Many functions in these
developments are not injective: $2^n$, multiplication, the power
$\sqrt2^\ell$, the reading of a matrix. An implicit argument that occurs
only under such a function is never solved, and Agda may reduce
operators for a long time looking for it; in one case this used
9.6\,GB. We give such arguments explicitly, and state lemmas so that
their arguments occur in positions that unification can read.

\paragraph{Pattern matching can force normalization.} A \aid{with} that
abstracts a term out of a goal normalizes the goal. When the goal
mentions a matrix with symbolic entries, or a large $\Sigma$-type such
as a level, this exhausted the heap several times. The same happened
when we compared the levels of two convertible but syntactically
different states: Agda computed the pivots of both. In the qudit
Clifford development, abstracting the map that lifts a derivation out of a goal
at a symbolic prime used more than 13\,GB; taking the value apart in a
helper function instead checks in 90 seconds. We split such cases
with helper functions that take the decision as an argument, cast
between levels along \aid{refl} with both matrices given explicitly, and
avoided records whose fields mention levels, since the positivity
checker normalizes their types.

\paragraph{A rewrite loop that was exponential.} Bian and Selinger's
tactic library, which we ported, rewrites a word to normal form by a
loop that, when no rule applies, returns its argument. Under Agda~2.8,
returning the argument rather than the list the loop has just rebuilt
makes a normal form nested $k$ levels deep cost $2^k$ steps; the
three-qubit Clifford+$CS$ proof nests 20 levels. Returning the rebuilt
list made one step of that proof go from running out of memory to 13
seconds, and made four of the five modules that the original checks
with call-by-name evaluation check with the default strategy; the fifth
followed once the coset normal forms were computed incrementally.

\paragraph{Memory is kept across modules.} Agda keeps every module it
has checked in memory, and frees the memory used by evaluation only
when a module is done. Two consequences followed. Modules that decide
many instances by evaluation, such as the check of Figure~8 of the real
Clifford+$CH$ paper on three qubits, must be split, a few thousand
instances per module; in one module, all of them exhausted 10\,GB. And
the largest developments did not fit a 3\,GB heap in one process from
scratch. We check such developments with a script that reruns Agda,
and checks on its own each module at which a run runs out of memory;
each module but a handful fits in 3\,GB on its own.

\paragraph{Generated code needs care too.} A single generated module of
6{,}000 lines, the derivations of the Clifford supplement, exhausted the
heap; split into one module per figure it checks. A chain of 101
rewriting steps written as one term exhausted 4\,GB; split into
segments of six steps it checks in 16 seconds. And the lazy power
$\sqrt2^{\,n}$, written as repeated multiplication, is a tree of size
$2^n$ to the conversion checker; computing it level by level, forcing
each intermediate value, removed the problem.
