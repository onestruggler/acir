Language models now write machine-checked proofs of millions of lines
in days. Checking that a statement, and the definitions under it, say
what was meant still needs a person, and on generated code that is
slow. We study how to keep that check small in one area of quantum
computing, the completeness of equational theories for circuits, where
papers come with appendices of dozens or hundreds of pages and all
share one semantics: a circuit is a word over gates and denotes a
matrix.

Our earlier Agda library fixed the syntactic half of such a theorem
once: words, circuits with their structural rules, and what it means
for a relation set to present a group, with any monoid or group as the
semantics. Here we add the semantic half, a layer of matrix semantics
over the rings these papers use, and a framework for proving rules
independent. Every completeness theorem then has one form with two
parameters: a relation set over the library's words, in its circuit
syntax for circuits, and a group of matrices taken from the layer, or
an algebraic model of one. A reader learns the form once and checks,
per paper, the relations, the gate matrices and a one-line theorem; for
real stabilizer circuits that is under 500 of 12{,}010 lines. An author
of a new theory supplies the same two parameters and the mathematics of
one of four proof routes: a normal form, the main lemma of an exact
synthesis algorithm, an induction on the width, or an encoding. The
derivations and case analyses that a paper gives as computations are
generated by scripts, and soundness is decided by evaluation.

On this scaffold we formalized the completeness theorems of fifteen
papers written between 2014 and 2026 and the minimality theorem of a
sixteenth. LLM coding agents wrote fifteen of the sixteen developments
under our direction, within four weeks; the qudit Clifford development
was written mainly by hand. Together they are about 420{,}000 lines of
safe, constructive Agda with no postulates, and typecheck in about 40
minutes. Most of the proofs are correct up to misprints, which we list.
One is not: the
completeness proof for Figure~6 of Hadamard-$\Pi$ (POPL 2026) relies on
two false lemmas, and its Main Lemma fails on an explicit state in
dimension~9. The theorem is true. We prove it by formalizing the
corresponding case of an unpublished 2019 thesis of Cl\'ement, and then
the whole thesis, and so discharge the import on which Cl\'ement's 2026
completeness theorem for real Clifford+$CH$ circuits rested.
