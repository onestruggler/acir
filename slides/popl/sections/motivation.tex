\begin{frame}{Completeness proofs are gigantic computations}
\begin{columns}[c]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
A rule set for circuits is \alert{complete} when it derives every true
equation:
\[
  \llbracket w\rrbracket = \llbracket v\rrbracket \;\Longrightarrow\; w \approx v .
\]
Soundness is a finite check. Completeness is not:
\begin{itemize}
  \item define a \alert{normal form};
  \item show every rule, meeting every shape of normal form, reduces again
        --- mechanical, unforgiving, enormous;
  \item show distinct normal forms mean distinct things.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{This paper}
\begin{columns}[c]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\small
An \textbf{Agda library} for presentations, normal forms, and completeness ---
with \alert{circuits} as the motivating instance.
\begin{itemize}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item a circuit is a \alert{word}; a rule set is a \alert{datatype};
        the theory is their \alert{inductive congruence};
  \item a normal form is a \alert{function} with a \alert{section};
  \item \textbf{completeness = soundness + unique normal form}, proved once;
  \item normal forms grow \alert{one wire at a time} by verified \alert{coset
        tables}, whose obligations often close by \aid{refl}.
\end{itemize}
\small Plain, setoid-based Agda on the standard library; \texttt{--safe}.
\end{column}
