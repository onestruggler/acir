# QCert in Lean

A Lean 4 formalization of the completeness and minimality results of
Table 1 of `qcert.pdf`, written from the source papers (independently of
the Agda development in this repository).  Lean 4.30.0, Mathlib pinned in
`lakefile.lean`.

## Building

The project builds in WSL (Linux paths; `.lake` lives on the Linux file
system):

```sh
./check.sh QCert/Some/Module.lean   # one module and its dependencies
./check.sh                          # the whole library
./check.sh QCert/Audit.lean          # axioms of the Table 1 results
```

On a fresh machine, `lake exe cache get` fetches the prebuilt Mathlib.

`QCert.lean` imports `QCert/Table1.lean`, which includes all sixteen rows.
A successful build alone does not establish completeness: Lean accepts
`sorry`. The audit prints `sorryAx` for a result with an unfinished proof
anywhere in its dependencies. Independent invocations of `check.sh` are
serialized to avoid races on Lake's output files.

## Framework

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `QCert/Presentation` | Words (`FreeMonoid`), the congruence `Derivable Γ` generated by axioms, `IsSubPresentationOf Γ ⟦·⟧` (sound, complete, every generator invertible: Γ presents the subgroup the generators generate) and `IsPresentationOf` (plus surjective: Γ presents the whole group), with the quotient isomorphisms they give. The semantics is part of each statement. |
| `QCert/Circuit/Basic` | Circuits over a gate family `Gate : ℕ → Type`: generators are gates on the bottom wires and shifts of generators (`Gen`); `Lift R` closes a rule set under the structural rules (shift, disjoint gates commute, scalars); `Padded Base` applies rules stated on their own wires at every width. |
| `QCert/Circuit/Semantics` | Operators on basis states `Fin n → Fin d` (wire 0 is the bottom wire, the least significant digit); gates on the bottom wires and shifts as Kronecker products (`onBottom`, `onTop`); a gate set's matrices (`GateSem`) and the semantics of circuits; soundness of the structural rules and of padding for every gate set. |
| `QCert/Circuit/Eval` | Evaluation over any commutative ring, commuting with ring homomorphisms: an equation decided over a computable ring (`decide +kernel`) holds in the unitary semantics. |
| `QCert/Matrix/Level` | m-level matrices `1 + E (M - 1) Eᵀ` and their unitarity. |
| `QCert/Matrix/Rings` | ℤ[1/√2, i], ℤ[1/2, i] ⊆ ℂ and ℤ[1/√2], ℤ[1/2] ⊆ ℝ, and the unitary (orthogonal) matrices with entries in them. |
| `QCert/Exact/*` | `Quartic p q` = ℚ[x]/(x⁴ - p x² - q), computable, with homomorphisms to ℂ and ℝ (ω = e^{iπ/4}, √2). |

The framework has no `sorry`. No development uses `native_decide`.
Completed proofs use only Lean's standard axioms; unfinished results
additionally depend on `sorryAx`, as reported by the audit.

Thirteen rows currently have complete proofs without `sorryAx`. The open
rows are three-qubit Clifford+CS, real Clifford+CH, and the Clifford+CS
completeness dependency in the final minimality row.

## Typechecking cost

Nearly every slow module here is a computation checked by `decide +kernel`
(certificate replays, matrix products, window checks), so two costs
matter, and `lake env lean --profile -Dprofiler=true -Dprofiler.threshold=1000 QCert/X.lean`
(read-only, imports must be built) tells them apart in its summary:
`type checking` is the kernel, `elaboration` is term elaboration.

* The kernel pays per unfolding, not per arithmetic operation: `Nat`
  arithmetic on literals is GMP-accelerated and nearly free, `Int`, `Rat`,
  ring structures and `Fin`-indexed matrices are not.  Write the checked
  function for the kernel: precompute per-rule or per-gate data once,
  look things up by primitive recursion (`List.rec`, `Nat.rec`; mark the
  definition `noncomputable` if the compiler objects), test with
  `Nat.beq`/`Nat.ble` under `bif` rather than `if` on propositions, decide
  an equation of large structures by one Boolean function with a soundness
  lemma, not by `DecidableEq`.  `QCert/CliffordT2/FastRewrite.lean` is the
  worked example (3-4x).
* A dotted constructor application `.ctor args` elaborated before its
  expected type is known costs about 0.1 s per failed attempt (it is
  postponed and retried; `set_option trace.profiler true` shows
  `💥 expected type: ?m, term .CZ 0`).  That happens to the elements of a
  list literal of 64 or more elements, to operands of `++` and other
  `binop%` operators, and to arguments whose type is a metavariable.
  Qualify the names (`Step.rw 5 1 false`, `Lt.CZ 0`): a 675-element list
  went from 52 s to 0.16 s, `RealStabilizer/Relations1` from 190 s to 9 s,
  `Minimality/QutritClifford/Derivations` from 328 s to 37 s.  Nullary
  letters (`[.ω, .S₀, …]`) and constructors in positions with a known type
  are fine.
