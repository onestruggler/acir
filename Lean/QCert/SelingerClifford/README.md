# Selinger, *Generators and relations for n-qubit Clifford operators* (LMCS 2015)

Lean formalization of Table 1's Clifford row: the relations C1–C15 of
Figure 8 present the group generated by the Clifford gates `ω`, `H`, `S`,
`Z_c` on every number of qubits (Proposition 7.1).

## Statement (what a reviewer checks)

| Paper | Lean |
|---|---|
| gates ω, H, S, Z_c (1.1) | `Syntax.lean`: `Gate` |
| Figure 8, C1–C15 | `Syntax.lean`: `Rel`, one constructor per relation, on the wires it is drawn on |
| matrices (1.1) | `Semantics.lean`: `matH`, `matS`, `matCZ`, `S : GateSem Gate ℂ 2` |
| Proposition 7.1 | `Theorem.lean`: `presentation (n) : IsSubPresentationOf (Theory n) (S.gen (n := n))` |

**Wires.** The paper numbers qubits from the top; Figure 8's diagrams are
drawn at heights `y = 0, 1, 2` and the wire at height `0` (the bottom) is our
wire `0`.  `Z_c` is symmetric.  The one-qubit relations C2–C4 are printed as
matrix products and are transcribed in operator order; C5–C15 are written in
the time order of the diagrams with `tseq`.  Every transcription was also
checked numerically, and `Soundness.lean` proves each one exactly.

## What is proved

* **Soundness** of every relation (`Soundness.lean`), decided exactly by
  `Sim.check` (a tree-based state-vector simulator, `Sim.lean`, proved to
  compute `GateOps.eval`), run over ℤ[ω] (`ZOmega.lean`) with `√2 H` in place
  of `H`; a circuit with `h` Hadamard gates is `√2^h` times its matrix
  (`eval_scaled`).  All of Figure 8 checks in about ten seconds.
* **Grouplike** (`Theorem.lean`).
* **The rewrite rules of Figures 3–7** (equations 3.1–3.101 of the
  supplement), each as an equation between words of letters that holds on
  every number of wires (`Rules1.lean`–`Rules5.lean`), derived from C1–C15
  and the supplement's lemmas by a checked derivation.  The 8563 derivation
  steps were translated from the supplement's own machine-readable proofs
  (`anc/clifford-supplement.txt`), step by step: each step of the supplement
  becomes a rewrite with the cited lemma, placed, possibly upside down,
  after moving letters past letters on disjoint wires.  `Rewrite.lean` is the
  checker (`Holds.of_derives`, proved sound) and `Letters.lean` the letters
  and `Holds k l r` (`l = r` on every `n ≥ k` wires), the commutation of
  letters on disjoint wires and the relations as equations of letters.
* **The lemmas of the supplement** (Lemmas 1.1–1.3), `Lemmas.lean`: all of
  (4)–(10), (14)–(18), (20), (21) and the upside-down versions of the two-
  and three-qubit ones are derived from Figure 8 by checked derivations.
  The supplement proves them with diagrams only; the derivations were found
  by a search guided by the lemmas cited at each step of those proofs, with
  intermediate lines read off the figures (each checked numerically), and
  the upside-down versions by mirroring.  So the rules of Figures 3–7 follow
  from C1–C15 with no gap.
* **Normal forms** (Definition 4.3), `NormalForm.lean`, and completeness from
  Propositions 6.3 and 5.5 (`Theorem.complete`).
* **Proposition 6.3 with Lemma 6.2** (`Push.lean`, `Theorem.exists_normal`):
  every circuit equals a normal form by the relations of Figure 8.  The rules
  3.3–3.101 are read as push tables (`Tables.lean`, generated by
  `gen/tables.py`): a dirty gate immediately before one or two boxes equals
  boxes followed by dirty gates, and each table checks, by `decide`, that the
  dirty gates it leaves behind carry the labels ①–④ of Section 6 where they
  stand (`okB`, `okD` in `Dirty.lean`), so that some rule applies to each of
  them next.  Instead of the paper's lexicographic termination argument the
  rules are applied in a fixed order, by structural recursion: a dirty gate
  goes through the `B`-ladder of the Z-normal circuit (`pbc`), the `A` box in
  front of it (`pushL1`), the `D`-ladder of the X-normal circuit (`pdc`), and
  what comes out on the top `n - 1` wires goes into the normal form there
  (`pushN`).  The circuit is pushed, gate by gate, into the normal form of the
  identity, which equals the empty circuit by equation 3.2 (`nid_word`).
* **Proposition 5.5 with Lemmas 5.1–5.4** (`Unique.lean`,
  `Theorem.normal_unique`): distinct normal forms denote distinct operators.
  Pauli operators are data `PD n` (`i^c ⊗ X^a Z^b`, `Pauli.lean`); a circuit
  acts on them by an explicit function `actC`, and `sem_conj` shows that it
  is conjugation by the circuit's matrix (the one- and two-wire cases decided
  exactly over `ℤ[i]`), so circuits with the same matrix act alike, and act
  injectively.  `PauliWires.lean` reads the action letter by letter.  Then,
  as in the paper: the action of each box on the Pauli operators of Figure 6
  is decided on two wires and moved into place (`boxA` … `boxEZ`); a Z-normal
  circuit `L` sends a Pauli operator `encL L`, read off from its boxes, to `Z`
  on the top wire, and is determined by it (Lemma 5.1, `encL_spec`,
  `encL_inj`); an X-normal circuit sends `Z` on the top wire to `Z` on the
  bottom one (Lemma 5.3, `DC_Z`) and a Pauli operator `encDC` to `X` on the
  bottom wire, and is determined by it (Lemma 5.2); so two normal forms that
  act alike have the same `L`, `M` and, by induction on the width, differ at
  most in their scalar (`nf_act_unique`), which the matrix then fixes
  (`ω^p`, `p < 8`, are distinct).

## Notes on the sources

* All 101 statements and all 2617 proof lines of the supplement's Section 3
  are correct for the matrices of (1.1) (checked numerically with our wire
  conversion, then in Lean).
* The supplement's proof steps apply lemmas modulo commutation of gates on
  disjoint wires, sometimes several times in one step, and its `(inv)`
  steps insert or remove `x x⁻¹` for words `x` of involutions and of `S`
  (`S⁻¹ = S³`); the translation makes these explicit.
* The supplement's proof of Lemma 1.3 says "Property (19) is (C10)"; it is
  (C12) of Figure 8.  In its proof of (21), two steps labelled (11)
  (`Z_c² = 1`) exchange two overlapping controlled-`Z` gates, which is (19)
  (C12); we use C12 there.

## Files

`Syntax`, `Semantics`, `Soundness`, `Theorem` (statement and soundness);
`Sim`, `ZOmega` (exact simulation; `Sim` is generic in the gate set and is
also used by `RealStabilizer`); `Derivation`, `Letters`, `Rewrite`
(derivations); `Axioms`, `Lemmas`, `Boxes`, `Rules1`–`Rules5` (generated:
the relations, the supplement's lemmas and rules); `NormalForm`; `Dirty`,
`Tables` (generated), `Push` (Section 6); `Pauli`, `PauliWires`, `Unique`
(Sections 3 and 5).

`Axioms`, `Lemmas` and `Rules*` are generated by the Python scripts in
`gen/` from the supplement's text (the arXiv ancillary file, not in the
repository): parse the supplement, convert its top-down wire numbering to
ours, find each step's lemma instance modulo commutation, and print the
steps.  `python gen/generate.py --check` reproduces them byte for byte
(about ten minutes); `gen/README.md` says which script writes what.

## Typechecking cost

`Rules1`–`Rules5` and `Lemmas` spent about half their time elaborating
the step lists and the statements: a dotted constructor with arguments
is elaborated before its type is known when it is an element of a list
literal of 64 or more elements (the longer `steps := [.mv …, .rw …]`
lists) or an operand of `++` (`[.CZ 0] ++ bD 4 0`), and each such attempt
is postponed and retried at about 0.1 s.  `gen/genlean.py` now writes
them qualified (`Step.mv`, `Step.rw`, `Lt.H`, …), which took `Rules5`
from 70 to 27 s and `Rules3` from 50 to 23 s in a lone run; what remains
is the kernel's replay of the derivations (`by decide +kernel`, 16 s of
`Rules5`).  The existing `Rules*.lean`/`Lemmas.lean` were transformed by
the same substitution rather than regenerated (the generator needs the
supplement's PDF); the generator produces the same text.
