# Four-bit reversible circuits over X, CX and CCX

*Block Normal Forms and Certified Equations for Four-Bit Reversible
Circuits* (`pldi/four-bit-astra/`).

A Lean translation of the Agda development `Examples/Groups/XCXCCX-4bit/`.
The Lean follows the Agda module for module (40 modules, the same names),
with the same definitions, the same lemmas (names ASCII-fied where Lean
needs it) and the same proof route, and the same generated certificates,
written by ports of the Agda's generators; what the Agda imports from shared
libraries is translated in `Lib/` (7 modules).  Departures are listed under
"Deviations" below, and each is stated in its module's header.

## What is proved (`MainTheorem.lean`)

The Agda's `MainTheorem.agda`, statement for statement, in the namespace
`QCert.XCXCCX4bit.MainTheorem`.  The circuits on `n` wires are `Circ n`,
the theory `T` of Figure 1 (rules (1)–(7), `SRel`) with the swap and
structural rules is `VRel SRel n` at width `n`, `n ⊢ w ≈ v` is derivability,
`sem w` is a circuit's function on the bit vectors of its wires and `≐`
pointwise equality.  Wire 0 is the bottom wire, and words are in diagram
order: `w * v` is `w` then `v`.

* `sound : n ⊢ w ≈ v → sem w ≐ sem v`, and `complete` (four wires),
  `complete₁`, `complete₂`, `complete₃`: `sem w ≐ sem v → n ⊢ w ≈ v`
  (Theorem 1.1, Propositions B.1–B.3);
* `presentation₁ : IsPresentationOf (VRel SRel 1) perm₁`, and
  `presentation₂`, `presentation₃`, `presentation₄`: the circuits on one,
  two, three wires present `Sym(2)`, `Sym(4)`, `Sym(8)`, and those on four
  wires `Alt(16)` (Propositions B.1–B.3, Corollary 5.2), with
  `lift_perm₁ … lift_perm₄` saying that a circuit is read as its function on
  the numbered states, and `group₁ … group₄` the isomorphisms
  `(conGen (VRel SRel k)).Quotient ≃* Equiv.Perm (Fin 2ᵏ)` (`≃* Alt 16` on
  four wires);
* `normal_form : 4 ⊢ u ≈ NF (coords u)`: every four-wire circuit rewrites
  to the block normal form of its coordinates (`E ∈ Alt(8)`, `B ∈ Sym(8)`,
  `S` an eight-subset of the sixteen states);
* `unique`: the coordinates of a normal form are determined by its function;
* `onto`: every even permutation of the sixteen states (numbered by
  `Four.enc`) is the function of a normal form (with `unique`, the semantic
  bijection of Proposition 3.3);
* `absorption : 4 ⊢ w * NF c ≈ NF (absorb c w)`: a circuit followed by a
  normal form rewrites to the normal form of the updated coordinates, which
  `NormalForm.Absorb` gives as the paper's Theorem 5.1 does (`absorb_S`:
  `S′ = w⁻¹ S`; `absorb_B`: `B′ = B A₀`; `absorb_E`: `E′ B′ = E B A₁`).

**The form of the presentations.**  The product of the Agda's
`Permutation′-group` and `Alt-group` is diagram-order composition (`∘ₚ`),
as is the product of circuit words, while Mathlib's `Equiv.Perm` and
`alternatingGroup` multiply in operator order and the framework's
`IsPresentationOf Γ sem` reads a word by the homomorphism
`FreeMonoid.lift sem`.  So the Agda's groups are the opposites
`(Equiv.Perm (Fin N))ᵐᵒᵖ` and `(Alt 16)ᵐᵒᵖ`, and the presentations are
stated there: a generator is read as `op` of its permutation of the
numbered states (`perm₁ … perm₄`), and then a circuit is read as `op` of
its own function (`lift_perm₁ … lift_perm₄`).  `group₁ … group₄` compose
the presentation's `quotientEquiv` with `Gᵐᵒᵖ ≃* G` (inversion,
`MulEquiv.inv'`), so that the statements about Mathlib's groups themselves
are there too.

No `sorry`, no new axioms, no `native_decide`.  `#print axioms` of every
statement of `MainTheorem` gives `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`
(`sound` and `complete₁` only `[propext, Quot.sound]`).

## The proof

As the Agda's README describes it, with the Lean names:

1. **Three wires and below** (`Width1`–`Width3`, `Involutions`, `Kit`): the
   edge Toffolis along the Gray code satisfy the Coxeter relations of `S₈`,
   so the circuits present `Sym(8)` and `T` is complete on three wires
   (`ByCoxeter`, over `Coxeter`'s frame lemma).
2. **Placed letters** (`Placed`, `PlacedEngine`, `ToPlaced`, `Narrow3`): a
   gate on any wires is a conjugate by swap networks, and a step on any
   three wires is justified by completeness on three wires.  `Equations4`
   has the four-wire equations found by search, among them the paper's
   equation (8).
3. **The top layer** (`EvenRS`, `ByEvenRS`, `Four`, `Faithful`): `EvenRS N`
   presents `Alt(N)` by Reidemeister–Schreier from `S_N`'s Coxeter
   presentation; at `N = 16` each symbol is a short circuit targeting only
   two wires, so each relation keeps some wire, and completeness reduces to
   `Faithful 3`.
4. **The circuits keeping wire 3** (`KFaithful`) are `K = C ⋊ P`: `C` is
   generated by the controlled perfect matchings `Xm μ`, frames of
   `G = CX₃₀` (`CBase`, `CFrame`'s frame lemma `W'`, `CLabels`), which
   satisfy involution, conjugation and Klein relations under decided side
   conditions (`CRel`, with the five base commutations of `CBC` and
   `BaseComm`); words of labels are rewritten by `LabelEngine` certificates
   deciding `EvenRS 8`'s relations for `C` (`CCerts`, `CFaithful`); so
   `KFaithful.faithful₃`, and `Completeness.complete₄`.
5. **The groups** (`ByPerm`, `Groups`) and **the normal form**
   (`NormalForm`, with `NFLib`, `NFTables`, `EvenWord`).

## Generators (`gen/`)

Each is a port of the Agda generator of the same name and writes the Lean
module from the same inputs, which it reads from the Agda folder
(`Examples/Groups/XCXCCX-4bit/gen/`): by importing the Agda's search code
and running it (`mk_involutions.py`: `appA.py`, `derive.py`;
`mk_width2.py`: `width2.py`; `mk_four.py`, `mk_clabels.py`, `mk_cframe.py`,
`mk_kdata.py`: the Agda generators' data and breadth-first searches;
`mk_ccerts.py`: `ccerts.py` and `c8/certgen.py`), or from the saved search
results (`mk_width3.py`: `width3_lemmas.json`; `mk_equations4.py`:
`pg_results.json`; `mk_basecomm.py`: `bc_results.json`, both replayed by
the Agda generator's `steps_of`).  `mk_involutions.py` and `mk_width2.py`
also check that their search finds the derivations of the checked-in Agda
modules.  `common.py` turns an Agda `derive` block into Lean;
`mk_nftables.py` is self-contained (the Agda's script writes its module when
imported).  Like the Agda's, the generators are untrusted: Lean checks what
they write.  Run them with WSL's `python3` from `gen/`:

```sh
cd QCert/XCXCCX4bit/gen
for g in mk_involutions mk_width2 mk_width3 mk_equations4 mk_four \
         mk_clabels mk_cframe mk_kdata mk_nftables mk_basecomm mk_ccerts; do
  python3 $g.py
done
```

They regenerate the checked-in modules byte for byte (about a minute in
all; `mk_involutions`, `mk_width2` and `mk_four` take 15–20 s each, their
searches).

## Deviations from the Agda

Collected from the module headers, which state each one where it occurs.

* **Words.**  The framework's words are `FreeMonoid` (lists), multiplied
  left-associatively; the Agda's associativity steps are reasoning in the
  quotient monoid of the theory (`CircuitProp.cl`; in `CircuitProp`,
  `Placed`, `CBase`, `CRel`, `CFaithful`, `KFaithful`, `EvenRS`), and the
  Agda's `⟪_⟫`, `word-of-list` and `general-assoc` are `FreeMonoid.ofList`
  or not needed (`Lib/Engine`, `Lib/Words`).
* **Computation.**  The Agda's `refl` on a Boolean is `decide +kernel` on a
  Boolean checker with a soundness lemma (`Engine.check`,
  `PlacedEngine.check`, `LabelEngine.check`, `same`, `eqF`); its tables of
  `refl` proofs are one `decide` each (`CFrame`, `NFTables`, `CSem.pos_enc8`),
  or one per letter or symbol (`Width2`/`Width3`'s `T_act`, `Four.R_act`).
  The kernel decides closed propositions only, so a derivation is checked at
  the width it is drawn on and padded (`Engine.Eqn.pad`), where the Agda
  checks it at a symbolic width.  Two letters commute in `perm` steps when
  they act on disjoint wires (`QCert.Minimality.Gen.apart`).
* **Kernel cost.**  `BaseComm` cuts each derivation into segments of at
  most 40 steps joined by transitivity (one check of the 496-step `BC31` did
  not finish in eight minutes at 11 GB); `CCerts` checks each certificate in
  a lemma of its own and the closure in seven ranges; `KFaithful` decides
  the lower letters one by one; `CSem` evaluates the controlled matchings on
  lists of bits (`semK`); `CFrame` has a fast action on the values of the
  positions (`actF`, with `actF_ok`).
* **Symmetric and alternating groups.**  A letter of `S_N` is its index with
  its bound (`SG N = {d // 2 + d ≤ N}`), and `SymRel N` lists the order,
  braid and far relations at every index (the Agda's are circuits of
  `Circuit.Base` with structural rules).  The completeness of the Coxeter
  presentation, `stab` and the frame lemma come from
  `QCert.ToffoliHadamardRS.CoxeterFrames`, so the Agda's `stripN`,
  `strip-ok`, `liftW`, `cyc`, `outs` are not reproduced; `frame` is total.
  The sign is Mathlib's `Equiv.Perm.sign` read as a Boolean and `Alt n` is
  `alternatingGroup (Fin n)`, where the Agda defines the sign as the parity
  of Symmetric's word.  Permutations are `Equiv.Perm`: `π ⟨$⟩ʳ i` is `π i`,
  `π ⟨$⟩ˡ i` is `π.symm i`, `π ∘ₚ ρ` is `π.trans ρ`.
* **Parameterised modules** are structures (`ByEvenRS.Realisation`,
  `ByPerm.Data` with `Full` and `Even`) or namespaces whose lemmas take the
  parameters as arguments (`ByCoxeter.Via`, `Coxeter.Circuits`,
  `LabelEngine`, `CRel.Calculus`, `EvenWord.Coset`/`Lift`,
  `NormalForm.Of`/`Absorb`; `Four`'s parameter `f₃ : Faithful 3`); the Agda's
  public re-exports with arguments applied are restated (`PFrames`,
  `Narrow3`, `CBC`).
* **Data.**  `Vec` tables are lists (`CLabels`, `CRel`, `CCerts`), `Maybe`
  is `Option`, and decision procedures returning proofs are Booleans or
  `Decidable` instances (`CRel.distinct?`, `NFLib`); states are an inductive
  vector `Bits n` (`Semantics`).
* **Opaque definitions** are `irreducible_def`s with their defining
  equations (`EvenWord.Coset.syms`, `ByPerm.Even.circA`, `NormalForm.D`,
  `C`, `r`), and `Symmetric.go` is irreducible, so that the kernel never
  evaluates a sign or Symmetric's word for a symbolic permutation (the
  Agda README's two traps).
* **The main statements** are stated in the opposites of Mathlib's groups,
  with the semantics named (`perm₁ … perm₄`) and characterised
  (`lift_perm₁ … lift_perm₄`), and the isomorphisms `group₁ … group₄` added;
  `onto` and the other `Σ`s are subtypes.
* **Smaller reuse.**  `NormalForm` reuses `Groups.Fourw.at_` and
  `parity_f`, `Placed.word_down` (the Agda's private `word↓`) and
  `KFaithful.Xs_low`; `Groups` reuses `EvenWord.Coset.rs_parity`;
  `NormalForm.e_lift` is proved on the two halves of `Fin (8 + 8)` where the
  Agda lists the sixteen states.
* **Not translated** (unused by the development): `Examples/Independence/Prop`'s
  `Axioms`, `Reasoning`'s `Width`.
* **Names.**  `x⁼` is `xE`, hyphens are underscores, `ᵖ` is `ₚ` (`PG.Xₚ`),
  `⟦_⟧` is `sem`, `Circuit`/`Gen` are `Circ`/`Gn` (the framework's names
  being visible), and Lean keywords are avoided (`at` is `at_`, `local` is
  `localStep`, `match` is `matchW`, `by` is `by_`).

## Typechecking

```sh
./check.sh QCert/XCXCCX4bit/MainTheorem.lean
```

`MainTheorem.lean` reaches all 47 modules of the folder.  With its imports
outside the folder built and its own build artifacts removed, the folder
builds in 277 s (`LEAN_NUM_THREADS=2 lake build` of the 47 modules, wall
time, 10 November 2026 on an Intel Core i5-13400TEF under WSL; Agda checks the
40 modules of `XCXCCX-4bit` in 85 s there, the sum of its per-module times;
`pldi/compare/th4.json`).  Each module checks
in under a minute on its own given its imports (Lean 4.30, Mathlib cached);
the slowest are the certificates, `CCerts` and `KFaithful`, then `BaseComm`
(its 30 segments), `CSem`, `Lib/Symmetric`, `NFLib`, `NormalForm` and
`Four`, between about 13 s and 50 s each, the time of loading Mathlib
included.  The kernel's checks of the certificates peak at about 5 GB of
memory with the imports (`CCerts`), so build with at least 6 GB free.
