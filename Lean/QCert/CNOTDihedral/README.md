# Amy, Chen and Ross, *A finite presentation of CNOT-dihedral operators* (QPL 2017)

Lean formalization of Table 1's CNOT-dihedral row: the relations R₁–R₁₃ of
Figure 1, with the laws of the symmetry (§2), present the group generated by
`ω`, `X`, `T`, `CNOT` and `SWAP` on every number of qubits (Propositions
3.6, 5.5 and 6.4).

**The symmetry.** The paper presents CNOT-dihedral operators as a symmetric
monoidal groupoid: the bifunctorial law and a symmetry (SWAP) that is natural
and coherent are assumed (§2) and SWAP appears in R₅ and in gates on
non-adjacent qubits.  Our structural rules give the bifunctorial and spatial
laws; the symmetry is the gate `SW` with three rule schemes: `SW² = 1`, and
naturality for every one-wire and every two-wire gate (for `SW` itself the
Yang–Baxter equation).  Coherence follows from these.

**Wires.** Figure 1 is drawn at heights `y = 0, 1.6, 3.2, 4.8`; the bottom
wire is our wire `0`.  `CNOT` is drawn with its control above its target:
the gate `CX` has control wire `1` and target wire `0` (its printed matrix
reads the upper wire as the most significant bit).  Gates on non-adjacent
wires are, as in the paper, the gate on the top-most wires conjugated by
SWAPs (`CX₂₀`, `U₀₂`, `U₁₃`, `U₀₃`, `V₀₂₃`, `V₀₁₃`).

## What is proved

* **Soundness** (Proposition 3.6), `Soundness.lean`: each relation is decided
  on *phase-affine pairs* `|x⟩ ↦ ω^{p(x)} |f(x)⟩` (`PhaseAffine.lean`; the
  semantics of Definition 3.5), which are proved to give the matrix semantics
  (`sem_eq_toOp`).  The four-wire relation R₁₃ checks in seconds (the generic
  matrix evaluation needed more than 5 GB).
* **Grouplike** (`Theorem.lean`).

## Remaining `sorry`

| Lean | Paper |
|---|---|
| `Theorem.presentation`, field `complete` | Proposition 5.5 (every circuit has a normal form: Lemma 5.1, the 25 commutations of Figure 2; Lemmas 5.2, 5.3, diagonal normal forms; Lemma 5.4, Lafont's affine normal forms) and Proposition 6.4 (uniqueness: Lemmas 6.1–6.3) |

The phase-affine semantics is the natural setting for the uniqueness half
(Proposition 6.4 compares phase polynomials and affine maps); the existence
half needs derivations the paper does not give ("a tedious but
straightforward exercise") and Lafont's normal forms for affine circuits.
