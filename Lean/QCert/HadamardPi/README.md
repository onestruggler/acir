Fang, Heunen and Kaarsgaard, *Hadamard-Π: Equational quantum programming*,
POPL 2026, arXiv:2506.06835.  Formalized from the paper (TeX source and PDF)

## Main theorem

```lean
-- QCert/HadamardPi/Theorem.lean
theorem theorem_4_13 {n} {w w' : Word (Gen n)} (h : Derivable Fig6 w w') :
    FreeMonoid.lift (sem n) w = FreeMonoid.lift (sem n) w'
theorem theorem_4_14 (n : ℕ) : IsPresentationOf (Fig6 (n := n)) (sem n)
```

* `Gen n` is the generating set 𝒢ₙ of Definition 4.5: `(-1)_[a]`, `X_[b,c]` and
  `H_[b,c]` with `b < c`.  **Indices start at 0** (the paper's start at 1).
* `Fig6` is Figure 6: one constructor per relation scheme (a1)–(d4).  The
  caption says "the indices are distinct"; a scheme's instances are the
  assignments of distinct indices for which every generator it mentions is in
  𝒢ₙ (increasing indices).  Each constructor carries exactly those order
  conditions plus the remaining distinctness conditions.
* `sem n : Gen n → O_ZInvSqrt2 n` sends a generator to its one- or two-level
  matrix (`levelMat`), an element of Oₙ(ℤ[1/√2]) (Definitions 4.3, 4.4).

## What is proved

* Soundness (Theorem 4.13), every relation, no sorry.
* Grouplike (every generator is an involution).
* Generation (Theorem 4.8) and correctness of Algorithm 1 (Theorem 4.10), no
  sorry: `lift_sem_surjective`.
* The reduction of completeness to the Main Lemma (Lemmas A.6, A.8, A.9 and
  Theorem 4.14 through `QCert.NormalWords.isPresentationOf`), no sorry.
  Lemma A.6 is proved with its level bound (the path of the basic word stays
  below any bound on the levels of its two ends).
* The Main Lemma (Lemma A.7), every case:
  * `G = (-1)_[0]`: Main Cases 5, 6, 7, 8, all subcases;
  * `G = X_[0,x]`: Main Cases 3 and 4 (exponent 0), every case with `x` at or
    above the pivot (retrograde squares), and Main Cases 1 and 2 by our own
    argument (pair confluence, below);
  * `G = H_[0,1]`: Main Case 10 (first odd entry not at `0`), Main Case 9 —
    its subcase (2)(b) with more than four odd entries by a diamond
    (`case9_hard`), with exactly four by Clément's Case 3.4 (`case9_four`,
    below) — Main Cases 11 and 12 (exponent 0), and the case where the pivot
    is the first column (retrograde);
  * the base case `s = ⟦G⟧`.
* Hence completeness: **Theorem 4.14, with no `sorry`**.

### How `case9_four` is proved

Main Case 9 (2)(b) with exactly four odd entries: `G = H_[0,1]` at a state
`s` whose entries `0` and `1` are odd of different classes, whose first
syllable is `H_[0,P]`, and which has exactly two more odd entries, `P` (of
the class of `0`) and `Q` (of the class of `1`).  This is where the paper's
proof is wrong (below); Clément's Case 3.4 closes it.

* *The square* (`CaseFour.lean`).  The first syllable of `H_[0,1] s` is
  `H_[0,P']` with `P' ∈ {P, Q}`; the square is closed by a join of
  `H_[1,Q] H_[0,P]` and `H_[1,Q''] H_[0,P'] H_[0,1]` (`Join.extend`), `Q''`
  the other of `P`, `Q`.  The transpositions `X_[2,P]` and `X_[3,Q₁]` keep
  the level and conjugate the two words (`move_opt`), so the four odd entries
  may be taken at `0, 1, 2, 3`: the words become `A = H_[1,3] H_[0,2]` and
  `B = H_[1,3] H_[0,2] H_[0,1]` (`P' = P`, Clément's `η = ε`) or
  `H_[1,2] H_[0,3] H_[0,1]` (`P' = Q`).
* *The case tree* (`Case34.lean`, generated).  A node is a state with a
  window `0, …, w-1` of the pivot column whose numerators are given by a
  *pattern* — affine forms over ℤ in integer parameters (`Sym.lean`) — and
  whose other numerators are divisible by `√2^δ`.  A node is closed by a
  path checked on the pattern for every value of the parameters together
  with a derivation (a leaf), by a transfer along a word (Clément's
  Lemma 5.3), by bringing in an entry outside the window (his Lemma 2.1),
  or by a split on the parity of a form in the parameters.  Its 274 nodes
  follow Clément: Lemmas 5.7 and 5.9 and Assumption 3 (transfers along
  signed permutations), Subcase 3.4.1 (a leaf by his relation (20)),
  Subcase 3.4.2 (the two entries `e₄`, `e₅` of depth 1, Assumption 5, the
  leaves of Subcases 3.4.2.1 by (21) and 3.4.2.2 by Lemma 3.17), and
  Subcase 3.4.2.3 (the tail cleaned, the entries `j`, `l` of depth 2 and `m`
  of depth 3 of Lemma 5.13, and the transfer along `(-1)_[α] X_[α,β] H_[j,m]`
  to a state of Subcase 3.4.2.1).
* *Checks.*  Every residue test, every state of every path and every change
  of parameters is decided by `decide +kernel` on lists of integers; the generator
  runs the same computations in Python first (`gen/c34mirror.py`).

(21) is derived from (d4) (`Derived.lean`, `c21`) through `T T' T ≈ V C V` (with `T = Q A Q`,
`T' = R A R`, `Q = H_[0,2] H_[1,3]`, `R = H_[0,4] H_[1,5]`, `V = H_[2,4] H_[3,5]`,
`C = H_[4,5]`, which is (d4) at `0,…,5`): (21) is equivalent to
`T' T T' ≈ M' T M'` with `M' = R (-1)_[0] (-1)_[1] R` a signed permutation,
and `T' T T' ≈ X (T T' T) X` for `X = X_[2,4] X_[3,5]`.

## Errors and gaps found in the paper

1. **Lemmas A.16 and A.17 are false.**  Both are stated for `M ∈ Oₙ(ℤ[1/√2])`
   with `level(M) = (j,k,4)`, `k ≥ 2`, the four odd entries in positions 1–4
   with residues `(1+√2, 1, 1+√2, 1)` (mod 2).  Counterexamples in dimension 6
   with `k = 4` (numerators `√2⁴ v`, a unit vector since
   `Σ(aᵢ² + 2bᵢ²) = 16` and `Σ aᵢbᵢ = 0`; any unit vector over ℤ[1/√2] is a
   column of some element of O₆(ℤ[1/√2])):
   * Lemma A.16: `√2⁴ v = (−1−√2, 1, −1−√2, 1, −2+√2, −√2)`.  Then
     `√2³ H₁₄H₂₃H₂₄H₂₃H₁₄ v` is in ℤ[√2]⁴ (the hypothesis) but
     `√2³ H₁₃H₂₄H₁₂H₁₃H₂₄ v` is not (the conclusion).
   * Lemma A.17: `√2⁴ v = (−1−√2, 1, −1−√2, −1, −2+√2, −√2)`.  Then
     `√2³ H₁₄H₂₃H₃₄H₂₃H₁₄ v` is in ℤ[√2]⁴ but `√2³ H₁₃H₂₄H₁₄H₁₃H₂₄ v` is not.

   Found and checked by an exhaustive search in exact arithmetic over ℚ(√2)
   (all unit vectors of dimension 6 with `k = 4` and entries of small norm;
   dimension ≤ 5 and `k ≤ 3` give none).  The proof of A.16 goes wrong in its
   second case (`a₃ + a₄` odd), where the norm equation is used to rule the case
   out.  Main Case 9 (`l = 4`, via Lemma A.19) rests on these two lemmas, so
   its proof is incomplete.  Clément's notes (row `QCert/Clement2019`) treat
   this situation as their Case 3.4.

   **The four odd entries alone do not suffice.**  In dimension 9 with
   `k = 7` (indices from 0), the column
   `√2⁷ v = (1+3√2, 1, −3√2, 1+3√2, 2, 5, −2+√2, −4+2√2, −2−2√2)` (a unit
   vector) has exactly four odd entries, 0, 1, 3, 5, with 0 and 1 of
   different classes; the first syllable of `s` is `H_[0,3]`, and that of
   `H_[0,1] s` is `H_[0,3]` too.  No word on the four odd entries joins
   `t = H_[0,3] s` to `q = H_[0,3] H_[0,1] s` below `s`: a breadth-first
   search over all words in `H`, `X` and `(-1)` on the indices 0, 1, 3, 5,
   from `t`, keeping every state integral at exponent 7 with fewer than four
   odd numerators (that is, below `s`), exhausts the 3840 reachable states
   without meeting `q` (`gen/` has the script).  So the square needs entries
   outside the four odd ones: Clément's Subcase 3.4.2 brings in two entries
   with numerators `√2 × odd`, and 3.4.2.3 three more.
2. **Main Case 1 (1) is wrong as stated.**  It claims that for `x < i₂` the
   first syllable of `X_[1,x] s` is `H_[1,i₂] X_[1,x]`.  This fails when an odd
   entry of the other class lies among the positions `2, …, x`.  Example
   (dimension 4, pivot column 4, `k = 3`):
   `√2³ v = (1, 1−√2, 1, 1+√2)` has `i₁ = 1`, `i₂ = 3`; with `x = 2`,
   `X_[1,2] v` has first syllable `H_[1,4]`, not `H_[1,3] X_[1,2]`.
3. **Main Cases 1 and 2 omit `x > j`.**  When `X_[1,x]` acts beyond the pivot,
   the first syllable of `X_[1,x] s` is `X_[1,x]` itself (the square is
   "retrograde"); formalized as `square_retro` with `syl_X_above`.
4. **Main Case 4 (3) for `x = j`.**  The case lists `x ≤ j` and cites (b2), (b3),
   but `X_[1,j]` and `X_[a,j]` share the index `j`; the square closes with
   `G' = X_[1,a]` instead, by (c3) (`main_X`, branch `x = j`).
5. **Main Cases 1 and 2 are replaced.**  Given items 2 and 3, the case
   analysis of Main Cases 1 and 2 (`G = X_[1,x]` below the pivot at a positive
   exponent) is not used; `Pairs.lean` closes these squares by *pair
   confluence*.  The first syllable of `s` is `X₁ H_A` (`X₁ = X_[1,i₁]` or `ε`,
   by (c4), (a2)), where `A = (i₁, i₂)` is a matched pair (two odd numerators
   of one class) of the pivot column; that of `r = X_[1,x] s` is `X₁' H_A'`,
   and `H_A' X_[1,x] ≈ X_[1,x] Y H_B` with `B` the image of `A'` under the
   transposition `(1 x)`, again a matched pair of `s`, and `Y` a word of `X`
   and `(-1)` letters (`conj_HX`, five configurations).  Two matched pairs are
   joined below `s` (`pair_confluence`): `D = ε` if `A = B`; `D = H_A H_B` if
   they are disjoint; if they share an index, their three entries and a
   fourth of the same class (a class has evenly many odd entries) form a
   window of four, on which a switch word through the pairings `(12)(34)`
   and `(13)(24)` and the relation (f1) joins them, every state being checked
   on the window for all residues modulo the parity of the pattern
   (`Switch.lean`, `Good.lean`).  The square is then `N' G ≈ (X₁' G Y D X₁) N`.
6. Small citation slips: the first diagram of Lemma A.6 commutes by (c1), not
   (c4); the conjugations of Lemma A.6 for `H_[b,c]` need separate words when
   `b = 1` (ours `bw`, `bwH0`).

## Notes on Clément's Case 3.4

Clément's notes (2019, pp. 31–42 and appendix A1.1, A1.5–A1.9) were followed
without finding a false statement; every diagram used was checked in exact
arithmetic over ℤ[√2] on random states (3000 per diagram) before being
formalized.  Points his text leaves implicit:

* His diagrams (31) and (33) of Lemma 3.17 (Subcase 3.4.2.2) rest on
  relations that are those of (30) and (32) conjugated by the signed
  transposition `(-1)_[4] X_[4,5]`, up to three-index identities that follow
  from `H_[1,2] (-1)_[2] H_[1,2] = X_[1,2]` (`Case34Der.lean`).  His `H[5,3]`,
  not a generator of 𝒢ₙ, is written `(-1)_[3] X_[3,5] H_[3,5]`.
* Subcase 3.4.2.2 cannot be avoided by choosing the extra entries
  differently: on some branches the class counts at exponent `k − 2` are
  even.
* Subcase 3.4.2.3 needs the entries outside the window cleaned first — his
  Assumption 4 and the reductions in his Lemma 5.13: while two entries of
  the same depth and class remain outside the window, `H` on them is a
  transfer that lowers both (`clean_ind`).  The entry `m` of depth 3 is
  forced by the parity of the number of odd entries (`tail_odd_entry`).

## Design notes

* Exact checks: an identity of words at symbolic indices is the image under
  `levelMat f` (`f` injective) of the identity at the indices `0, 1, …`, which
  is decided over `ℤ√2` with every generator scaled by `√2`
  (`wordMat_eq_of_check`); (d3) is checked in dimension 4 and (d4) in
  dimension 6.
* The level is `toLex (piv M, toLex (k, l))` with `piv M` one more than the
  index of the last column different from the basis vector.
* Derivations are chains of `rw_at w k h`: the segment of `w` at position `k`
  is rewritten by `h : L ≈ R`, checked by `rfl` on lists with symbolic
  indices.
