# Searching for simple complete circuit relations

The presentations proved in this development are complete at every
width, but at four or more wires they are finite families of decoded
relations (millions of raw equations at four wires), not a short list of
natural circuit identities. This directory is the research tooling for
finding such a list, in the spirit of Clément's theory for
Real-Clifford+CH: a few small rules, plus whatever schemas over the
width turn out to be needed. The proofs are in `../Natural/` (see In
Agda below); the scripts here also write their generated parts.

## Tools

- `core.py`: exact gate actions (wire 0 the low bit; `CX i` targets wire
  i with control i+1, `CCX i` targets i with controls i+1 and i+2, `HH i`
  is H ⊗ H on wires i and i+1), a faithful permutation representation on
  the orbit of the vectors 4·eᵢ (16 points on two wires, 2160 on three,
  the norm-4 vectors of E8 up to scale), and coset enumeration for
  presentations whose generators are involutions.
- `tc.c`: the same coset enumeration in C, for millions of cosets.
  Build it with `gcc -O2 -o tc tc.c` (the binary is not tracked).
- `search.py`: short relators by meet-in-the-middle over the exact group,
  and an irredundant subset that still presents the group.
- `staged.py`: derived letters (such as the reversed CNOT
  `CXr i = HH i CX i HH i`), rules at every placement, and completeness
  bounds through a chain of subgroups.
- `stage3.py`: the three-wire experiments.
- `proofs.py`, `natproof.py`, `gen_axioms.py`, `gen_width.py`: the
  proof-producing enumeration and the Agda it writes (see In Agda);
  `natproofH.py`, `gen_axiomsH.py` the same for CCX+H, and `labelled.py`
  the enumeration with Felsch's strategy, which also carries labels in
  the generators of a subgroup.
- `selftest.py`: known orders (S₃ … S₈, and the gate groups on one and
  two wires, from complete Schreier presentations).

## Method

Completeness at a fixed width w is decided by bounding the order of the
presented group G_R. For a subgroup K generated by some of the letters,

    |G_R| ≤ [G_R : K] · |P_K|,

where P_K is presented by the rules that only use K's letters: those hold
in G_R, so the subgroup they generate there is a quotient of P_K. Since
the rules are sound, G_R maps onto the true group G, and |G_R| ≤ |G| means
the map is an isomorphism: the rules are complete at width w. Both factors
are coset enumerations. On three wires K is the classical subgroup
generated by `X i`, `CX i` and `CCX 0` (order 128), and the index to reach
is 696 729 600 / 128 = 5 443 200 (|W(E8)| = 696 729 600).

## Results so far

**Two wires.** Besides the structural rules of `Circuit.Base`
(involutions, commutation of gates on disjoint wires), four rules present
the 384-element group exactly:

| Rule | Reading, with `CXr = HH CX HH`, the CNOT reversed |
| --- | --- |
| `X1 CX X1 = CX X0` | an X on the control propagates to the target |
| `X0 (HH X1 HH) = (HH X1 HH) X0` | X on one wire commutes with Z on the other |
| `CXr X0 CXr = X0 X1` | the same propagation for the reversed CNOT |
| `(CX HH)⁶ = ε`, i.e. `(CX · CXr)³ = ε` | the CNOTs on two wires generate S₃ |

`python3 -B search.py --width 2 --half 6` finds them (about 40 s).

**Three wires.** Short relators in the primitive gates do not suffice —
every relator of length at most 10 that spans the three wires, 9077 of
them, still leaves the coset enumeration unbounded — because the natural
three-wire rules mention swaps and reversed CNOTs, which are words of
length 5 and 3. With those as derived letters (`CXr i = HH i CX i HH i`,
`SW i = CX i HH i CX i HH i CX i`), the 5898 relators of length at most 8
that span the three wires, together with the two-wire rules at both
placements and the structural rules, are **complete on three wires**,
certified twice:

| Subgroup K | Bound on K from its own rules | Index of K | Product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `X i`, `CX i`, `CCX 0` | 128 (enumeration over 1) | 5 443 200 | 696 729 600 |
| the classical letters, S8 | 5040 · 8 = 40 320 | 17 280 | 696 729 600 |

Both products are |W(E8)|. `minimise3.py` shrinks the set while both
enumerations through S8 still pass (about 12 s per check); it leaves 12
relators, irredundant but not readable.

**A natural complete rule set on three wires** (`natural3.py`,
`classical3.py`). Each rule is stated on the bottom wires and used at
every placement, with the structural rules, `CXr i = HH i CX i HH i` and
`SW i = CX i CXr i CX i`:

| Classical rules | |
| --- | --- |
| `SW0 = CX0 CXr0 CX0` | a swap is three CNOTs |
| `X1 CX0 X1 = CX0 X0`, `CXr0 X0 CXr0 = X0 X1` | an X on a control propagates |
| `(CX0 CXr0)³ = ε` | the CNOTs on two wires: S3 |
| `SW0 X0 SW0 = X1`, `SW0 CX0 SW0 = CXr0` | naturality of the swap |
| `SW0 SW1 SW0 = SW1 SW0 SW1` | braid |
| `SW0 SW1 CX0 SW1 SW0 = CX1` | a two-wire gate moved up a wire |
| `X0 CCX0 = CCX0 X0`, `CX0 CCX0 = CCX0 CX0` | gates on the Toffoli's target |
| `SW1 CCX0 SW1 = CCX0` | the Toffoli's controls are symmetric |
| `X1 CCX0 X1 = CCX0 SW1 CX0 SW1` | a negated control |
| `CX1 CCX0 CX1 = X1 CCX0 X1` | a CNOT between the controls |
| `CCX0 CXr0 CCX0 CXr0 = SW0 CCX0 SW0 CCX0` | a CNOT from the target to a control |

| Hadamard rules | |
| --- | --- |
| `X0 (HH0 X1 HH0) = (HH0 X1 HH0) X0` | X commutes with Z on the other wire |
| `SW0 HH0 SW0 = HH0` | HH is symmetric |
| `HH0 HH1 = HH1 HH0` | the two HH commute |
| `SW0 HH1 SW0 = HH0 HH1`, `SW1 HH0 SW1 = HH0 HH1` | H₁H₂ and H₀H₁ swapped are H₀H₂ |
| `CCX0 (CXr0 HH0 CXr0) = (CXr0 HH0 CXr0) CCX0` | the Toffoli commutes with HH under a CNOT |

The classical rules present S8 (index 5040 of ⟨X0, X1, X2⟩), and all of
them present W(E8) (index 17280 of S8). The last classical rule cannot be
replaced by `(CCX0 CXr0)⁶ = ε`, its order relation: with it the classical
rules no longer present S8.

**Twelve rules, complete on one, two and three wires** (`final.py`). A
rule only applies where it fits, so completeness is checked at each width
with that width's rules: on two wires by enumeration (order 384), on
three through S8 as above. Eight of the twenty are redundant, which
leaves, with the abbreviations `CXr = HH CX HH` and `SW = CX CXr CX`:

| | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | `X1 CX0 X1 = CX0 X0` |
| 2 | `CXr0 X0 CXr0 = X0 X1` |
| 3 | `(CX0 CXr0)³ = ε` |
| 4 | `X0 · HH0 X1 HH0 = HH0 X1 HH0 · X0` |
| 5 | `SW0 SW1 SW0 = SW1 SW0 SW1` |
| 6 | `SW0 SW1 CX0 SW1 SW0 = CX1` |
| 7 | `SW1 CCX0 SW1 = CCX0` |
| 8 | `X1 CCX0 X1 = CCX0 · SW1 CX0 SW1` |
| 9 | `CCX0 CXr0 CCX0 CXr0 = SW0 CCX0 SW0 · CCX0` |
| 10 | `SW0 HH1 SW0 = HH0 HH1` |
| 11 | `SW1 HH0 SW1 = HH0 HH1` |
| 12 | `CCX0 · CXr0 HH0 CXr0 = CXr0 HH0 CXr0 · CCX0` |

Rules 1–4 are two-wire rules and 5–12 three-wire rules. (`final.py` also
lists `SW0 = CX0 CXr0 CX0`, which only links the abstract swap letter of
the classical check to the CNOTs; for circuits it holds by definition.)
Each rule is irredundant: without it the rules are incomplete on two or
on three wires. Which rules are redundant depends on the order of removal;
this is one irredundant choice, made by removing from the end of the list.

The certificate is |G_R| ≤ 17280 · 5040 · 8 = 696 729 600 = |W(E8)|, from
coset enumerations (`tc.c`) that are not themselves formally verified.

## CCX+H on one and two wires

With the single Hadamard (`coreH.py`: entries in ℤ[1/√2], exact) the group
is the dihedral group of order 16 on one wire and the real Clifford group
of order 2304 on two; on three wires it is infinite. Four rules, with
`CXr = H0 H1 CX H0 H1`, `SW = CX CXr CX`, `CZ = H1 CXr H1` and `Z = H X H`,
present both exactly (`naturalH2.py`):

| | Rule | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `(X0 H0)⁸ = ε` | one wire: dihedral of order 16 |
| 2 | `CXr0 X0 CXr0 = X0 X1` | an X on the control propagates |
| 3 | `SW0 H0 SW0 = H1` | the swap moves H |
| 4 | `CZ0 CXr0 CZ0 CXr0 = Z0` | a CZ and a CNOT anticommute up to Z on the control |

Rule 1 is redundant on two wires but needed on one. The natural seed
`naturalH2.SEED` (also the CCX+HH two-wire rules, the symmetry of CZ and
how Z passes a CNOT) is complete on two wires too; rules 3 and 4 are what
the seed first missed, found by the search and rewritten in natural form.

From three wires on the CCX+H group is infinite, so completeness there
(where Amy, Ross and Wesley's 3-qubit theory lives) needs a proof, not an
enumeration.

## In Agda

`../Natural/` states the rules as a circuit calculus (`Syntactics`, the
twelve rules and the two four-wire ones) and proves them sound at every
width (`Soundness`: each rule on its own wires by the completeness of the
translated calculus, then on more wires by adding idle ones).

Completeness is proved on one and two wires (`Width1.agda`,
`Width2.agda`) from a coset enumeration that proves its own table:
`proofs.py` enumerates the cosets of the trivial subgroup and records, for
every entry c·y = d, a derivation of `rep c ++ [y] = rep d` as rewrite
steps by the rules (`natproof.py` supplies the rules at every placement,
the involutions and the commutations of disjoint gates, and replays every
derivation). `gen_width.py` writes those derivations as equations of
`Natural/Engine`, each checked by `derive`, together with the tables and
two checks by one boolean each: the stored matrices of the
representatives are theirs (`Natural/Prefix`, one gate at a time from a
parent word) and are pairwise distinct. `Natural/Automaton` then
concludes: every circuit is derivably equal to the representative its
letters lead to, and two circuits with one matrix lead to the same one.
On two wires that is 384 representatives and 3528 derivations in
21 modules, about ten minutes in all and none over 2 GB.
`gen_axioms.py` writes `Natural/Axioms`; `python3 -B gen_width.py HH N
--check` compares the modules with the generator.

The four CCX+H rules above are proved the same way in `../../CCX+H/Natural/`:
sound at every width, complete on one and two wires (`natproofH.py`,
`gen_axiomsH.py`, `gen_width.py H N`). There the enumeration follows
Felsch's strategy (`labelled.py` with no subgroup), which defines 5788
cosets for the 2304 elements where HLT defines 22042, and needs 35519
derivations where HLT needs 107180. The CCX+H gate matrices are costly
to evaluate (through the general level action), so every module stores
the matrices of the letters too (`Gates`), each checked once. Its
two-wire proof is 87 modules and about 1¾ hours; the three that load
every derivation (`Tables`, `Sides`, the assembly) need up to 5.5 GB.

### Three wires

Three wires is not formalised yet. The group there is the Weyl group
W(E8): it has order 696729600 and preserves the 240 vectors listed in
`../ThreeQubit/README.md`, which are the roots of E8 up to scale; CCX is
the reflection in e₆ − e₇ and `HH1 CCX0 HH1` the reflection in a root with
eight entries ±1. That is too large to enumerate with proofs, and the
obvious towers fail the same way: no subgroup tried — ⟨X, CX, HH⟩, the
classical S₈, the affine group, the even signed permutations — is
presented by the rules that use only its letters (the enumerations
overflow), so each level down needs relations derived through letters
outside it. Over the 135 frames of `../ThreeQubit/` with labels in the
stabilizer's generators (`labelled.py`), the labels grow without bound
(over 5000 letters by 6000 cosets); over the classical S₈, Felsch's
strategy defines 244103 cosets for 17280. The promising route is the
Coxeter presentation of W(E8): its tower of parabolic subgroups needs no
derived relations, every level being presented by the Coxeter relations
of the level above that use only its generators. It leaves the
derivation from the natural rules of the decoded Coxeter relations (28
pairs, for a base of reflections with short circuits `w CCX0 rev(w)`,
`w` at most seven letters) and of each gate as a word in the
reflections — a few dozen three-wire identities, for which no derivation
engine is built yet.

## Beyond three wires

**The twelve rules are not complete on four wires**, and a parity
argument shows why (`abelian.py`). Every generator is an involution, so a
character of a presented group is a set of gates whose total count every
relator preserves mod 2. The Toffoli occurs an even number of times in
each of the twelve rules, and no rule carries a Toffoli from one triple of
wires to another, so the count of `CCX i`, for each placement i, is a
character of the presented group at every width. On three wires that is
the determinant (a Toffoli there is a transposition of basis states), but
from four wires on the true group is perfect (`GeneralN/Perfect.agda`), so
it has no such character. Two four-wire rules, both sound (checked exactly
on basis vectors, `abelian.sound`), remove every character on four, five
and six wires:

| Rule | |
| --- | --- |
| `CCX1 CX0 CCX1 CX0 = SW0 CCX1 SW0` | a Toffoli is the commutator of a Toffoli and a CNOT |
| `SW0 SW1 SW2 CCX0 SW2 SW1 SW0 = CCX1` | a Toffoli moved up a wire |

Neither fits on three wires, so they leave the small widths unchanged.
A trivial abelianization is only a necessary condition: whether these
fourteen rules are complete on four wires is open.

### Rules on boundedly many wires never suffice

**No complete theory has all its rules on boundedly many wires**: from
seven wires on, every width needs a rule on all of its wires. So a
schema like Clément's (19) is necessary here (Clément leaves this
question open for Real-Clifford+CH). The witness is the spin double
cover, and `spin/` computes it.

From four wires on every gate is a rotation of R^(2^n): X, CX and CCX
are products of 2^(n−1), 2^(n−2) and 2^(n−3) disjoint transpositions,
and HH and H have determinant 1. So the gates lift to Spin(2^n). Let G
be the circuit group on n wires and G̃ its preimage there. G̃ is a
non-split double cover, because G contains the even permutations of
the 2^n basis states, and the spin cover restricted to them is Schur's
non-split cover 2·A_m.

Take any rules, each on at most n − 1 wires, together with the
structural rules of `Circuit.Base`, and let n ≥ 7:

- **The circuits avoiding a wire j lift.** They form the group on n − 1
  wires, acting as U ⊗ I. The cover restricted to them is split, since
  w₂(V ⊕ V) = w₁(V)² = 0 on SO(V). The splitting is unique, since that
  group is perfect from four wires on (`GeneralN/Perfect.agda`).
- **Each gate gets one lift.** Two such splittings, for wires j and k,
  agree on the circuits avoiding both, again by perfectness, now on
  n − 2 ≥ 5 wires. So every gate g has one lift g̃. Every equation
  between circuits that avoid a common wire lifts to +1, in particular
  every instance of every rule.
- **The structural rules lift to +1.** A commutation rule moves a gate
  on k ≤ 3 wires past a circuit on the other n − k ≥ 4 wires, whose
  matrix is a rotation. The cover pulled back to O(2^k) × SO(2^(n−k)) is
  split, because w₂ = C(2^(n−k), 2)·w₁² = 0. So the two lifts commute.

The group the rules present on n wires therefore maps onto G̃ ≠ G. The
true equations that lift to −1 are not derivable, so the rules are
incomplete on n wires. The argument is written for X, CX and CCX with
or without HH. For CCX+H the group on four or more wires has the
H-parity character, and the lifts of the H gates have to be chosen
compatibly; that is expected but not checked.

Below seven wires the argument does not apply. There the commutation of
two odd gates covering every wire lifts to −1, so the structural rules
themselves can kill the obstruction. `spin/test_n.py` evaluates the
lifts on the spinor space, for n ≤ 5 only (its dimension is 2^(2^(n−1))):

| n | Relation | Lift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | `CCX²` (two transpositions) | −1 |
| 5 | `CCX²`, `X²`, `CX²`, `HH²`, `H²` | +1 |
| 5 | `(C³X)²`, with `C³X = A B A B`, `A = CCX(a,b;w)`, `B = CCX(w,c;t)` | −1 |
| 5 | `[C³X, X_w]`, X on the borrowed wire w | +1 |
| 5 | `[CCX(2,3,4), CX(0,1)]` | −1 |
| 5 | `[X₀, CCX(2,3,4)]`, `[HH₀₁, CCX(2,3,4)]` | +1 |

So a schema must contain, at each large width, an equation that lifts
to −1. "The multi-controlled Toffoli, built from Toffolis with one
borrowed wire, is an involution" does. "It commutes with X on its
borrowed wire" does not.

### Completeness at every width: the plan

From four wires on the group is infinite (the commutator subgroup of
O₂ⁿ(ℤ[1/2]), `GeneralN/Commutator.agda`), so coset enumeration can refute
completeness through finite quotients but never establish it. It has to
be a proof by induction on the width, with completeness one width down
(IH) as the hypothesis, as in Clément's paper. The section above rules
out the alternative: relations on n − 1 wires never suffice.

**Half subgroups.** Fix a wire w and a bit b. The operators of the
n-wire group G_n supported on the half {x : x_w = b} form the *half
subgroup* H_{w,b}. Such an operator is I ⊕ U (or U ⊕ I) with U a
charge-00 element of O₂ⁿ⁻¹(ℤ[1/2]). From five wires on, those U are
exactly the circuits on n − 1 wires (`Subgroup/`). So H_{w,b} is the image
of a *controlled* circuit, `ctrl_w(C)` or `ctrl⁻_w(C)`, and two
controlled circuits with the same matrix are equal as soon as `ctrl_w`
respects the rules:

- **ctrl.** `ctrl_w` is defined gate by gate: X ↦ CX, CX ↦ CCX, CCX ↦ a
  Toffoli-built C³X with a borrowed wire, HH ↦ a controlled HH.
- **The ctrl lemma.** For a rule instance on k wires, its controlled
  version acts on at most k + 2 wires. Once that is fewer than n, it is
  an equation one width down, and IH proves it. The schema instances
  are the exception, so the schema is chosen to be closed under `ctrl`:
  the multi-controlled versions of a base relation, such as (C³X)² = ε.
- **The bounded facts.** "A gate is its positive-controlled part times
  its negative-controlled part" and "positive- and negative-controlled
  gates commute" act on boundedly many wires, so IH proves them too.

**The gluing theorem.** Completeness on n wires then follows if G_n is
the amalgam of its half subgroups: generated by them, with relations

- those inside each H_{w,b};
- the identification of their intersections (the quarter subgroups);
- the commutation of opposite halves.

Each of these relations is a controlled relation one width down, or a
bounded fact. This is a statement about matrix groups only.

- **Classical case, four wires.** `w3/amalgam.py` checks the classical
  analogue by coset enumeration: the amalgam of the eight copies of A₈ in
  A₁₆ has index 25740 = |A₁₆| / |A₈|² over two opposite halves, so it is
  A₁₆.
- **Classical case, every width.** It should follow from the Coxeter
  presentation of S_{2ⁿ}, whose relations involve transpositions
  supported in a common half or in opposite halves. A Reidemeister–Schreier
  argument with routing through quarters would then pass to A_{2ⁿ}.
- **Orthogonal case.** It should reduce to the classical case. The
  classical part of the amalgam is then exactly A_{2ⁿ}, so relabelling
  by classical elements is well defined there. Every relation of Table 1,
  rewritten for the charge-00 subgroup, involves at most ten basis states.
  From five wires on, those fit in one half, of 16 or more states. A
  relabelling moves them there, after which the relation holds inside
  that half.

**Base cases.** The induction step needs five wires: the half subgroups
on four wires, O₈(ℤ[1/2])'s charge-00 part, are much larger than the
controlled three-wire circuits (W(E8)). So the base cases are three wires
(W(E8), finite) and four wires (infinite) for HH. For CCX+H they are
three wires (infinite); from four wires on CCX+H follows from CCX+HH by
the decomposition H = HH ⋊ C₂ (`../../CCX+H/GeneralN/`). On three
wires the half subgroups of W(E8) are only the even signed permutations
of a face (order 192, `w3/halfw3.py`), so this argument does not reach
there. Two attempts at a derivation oracle on three wires did not work:

- A labelled Felsch enumeration over W(E7) (`w3/tower.py`) exhausted
  20 GB.
- The subgroup ⟨X, CX, HH⟩ is not presented by its own rules, even with
  "Z commutes with gates on other wires" added (`w3/qtest*.py`).

So the three-wire case needs either the Coxeter presentation of W(E8)
with hand-derived (or better searched) decoded Coxeter relations, or a
cheaper certificate.

### The controlled-circuit machinery, so far

`../NaturalP/` restates the natural rules with the swap as a gate
(`Circuit.Prop`), so that wire permutations are free. In Agda:

- `NaturalP/Bridge`: every NaturalP derivation is a Natural one, and
  completeness transfers back (`complete⇒`).
- `NaturalP/Pad`: a derivation on k wires holds on the bottom k wires of
  any wider circuit.
- `NaturalP/Ctrl`: `ctrl`, gate by gate. X, CX and the swap become XC,
  a Toffoli and the Fredkin gate. HH and CCX become CHHa and C3Xa, each
  borrowing the wire above. A gate one wire up is the gadget with the
  control moved up by swaps, borrowing wire 1 (`CTRL₁`).
- `NaturalP/CtrlSem.ctrl-sem`: for C on four or more wires, `ctrl C`
  acts on vectors as C on the coordinates with wire 0 set and as the
  identity on the others. The gadgets are checked on their own wires
  (`CtrlBase`, `CtrlBase5`) and carried up by idle wires, which commute
  with the control (`Blocks2.top-ctrl`). The swap of wires 0 1
  transposes the four low-bit blocks (`SwapBlocks`), so it moves a
  control from wire 1 to wire 0. Checking a gadget column by column
  costs about a millisecond per letter, column and entry
  (`Columns`). So the classical five-wire gadgets are compared by their
  permutations of the indices instead (`ClassicalWords`, `PermCheck`).
- `../../Symmetric-Prop/Networks`: in any PROP, two swap networks with
  the same permutation are equal. `../../Symmetric-Prop/Placement`: in
  any PROP, a circuit on the bottom k wires commutes with one shifted k
  wires up (`apart`). A placement by a network is also unchanged when
  the network changes above those k wires (`frame`).

- `NaturalP/CtrlPad`: `ctrl` and `ctrl₁` (the control with an idle spare
  wire above it) commute with idle wires on top, and `ctrl (w ↑)` is
  `ctrl₁ w`. `NaturalP/CtrlSame`: both send circuits with the same
  matrix to circuits with the same matrix.
- `../../Symmetric-Prop/Frame`, `Cycle`: in any PROP, a placement depends
  only on where the network sends the placed wires (`frame-perm`), and an
  idle wire on top is an idle wire at the bottom moved by a cycle
  (`top-is-bottom`).
- `NaturalP/CtrlMove`: a controlled gate one wire higher is the controlled
  gate with an idle wire on top, conjugated by a cycle of the wires above
  the spare (`move`).

**The ctrl lemma, given completeness on at most eight wires**
(`NaturalP/CtrlLemma`: `ctrl-cong` from four wires on, `ctrl₁-cong` at
every width). The rule instances and the swap rules are bounded, so
their controlled versions are closed equations on at most six wires,
widened (`CtrlBounded`). In a commutation rule
`[g ↥ⁱ] • [h] = [h] • [g ↥ⁱ]` the gate g can sit anywhere above h, and
the two gadgets share wire 0 and, for HH and CCX, the borrowed wire, so
their letters do not commute one by one. But if g is at the bottom of
what is above h's gadget, the controlled equation is again a closed one,
on at most eight wires. Otherwise `move` conjugates g's gadget by a cycle
that lies above h's gadget, and the equation is the same one with g one
wire lower, under an idle wire on top (`CtrlComm`).

**The schema.** By the spin-cover argument above, rules on at most four
wires are incomplete from seven wires on, so "complete on at most eight
wires" is false for NaturalP alone. `NaturalP/Schema` adds a schema:
every controlled version of `CCX • CCX = ε`: `ctrlʲ (CCX • CCX) = ε` for
every j, with CCX on four or more wires (`CtrlCCX`). This is what the
spin cover asks for. `ctrl` lifts to the inclusion of the spin group of
the controlled half, which sends −1 to −1, and the square of a lifted
involution does not depend on the lift. So `(ctrl C)²` lifts to the same
sign as `C²` one width down. `(ctrl CCX)² = C3Xa²` lifts to −1 on five
wires (the table above), so `(ctrlʲ⁺¹ CCX)²` lifts to −1 at every width
from five on. The schema is closed under `ctrl` and idle wires on top
(`padC`). `CtrlBounded`, `CtrlComm` and `CtrlLemma` are stated in the
calculus with the schema, and NaturalP's own lemmas (`move`, `ctrl₁-↑`)
enter through `emb`. `ctrl₁` of an instance is handled by the spare-wire
lemma (`NaturalP/Spare`, with the same hypothesis):
`σ • (ctrl w ↑ • σ) ≈ ctrl₁ w` (`spare-w`). It says that the spare wire
of `ctrl₁` can be exchanged with an idle one. For a gate at the bottom
it is a closed core (`NaturalP/SpareSem`). Higher up it is that the
gadget with an idle wire 0 below it is fixed by exchanging wires 0 and 2
(`swap02`), which `move` carries up.

The ctrl lemma is a step of the induction on the width. Once the width
is nine or more, it uses only completeness below the width. Below nine,
its closed cores can fill all the wires.
`NaturalP/SchemaSound`: the rules with the schema are sound at every
width (a schema instance is the identity, by `CCX²` and `ctrl-same`).

**The two colours of a control** (`NaturalP/CtrlColour`, with the same
hypothesis). A circuit controlled by wire 0 and one controlled by its
negation, `ctrl⁻ v = X • ctrl v • X`, commute (`opposite`), and a circuit
one wire up is its two controlled versions, `w ↑ ≈ ctrl w • ctrl⁻ w`
(`split-w`). These are the "bounded facts" of the plan below: the two
half subgroups of wire 0 commute, and together they contain every gate
that avoids wire 0. Semantically (`NaturalP/ColourSem`), X on wire 0
exchanges the even and odd coordinates. For single gates the proof
follows `CtrlComm`. If one gate is at the bottom or one wire up, the
other is brought down to it by `move` until the pair is a closed core on
at most eight wires. There are 113 such cores, generated by
`NaturalP/gen/gen_colour.py`. If both gates are higher up,
`NaturalP/FullMove` moves both down by the same cycle of the wires above
the spare. That is `CtrlMove`'s step with the swaps cancelled, and it
needs no closed base.

**Two controls commute** (`NaturalP/Controls`, same hypothesis). The
swap of wires 0 1 fixes `ctrl (ctrl w)` (`dsym`). So a circuit under
several controls does not depend on the order of its control wires.
Also, an uncontrolled circuit one wire up, conjugating a controlled one,
gives the controlled conjugate: `u ↑ • ctrl x • u⁻ ↑ ≈ ctrl (u • x • u⁻)`
(`conj-ctrl`, from `split-w` and `opposite`). For a gate at the bottom
or one wire up, `dsym` is a closed core. Semantically, `ctrl (ctrl C)` is
`block₂` of a symmetric function of the two control bits
(`NaturalP/DoubleSem`). Higher up, `FullMove`'s cycle comes out of the
control by `conj-ctrl` and passes the swap. `NaturalP/WordAlg` collects
the conjugation algebra over word variables. Instantiating a lemma there
never makes Agda compare two spellings of a word by evaluating them, and
`SpareSem`/`DoubleSem` record why that matters.

**Towards the induction step.** `CircuitTranslation.Complete` takes any
`Decoder`: a circuit for each auxiliary edge, with its matrix. So a
decoder built from `ctrl` can replace the physical one. Natural
completeness at a width then means deriving three things from the rules
with the schema:

- the decoded Table 1 relations at the four charges, whose
  representatives sit on the indices 0 and 1;
- the round trips;
- the recovery of each gate.

A relation whose indices all agree on some wire is `ctrl` of a relation
one width down, so it follows from completeness there and the ctrl
lemma. The rest need the decoder to commute with the classical routing
that brings indices together, as in Clément's Section 8. That is what
remains.

**What the induction step needs, as far as it is worked out.** The
route is Clément's (his Section 8), with the translated calculus of
`../Presentation.agda` in the role of his Theorem 4.10. Its pieces:

1. **A decoder built from controlled gates.** Each auxiliary atom (a
   sign pair `MM`, a double exchange `XX`, a mixed block `J`; see
   `../GeneralN/DecodingPlans.agda`) becomes a gate under controls on
   the other wires, in colours given by the atom's indices, placed by a
   swap network. An atom whose indices differ in more bits needs a
   classical routing circuit around it.
2. **A calculus of multi-controlled gates** — done in its canonical
   frame (`NaturalP/Lambda`, `LambdaLaws`, `LambdaSem`). `Λ s w` is w
   under a control pattern `s` on the bottom wires. Each position is a
   control of either colour or an idle wire. `Λ` respects derivations
   and products. Patterns with opposite colours at some position commute
   (`Λ-opp`). A black and a white control merge into an idle wire
   (`Λ-merge`). The swap of any two adjacent positions exchanges them in
   the pattern (`Λ-swap`). `Λ-sem` gives the operator. What remains is
   placement on arbitrary wires by swap networks
   (`../../Symmetric-Prop/Frame`) and X on a control flipping its colour.
3. **Recovery.** The decoded encoding of each gate is the gate (his
   Lemma 8.7).
4. **The decoded relations** (his Lemma 8.8), by induction on the width.
   A relation whose indices share a wire value is a controlled relation
   one width down; the rest need the decoder to commute with the
   routing.
5. **Base cases.** The ctrl lemma uses closed cores of up to eight
   wires. So without explicit derivations of the cores that fill all
   the wires, the step only starts at nine wires, and completeness on at
   most eight wires stays a hypothesis. Clément's step starts at three
   qubits because his Appendix D derives the small-width equations by
   hand. The analogue here would need completeness on three wires (the
   W(E8) case) and explicit derivations up to eight.

Items 1, 3 and 4 are not started. Item 1 is the crux: Clément made his
auxiliary presentation (his Figure 8) Gray-local, so that every decoded
rule compares controlled gates with a separating wire or a common
subcube. Here that would mean a Gray-local presentation of the charge-00
subgroup, proved by Reidemeister–Schreier as in his Appendix A.

**Item 1 in detail, following Clément's construction.** In the repo
his Section 8 is `../../Real-Clifford+CH/Auxiliary` and `Lemma88`.

- *Auxiliary letters on arbitrary indices.* Indices are read as Gray
  codes. A letter on two indices whose codes differ only at wire t is a
  multi-controlled gate on t (a `Λ` here). Codes that differ at more
  wires give his Lemma 8.5: the letter is that gate conjugated by
  encoded CNOTs from t to the other differing wires. Swap networks and
  X on the controls move and recolour the gate (`Λ-swap`, and the
  colour lemma still to do).
- *The Hadamard-free letters.* Here these are the sign pairs `MM` and
  double exchanges `XX`. They need a unique normal form on the auxiliary
  side (his Lemma A.4 and Corollary A.5: a sign part and a Lehmer code)
  and the decoded rules of that fragment. Together these give his
  `dA5`: two Hadamard-free words with the same signed permutation have
  equal decodings. Most decoded rules reduce to it.
- *The letters with a Hadamard block* (`J` here, his `HH` letters).
  He handles these with a few rules proved by the box calculus, together
  with his Corollaries A.7–A.10 (words with at most one or two Hadamard
  letters).
- *The presentation of the auxiliary subgroup.* He uses his Figure 8,
  derived by Reidemeister–Schreier from the two-level presentation (his
  Theorem 4.10, Appendix A). Here `../Subgroup/PresentationAt.agda`
  already presents the charge-00 subgroup by edges and translated
  Table 1 relations. The decoded relations would then be those, through
  `../GeneralN/DecodingPlans.agda`.

**The route chosen: affine routing into a window.** One can avoid both
a Gray-local presentation and the induction on the width, and reduce
everything to completeness on at most eight wires. The plan:

- *Affine circuits are complete* — done (`../NaturalP/Affine`). Two
  circuits in X, CX and the swap with the same matrix are equal.
  Erasing T and ω carries Amy, Chen and Ross's derivations
  (`../../CNOT+Dihedral`) into the calculus.
- *Affine routing.* An affine circuit sends any set of at most nine
  basis states, which spans at most an 8-dimensional affine subspace,
  into the window W₈: the states whose wires 8, 9, … are all 0. On W₈,
  every gate is `Λ` with white controls on those wires. Conjugating a
  window gate by an affine map that fixes the window pointwise changes
  nothing. Such a map is a circuit on the control wires together with
  CXs from control wires, each of which commutes with white controls.
  Affine completeness makes this a derivation, once the map is
  synthesised as such a word (Gaussian elimination at symbolic width).
- *The decoder* sends a charge-00 element supported on at most six
  basis states to K⁻¹ • Λ(c) • K. Here K routes the support into W₈,
  and c is an 8-wire circuit for the routed element.
- *Decoded relations.* Take a relation among such elements whose total
  support spans at most 8 affine dimensions. Route it into W₈; each
  letter becomes a window gate (routing plus the stabiliser argument),
  and the product is ε by completeness on eight wires and `Λ-cong`.
  The translated Table 1 relations of `../Subgroup/PresentationAt` have
  at most ten points. For the few that span 9 dimensions, one 3-cycle,
  which moves the tenth point into the span of the other nine,
  conjugates the relation into one that spans 8. Each conjugation step
  is itself a relation on at most nine points.
- *Recovery.* Each gate is the product of its window pieces. These are
  merged by `Λ-merge` over every colouring of the other wires.

So completeness on at most eight wires gives completeness at every
width. The schema enters only through `Λ-cong`, that is, through the
ctrl lemma.

**The decoder, refined.** Pushed so far: `../NaturalP/AffineRoute`
(Gaussian elimination as an affine word), `Window` and `Stab` (an affine
word that fixes the window commutes with every window gate), and `Nest`
(the same for a window inside a window). The decoder now works as
follows. The hypothesis is completeness on at most twelve wires, and
the width is 4 + k + 8.

- *Atoms on a 4-wire window.* An atom (`MM`, `XX`, `J` of
  `../GeneralN/DecodingPlans`) is routed by an affine word K. K sends
  its sorted points to fixed positions of the 4-wire window W₄ (the top
  4 wires): 0, 1, 2, then 4, or 3 when the four points are coplanar.
  Since the order is kept, the routed atom is one of a few fixed atoms
  at width 4. Its circuit c₄ is the physical decoder at width 4
  (`../GeneralN/Decoding 0`), and the decoded atom is
  `⌈K⁻¹⌉ • Λw k (Λw 8 c₄) • ⌈K⌉`. No synthesis and no relabelling of
  Table 1 words is needed.
- *Frames.* K's remaining window directions are taken from the unit
  vectors e₀ … e₃, so `K⁻¹(W₄)` lies in the affine span of the atom's
  points and R₀ = {0, e₀, e₁, e₂, e₃}. A pivot argument chooses them
  independent of the atom's own directions. A translated Table 1
  relation has at most ten points, including 0 and 1, so its frame
  spans at most 12 dimensions. An affine word B routes the frame into
  the 12-wire window. For each atom, B K⁻¹ restricted to W₄ is a 12-wire
  affine word T after a correction that fixes W₄. The correction
  disappears by `stab₂`. The relation becomes `Λw k` of a 12-wire
  identity, which completeness on twelve wires derives.
- *Correctness* of the decoder is a small calculus of "G applied at the
  coordinates of an embedding". `../../CCX+HH-TwoLevel/EmbedAction`
  supplies it for Table 1 words, and `Λ-sem` supplies it for window
  gates. A routing word conjugates it to the atom's own embedding.

**Done: from twelve wires to every width** (`../NaturalP/EveryWidth`).
If the natural rules with the schema are complete on at most twelve
wires, they are complete at every width. The proof follows the plan
above, and every step is now an Agda module:

- *Decoded atoms* (`AtomDecoder`, `AtomCases`). Each atom of an edge
  plan is either trivial (M_a M_a, X_[a,b] X_[a,b]) and decodes to ε,
  or is routed into the top four wires by an affine word K (`Route4`)
  and decodes to `⌈inv K⌉ • Λw k (Λw c c₄) • ⌈K⌉`. Its matrix is the
  atom's (`decode-correct`), by a calculus of operators acting at an
  injection of coordinates (`AtCalc`). The inner block has width
  4 + (c + 0) with c symbolic: at the literal 8, `idx` unfolds to the
  interleaved partitions at concrete sizes, and Agda hung on
  `alternating 2048`.
- *Window gates in a common frame are complete* (`WinGate`,
  `WinComplete`, `AtomWin`). Conjugating a window gate by an affine
  word B that sends its frame into the window of the top 4 + c wires
  gives Λw k of a circuit on those wires (`conj-gate`, with `stab₂`).
  So two products of window gates whose frames B sends into that window,
  and which have the same matrix, are equal, given completeness on
  4 + c wires. B comes from `RouteTo.frame`, which routes the span of a
  list of at most 4 + c vectors into the window with two routes and no
  wire rotation.
- *The decoded relations* (`Decoded`). Each round trip and each Table 1
  relation translated at a coset has its atoms' indices among at most
  eight indices (`AxIdx`, generated by `gen/gen_axidx.py`) and indices
  below 16 (`PlanArgs`, `SmallIdx`). So its frame spans at most 12
  dimensions, and the relation is derived.
- *Recovery* (`Transport`, `Merge`, `GateMono`, `GatePieces`,
  `GateDecoded`). Every gate is a gate on the top four wires moved by
  swaps (`placed`, from `Symmetric-Prop/Cycle.top-is-bottom`). Its idle
  controls are every colouring (`merge₁`, from `split-w`). So it is a
  product of window gates, one per colouring (`recover-win`). Each piece
  lists its sixteen states in increasing order (`mono-placed`), so the
  Table 1 encoding of its four-wire circuit can be moved there, and the
  piece is the decoding of that word's auxiliary walk (`piece-decoded`).
- *Assembly* (`WidthComplete`). Gate recovery, the decoder respecting
  the auxiliary presentation, and the auxiliary presentation's own
  completeness give completeness on 4 + k + c wires from completeness
  on at most eight and on 4 + c. `EveryWidth` takes c = 8.

What remains is the base: completeness on at most twelve wires. The
calculus is already known complete on one and two wires (`Natural/`),
and three wires (W(E8)) is not done.

**CCX+H from CCX+HH** (`../../CCX+H/NaturalP/`). The CCX+H rules with
the swap gate (`Syntactics`) are Natural's for H together with the
NaturalP rules for HH, HH written out as `HHw = H • H ↑`, so that θ
(HH ↦ HHw, `Theta`) sends every NaturalP rule to a rule of the same
name; `Schema` adds the schema read through θ. Then:

- `Transfer.θ-cong`: θ carries every derivation of NaturalP with the
  schema to one here (HH • HH = ε, the swap's naturality at HH and HH
  past a circuit two wires up are short derivations).
- `Elim.elim`: on four or more wires every circuit is θ a • H^p, with
  H^p on wire 0. H on a higher wire is HH on it and wire 0 followed by
  H on wire 0, and H on wire 0 passes θ of any circuit, turning it into
  θ of another: X, CX and CCX on the bottom wires become their
  conjugates by HH on wire 0 and an idle wire (a fourth one for CCX).
- `Soundness.sound`: the rules with the schema are sound at every width
  for the ordinary matrices, the swap gate read as Natural's swap word.
  A schema instance θ w reads as the expansion of the CCX+HH circuit
  τ w, whose matrix is the identity by `NaturalP/SchemaSound`.
- `Complete.from-twelve`: completeness at every width from four on,
  given completeness of CCX+HH's NaturalP with the schema on at most
  twelve wires. The parity of the number of H is a matrix invariant
  (`GeneralN/HadamardParity`), so the two circuits have the same p;
  cancelling H^p, the expansions of τ a and τ b have the same matrix,
  so τ a and τ b do (`GeneralN/HadamardEmbedding.reflect`), hence
  a ≈ b and θ a ≈ θ b. `Complete.every-width` adds the widths up to
  three: `Bridge` derives Natural's rules and transfers its
  completeness on one and two wires (`complete⇒`, and `complete₀` on
  none); three wires, where the group is infinite, is not done.

### Three wires: a derivation finder and a tower (`w3/`)

Two findings make the three-wire case look tractable.

*A derivation finder.* `w3/partial.py` derives a three-wire identity from
the natural rules by a **partial** proof-producing coset enumeration over
the two-wire group P₂ on the wires 0, 1, whose relations are free
(completeness on two wires): labels are kept as canonical words of P₂,
the two-wire relators on the wires 1, 2 are added, cosets along the
target are defined first, and the enumeration stops at a few thousand
cosets; if the target then closes at coset 0 its trace is a derivation.
`X0 CCX0 = CCX0 X0` closes after 5000 cosets (501 facts). Longer
identities need **bootstrapping** (`w3/bootstrap.py`,
`bootstrap2.py`): every identity that closes becomes an extra relator for
the next. From 28 short classical lemmas (X0 and CX0 commute with CCX0,
…) the affine identities `(CX0 CX1)⁴ = ε`, `(CXr0 CXr1)⁴ = ε` and the two
commutator relations below all close at 20000 cosets.

*A tower with short relators.* `w3/levels.py`, `levels_min.py` and
`d8rels.py` find, for

    W(E8) > W(D8) > S8 > AGL(3,2) > Z2³ > 1,   indices 135, 128, 30, 168, 8,

relators in each level's own letters (X i, CX i, CXr i = HH CX HH, CCX0,
Z1 = HH0 X1 HH0) that present the level over the next, besides the free
two-wire relations and the commutations of disjoint letters:

| Level | Three-wire relators |
| --- | --- |
| AGL(3,2) over Z2³ | `(CXr0 CXr1)⁴`, `CX0 CX1 CX0 CX1 · CXr0 · CX0 CX1 CX0 · CXr0`, the same with `CXr1` |
| S8 over AGL(3,2) | the rules CCX-sym, CCX-neg, CCX-XC |
| W(D8) over S8 | `Z1 CCX0 = CCX0 Z1`, `CX1 Z1 CX1 = Z1 · Ex1 Z1 Ex1` |
| W(E8) over W(D8) | the natural rules |

All five new relators are derived by the finder. The top level, with
labels in W(D8) kept canonical (`w3/d8norm.py`), closes at 135 cosets
with 15146 facts in the closure of its table. What remains is to emit
all of this as Agda (planned: the W(D8) presentation as pure group
theory over signed permutations, the relators' derivations, and the top
table through ThreeQubit's `FiniteExtension` and `Separation`).

**Done: three wires** (`../Natural/ThreeWire/`). The natural rules are
complete on three wires (`Natural/ThreeWire/Complete.complete`), hence
NaturalP with the schema too (`NaturalP/Three.complete₃`), so
`NaturalP/Three.every-width₄`: complete at every width given
completeness on four to twelve wires. The proof follows the tower
above, in two parts.

- *Pure part*: W(D8), the even signed permutations of the eight levels,
  is presented by the 33 relators Γ in the nine letters (`Gamma`,
  generated by `w3/emit_gamma.py`): `D8.complete`, by the tower
  Z2³ < AGL(3,2) < S8 < W(D8). Each level is `Presentation/
  CosetExtension` over the level below, with a table found by a
  labelled coset enumeration whose labels are kept canonical
  (`w3/pipe.py`; `LevelKit`, levels generated by
  `w3/emit_levels.py`), its cosets separated by an invariant of signed
  permutations that the letters below keep (`Invariants`: images
  relative to level 0; which 4-sets are planes; signs). 22 700
  derivations, about 15 minutes.
- *Circuit part* (`Circuit/`, generated by `w3/emit_circuit.py` and
  `w3/emit_separate.py`): every relator of Γ, decoded, is derived from
  the natural rules (`Relators`), the five new ones through 24 lemmas
  found by bootstrapped partial enumerations (`w3/lemmas_data.py`), with
  two-wire steps by completeness on two wires (`Widen`, 514 checks by
  one boolean each). So two decoded words with one signed permutation
  are equal (`Decode`), and the matrix of a decoded word determines its
  signed permutation (`Columns`: the columns are the action on basis
  vectors). The top table writes every circuit as such a word followed
  by one of 135 representatives (`TopTable`, 15 478 derivations), whose
  cosets are told apart by ThreeQubit's quartic signature
  (`Separate`, through `ThreeQubit/Separation.separate-signed`), and
  `CosetExtension` concludes (`Complete`).
