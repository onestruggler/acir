# Four wires: what the base case needs

`../../NaturalP/EveryWidth` reduces completeness at every width to
completeness on at most twelve wires. One, two and three wires are done.
This directory looks at four wires: the first width where the group is
infinite, and so the first base case that no enumeration can certify.

The scripts need only Python 3 (no numpy). Run them under WSL with
`python3 -B` from this directory. `check4.py` also uses `../tc`
(`gcc -O2 -o tc tc.c` in `..`).

## No finite subgroup settles it

`fin4.py` tests which subgroups generated by placed gates are finite (a
finite group has a finite orbit of e₀):

| Generators | Finite? |
| --- | --- |
| gates on wires 0, 1, 2 (W(E8)) | yes, orbit 2160 |
| X, CX, HH, SW on four wires | yes, orbit 4320; order 89 181 388 800, the Barnes–Wall group |
| X, CX, CCX, SW on four wires | yes, A₁₆ |
| W(E8) on wires 0 1 2, and HH on wires 0 3 | no |
| CCX(0; 1, 2), HH on wires 0 3, and the two-wire gates on wires 0 1 2 | no |

So four-wire identities that mix a Toffoli with an HH crossing to the
fourth wire live in an infinite group. Coset enumeration cannot decide
them.

Nor do the rules restricted to a fragment present that fragment.
`check4.py clifford` enumerates the four-wire Clifford group over its
three-wire subgroup with the Clifford-only rules: it overflows 20 million
cosets (the index is 34 560). `check3c.py` shows that this already
happens on three wires (index 2240): the natural rules derive Clifford
facts through the Toffoli. So a proof on four wires cannot treat the
Clifford and classical parts as separate vertex groups with their own
rules.

## The rule R4 is an edge relation

`R4 = (CCX(0;1,2) · HH(0,3))⁴ = CZ(1,2)` holds (`r4.py`). Write
b = HH(0,3) and a = CCX(0;1,2). Then b a b = CCZ(0,1,2) as a matrix,
a signed permutation, and R4 says exactly

    b · a · b  =  CCZ(0,1,2)   and   (a · CCZ)² = CZ(1,2).

The second identity is a signed-permutation identity. The first is an
*edge relation* of the graph of lattices g·ℤ¹⁶ with vertex group V_Z,
the signed permutations in the circuit group. It says how the step b
conjugates an element of the edge group V_Z ∩ b V_Z b.

That edge group is generated by:

- elements supported on at most three wires together with wires 0 and 3:
  X₀, X₃, Z₀, Z₃, the CNOTs between 0 and 3 or into them from 1 or 2,
  and anything on wires 1 2;
- the Toffolis CCX(0;1,2) and CCX(3;1,2).

The first kind conjugate by b inside a three-wire group, so the rules
derive their edge relations. The Toffolis need all four wires: their
edge relation is R4 and its image under the swap of wires 0 and 3,
which fixes b.

Every way of deriving b a b = CCZ from smaller facts needs a wire that is
neither a Toffoli wire nor b's partner. With one (five wires) it goes
through:

1. CCX-comm writes a = A′ · CX(3→0) · A′ · CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2).
2. HH(0,4) passes A′ and turns CX(3→0) into CZ(3,0), on three wires.

On four wires the borrowed wire and b's partner are both wire 3. So R4
is probably not derivable on four wires.

The spin cover cannot decide this (`spin4.py`). Twist each gate's lift
to Spin(16) by a fourth root of unity, so that CCX, whose square lifts
to −1, becomes an involution. Then the structural commutations of two
gates covering all four wires still lift to −1 under every twist:
`X3 CCX0 X3 CCX0`, `CX0 CX2 CX0 CX2`, `SW0 SW2 SW0 SW2` and others.
The relators therefore contain the central −1, and the rules present a
quotient of the circuit group itself, not of its cover. The amalgam argument that
proved CCXH⁴ necessary for CCX+H does not carry over, however. The swap
rules link every placement of a gate, so the rules admit no splitting
into two vertex groups.

## Why four wires is the irreducible base

- The atoms (MM, XX, and J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) need four-wire windows: a
  J on a non-planar quadruple is not in W(E8).
- r5a needs them for every relabelling. Its quadruples abcd and abce
  share three points, so both cannot be affine planes.
- The letters of r3d, r5a and r6a generate infinite groups (`t1fin.py`),
  so these relations do not lie in any finite vertex stabiliser. They are
  genuine 2-cells.
- On four wires the half subgroups (gates acting on one half of wire 3)
  are the charge-00 part of O₈(ℤ[1/2]). That group is infinite, and it
  is not the controlled three-wire circuits: controlling a CCX gives a
  single transposition, which is odd and so not a circuit.

So any proof, by translation and routing or by gluing halves, needs
genuinely four-wire derivations. The ctrl lemma's cores are the other
recurring need: controlled rules on five to nine wires.

## Infinite vertex groups for free: the subgroups P_w

The Hadamard embedding φ_w sends a three-wire CCX+H circuit to four
wires, H_i to HH(i, w), and every other gate to itself on the wires
other than w. Its image P_w is the group of circuits that use wire w
only through HH gates. Each P_w is infinite (≅ the three-wire CCX+H
group), and each is complete given R4.

Every CCX+H rule on three wires is carried by φ_w to a consequence of
the natural rules:

- three-wire completeness on a triple, after HH(i,w) HH(j,w) = HH(i,j);
- the structural rules;
- R4, for the rule CCXH⁴, whose image is R4 itself.

So `../../CCX+H/NaturalP/ThreeWire.complete₃` gives every true identity
between words over the generators of P_w (the gates avoiding w, and
every HH).

For w = 0 this is proved in Agda, with R4 as a module parameter
(`../../NaturalP/FourWire/`). `Embed` defines φ₀ as `ψ`, with H on wire
i going to HH on wires 0 and i + 1. `Transport` carries every CCX+H
derivation on three wires through ψ: the two-wire instances by `Fast`
on three wires, CCXH⁴ to R4, and the rest one wire up or by the swap
calculus. `Partner.partner-complete` then follows from CCX+H's
`complete₃`.

Every four-wire rule of the natural theory lies in some P_w or is
classical:

- The rules on a triple lie in P_w for the fourth wire.
- So do the commutations of an HH with anything.
- The rest are classical: commutations of disjoint gates spanning four
  wires, CCX-comm, and the swap rules across four wires.

So with R4 the rules present at least the amalgam of P₀ … P₃ and A₁₆,
and four-wire completeness is the statement that this amalgam is the
four-wire group.

Two checks by hand go through:

- The decoder's sign-pair identity HH(0,1) · C̄C̄X(0; 2, 3) · HH(0,1) = M₁ M₃
  lies in P₁. It needs R4: it is CCXH⁴'s shape, H on a Toffoli target.
- r1c, with its two quadruples doubled along wire 2, lies in P₂.

[CCX(0;1,2), Z₃] = 1 lies in no P_w (each word for Z₃ passes an HH
through wire 0, 1 or 2). It still follows: write CCX(0;1,2) by CCX-comm
as A′ CX(3→0) A′ CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2). Then Z₃ conjugates
each factor inside one P_w.

Single-K atoms (J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) lie in no P_w as matrices, so the
relations that contain them need this gluing.

## Classical completeness on four wires

**The classical rules present A₁₆ on four wires.** `check4c.py`
enumerates the group they present over the affine subgroup (X, CX, SW).
The index is exactly 32 432 400 = |A₁₆| / |AGL(4,2)|. The run takes
12 minutes and 4 GB with `../tc`. The affine fragment is complete at
every width (`../../NaturalP/Affine`), so the classical rules are
complete on four wires.

A certificate this size cannot go into Agda as a coset table. A proof
there needs a chain with small indices instead. The coarser checks:
`check4.py classical` gives index 6435 for the subgroup P that preserves
the partition by wire 3, and `check4b.py` shows that P's own letters do
not present P (overflow), while S₈ on three wires is presented (index
5040).

### Towards a proof in Agda: a tower of point stabilisers

A coset table of 32 million rows cannot go into Agda, so the plan is
a tower with small indices (`tower4.py`). Fix basis states one at a
time in the order 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 3, 5, 10, 12. At each step the letters
fixing the states so far generate the whole pointwise stabiliser in
A₁₆, down to A₇:

| Level | Fixed states | Letters | Group | Index of the next level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | none | 34 | A₁₆ | 16 |
| 1 | 0 | 30 | A₁₅ | 15 |
| 2–4 | add 1, 2, 4 | 24, 19, 15 | A₁₄, A₁₃, A₁₂ | 14, 13, 12 |
| 5–8 | add 8, 3, 5, 10 | 12, 10, 8, 6 | A₁₁ … A₈, Toffolis only | 11 … 8 |
| 9 | add 12 | 4 | A₇ | over the trivial group, 2520 |

The letters are the 34 placed classical gates. The other placed
letters are defined as swap conjugates of the 15 adjacent ones, so in
NaturalP they are words. Level 0's relators (`tower4.relators_tower`,
125 of them) are:

- the natural classical rules at adjacent placements;
- the structural rules of the adjacent letters;
- every letter an involution;
- the definitions of the other letters;
- one Steinberg relation, [CX(0→1), CX(1→2)] = CX(0→2)
  (`affine4.txt`).

That last one is needed: the classical rules restricted to the affine
letters do not present AGL(4, 2), and enumeration over the translations
overflows. With it, the index is 20 160. (In NaturalP itself the affine
fragment is complete through the HH rules, so the classical subgroup
of the group NaturalP presents is A₁₆; the pure presentation has no HH.)

In Agda (`../../NaturalP/FourWire/Classical/`):

- `Perm`, `Gamma` (generated by `emit_gamma4.py`, every relator sound
  by `refl`), `Pure`, `LevelKit`, `Separation` and `Stab` carry the
  pure tower, adapted from the three-wire one.
- `Circuit` (generated by `emit_circuit4.py`) gives each letter its
  circuit and derives all 125 relators in NaturalP with the schema, on
  four wires, in about 90 s. Relators on three wires go by `Kit.fast₃`,
  involutions and definitions as palindromes, commutations by
  `Circuit.Base`'s comm-gate lemmas, and the rest are the two rules
  CCX-comm and σ-nat₃.

What is left is the relators of levels 1–9. Each level needs relators
among its own letters, derived from level 0. Two attempts so far:

- Reidemeister–Schreier relators from plain labelled enumerations
  (`rs4.py`) are derived automatically. But their labels grow with the
  depth (a median of 44 letters at level 2), and a set short enough to
  enumerate does not present the next level.
- Short extra relators found by search (`ext4.py`), with labels kept
  canonical by the completeness of the level below, as in the
  three-wire tower. These need their own derivations in NaturalP. The
  levels generated by Toffolis alone need extras with words of at least
  five letters.

The extras on the Toffoli-only levels are four-wire Toffoli identities.
Level 7 has 4 of length 5, for instance
[CCX(0; 1 2), CCX(2; 1 3)] = CCX(0; 1 3), a Steinberg relation
controlled by wire 1. Level 8 has 5 of length 6. Level 9 (A₇) has 5 of
length 15–16: with relators of length at most 14 the enumeration
closes at 7560, a triple cover 3·A₇.

They are not local. `local4.py` looks for a small alphabet around an
extra whose own relators present the group it generates, and then
derives the extra by a proof-producing enumeration. It fails for 13
of the 14 extras of levels 7–9, even with the rules at every placement
(`T.relators()`).

Partial enumeration also fails so far. `partial4.py` mirrors
`../w3/partial.py`: a proof-producing enumeration over the three-wire
classical group on wires 0, 1, 2, with labels normalised by
completeness on three wires. But that group has index about 2.6·10⁸ in
A₁₆, and 100 000 cosets do not reach the coincidence.

`conj4.py` starts the conjugation calculus: every letter as a swap
conjugate of one base letter per kind, with S₄ presented by the swap
relators (24 cosets). It would derive the rules at every placement from
level 0.

So a four-wire classical proof in Agda still needs a derivation finder
for four-wire Toffoli identities. The extras are true and short, but
their derivations pass through the affine letters.

## The lattice graph g·ℤ¹⁶

`gz.py`, `gz2.py`: the vertex group V_Z is the signed permutations in
the circuit group, 2¹⁵ · A₁₆ (order 342 798 989 524 992 000). The step is
h = HH(0,1). The edge group V_Z ∩ h V_Z h has order at least 1.63·10¹⁰,
so ℤ¹⁶ has at most 21 021 000 neighbours: a partition of the levels into
four quadruples, times 8. Sampled two-step lattices h σ h ℤ¹⁶ sit at
relative positions (2-adic elementary divisors)

| Position | Share of samples |
| --- | --- |
| (−2², −1², 0⁸, 1², 2²) | 60% |
| (−2³, 0¹⁰, 2³) | 30% |
| (−2, −1⁴, 0⁶, 1⁴, 2) | 10% |
| (−1⁶, 0⁴, 1⁶) | rare |

The last is at distance one but is not a neighbour: a neighbour sits at
(−1⁴, 0⁸, 1⁴). So the local geometry has more cases than the E8 graph
of CCX+H on three wires.

## Gluing the Clifford group

`cl4glue.py` asks whether the four-wire Clifford group (order
89 181 388 800, the Barnes–Wall group) is the amalgam of the four
embedded three-wire real Clifford groups φ_w(C3) and the affine group.
`cl4glue.py calibrate` completes the three-wire Clifford presentation.
The transcribed rules miss three relators, stored in `c3_extra.txt`, and
with them the index is 2240. `cl4glue.py glue` enumerates over φ₃(C3);
the expected index is 17 280.

**It is not.** With a complete presentation of the affine group (short
relators, checked by the index 20 160 of the translations), the
enumeration closes at index 8 847 360 = 2⁹ · 17 280: the group that the
four φ_w(C3) and AGL(4, 2) present is 512 times larger than Cl4 (about
70 minutes, 4.5 GB). So Cl4 needs four-wire relations beyond its
three-wire Clifford subgroups and the affine group. Within the natural
rules these must come from the Toffoli rules, or from the gluing of
the P_w, which contain the φ_w(C3).

## The decoded relations as they stand

`dec4.py` decodes Table 1 relators at the four cosets with
`../../GeneralN/normalize.decode_dyadic`. On four wires these are long:

| Relation | Gates | HH | P_w-runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1c | about 1100–1340 | 40–48 | about 200 |
| r2f, r5a | about 2500–3000 | 84–96 | about 450 |
| r6a | about 5200–5800 | 178–190 | about 900 |

The routes, classical circuits that place each atom, make them long. A
four-wire proof would decode each atom as a classical conjugate of a few
short canonical atoms (a sign pair, a K pair, J), with classical
completeness absorbing the routes. The decoded relations would then
become short canonical relations among those atoms, one per Table 1
family and equality pattern.

## Scripts

- `r4.py`: R4 holds exactly.
- `spin4.py`: the twisted spin lifts of the rules on four wires.
- `bw16.py`: the Barnes–Wall lattice has 2025 neighbours g·CCX·BW16
  under the Clifford group (a much smaller graph than ℤ¹⁶'s).
- `tower4.py`, `ext4.py`, `rs4.py`, `pipe4.py`: the tower of point
  stabilisers for A₁₆, and its relators.
- `emit_gamma4.py`, `emit_circuit4.py`, `emit_levels4.py`: the Agda for
  the tower.
- `fin4.py`: finiteness of generated subgroups.
- `t1fin.py`: finiteness of the group generated by each Table 1
  relation's letters.
- `check4.py`, `check3c.py`: fragment presentations by coset
  enumeration.
- `lat4.py`, `nb4.py`, `vorder.py`, `ss.py`: the lattice E8 ⊕ E8 split by
  wire 3. Its stabiliser V has order |W(E8)|². The V-orbit of HH(2,3)·L
  grows fourfold per level beyond 7637, so that graph is not small
  locally.
