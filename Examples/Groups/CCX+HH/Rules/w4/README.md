# Four wires: what the base case needs

`../../NaturalP/EveryWidth` reduces completeness at every width to
completeness on at most twelve wires. One, two and three wires are done.
This directory looks at four wires: the first width where the group is
infinite, and so the first base case that no enumeration can certify.

The scripts need only Python 3 (no numpy). Run them under WSL with
`python3 -B` from this directory. `check4.py` also uses `../tc`
(`gcc -O2 -o tc tc.c` in `..`).

## No finite subgroup settles it

`fin4.py` tests which subgroups generated by placed gates are finite (a
finite group has a finite orbit of e₀):

| Generators | Finite? |
| --- | --- |
| gates on wires 0, 1, 2 (W(E8)) | yes, orbit 2160 |
| X, CX, HH, SW on four wires | yes, orbit 4320; order 89 181 388 800, the Barnes–Wall group |
| X, CX, CCX, SW on four wires | yes, A₁₆ |
| W(E8) on wires 0 1 2, and HH on wires 0 3 | no |
| CCX(0; 1, 2), HH on wires 0 3, and the two-wire gates on wires 0 1 2 | no |

So four-wire identities that mix a Toffoli with an HH crossing to the
fourth wire live in an infinite group. Coset enumeration cannot decide
them.

Nor do the rules restricted to a fragment present that fragment.
`check4.py clifford` enumerates the four-wire Clifford group over its
three-wire subgroup with the Clifford-only rules: it overflows 20 million
cosets (the index is 34 560). `check3c.py` shows that this already
happens on three wires (index 2240): the natural rules derive Clifford
facts through the Toffoli. So a proof on four wires cannot treat the
Clifford and classical parts as separate vertex groups with their own
rules.

## The rule R4 is an edge relation

`R4 = (CCX(0;1,2) · HH(0,3))⁴ = CZ(1,2)` holds (`r4.py`). Write
b = HH(0,3) and a = CCX(0;1,2). Then b a b = CCZ(0,1,2) as a matrix,
a signed permutation, and R4 says exactly

    b · a · b  =  CCZ(0,1,2)   and   (a · CCZ)² = CZ(1,2).

The second identity is a signed-permutation identity. The first is an
*edge relation* of the graph of lattices g·ℤ¹⁶ with vertex group V_Z,
the signed permutations in the circuit group. It says how the step b
conjugates an element of the edge group V_Z ∩ b V_Z b.

That edge group is generated by:

- elements supported on at most three wires together with wires 0 and 3:
  X₀, X₃, Z₀, Z₃, the CNOTs between 0 and 3 or into them from 1 or 2,
  and anything on wires 1 2;
- the Toffolis CCX(0;1,2) and CCX(3;1,2).

The first kind conjugate by b inside a three-wire group, so the rules
derive their edge relations. The Toffolis need all four wires: their
edge relation is R4 and its image under the swap of wires 0 and 3,
which fixes b.

Every way of deriving b a b = CCZ from smaller facts needs a wire that is
neither a Toffoli wire nor b's partner. With one (five wires) it goes
through:

1. CCX-comm writes a = A′ · CX(3→0) · A′ · CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2).
2. HH(0,4) passes A′ and turns CX(3→0) into CZ(3,0), on three wires.

On four wires the borrowed wire and b's partner are both wire 3. So R4
is probably not derivable on four wires. The amalgam argument that
proved CCXH⁴ necessary for CCX+H does not carry over, however. The swap
rules link every placement of a gate, so the rules admit no splitting
into two vertex groups.

## Why four wires is the irreducible base

- The atoms (MM, XX, and J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) need four-wire windows: a
  J on a non-planar quadruple is not in W(E8).
- r5a needs them for every relabelling. Its quadruples abcd and abce
  share three points, so both cannot be affine planes.
- The letters of r3d, r5a and r6a generate infinite groups (`t1fin.py`),
  so these relations do not lie in any finite vertex stabiliser. They are
  genuine 2-cells.
- On four wires the half subgroups (gates acting on one half of wire 3)
  are the charge-00 part of O₈(ℤ[1/2]). That group is infinite, and it
  is not the controlled three-wire circuits: controlling a CCX gives a
  single transposition, which is odd and so not a circuit.

So any proof, by translation and routing or by gluing halves, needs
genuinely four-wire derivations. The ctrl lemma's cores are the other
recurring need: controlled rules on five to nine wires.

## Infinite vertex groups for free: the subgroups P_w

The Hadamard embedding φ_w sends a three-wire CCX+H circuit to four
wires, H_i to HH(i, w), and every other gate to itself on the wires
other than w. Its image P_w is the group of circuits that use wire w
only through HH gates. Each P_w is infinite (≅ the three-wire CCX+H
group), and each is complete given R4.

Every CCX+H rule on three wires is carried by φ_w to a consequence of
the natural rules:

- three-wire completeness on a triple, after HH(i,w) HH(j,w) = HH(i,j);
- the structural rules;
- R4, for the rule CCXH⁴, whose image is R4 itself.

So `../../CCX+H/NaturalP/ThreeWire.complete₃` gives every true identity
between words over the generators of P_w (the gates avoiding w, and
every HH).

Every four-wire rule of the natural theory lies in some P_w or is
classical:

- The rules on a triple lie in P_w for the fourth wire.
- So do the commutations of an HH with anything.
- The rest are classical: commutations of disjoint gates spanning four
  wires, CCX-comm, and the swap rules across four wires.

So with R4 the rules present at least the amalgam of P₀ … P₃ and A₁₆,
and four-wire completeness is the statement that this amalgam is the
four-wire group.

Two checks by hand go through:

- The decoder's sign-pair identity HH(0,1) · C̄C̄X(0; 2, 3) · HH(0,1) = M₁ M₃
  lies in P₁. It needs R4: it is CCXH⁴'s shape, H on a Toffoli target.
- r1c, with its two quadruples doubled along wire 2, lies in P₂.

[CCX(0;1,2), Z₃] = 1 lies in no P_w (each word for Z₃ passes an HH
through wire 0, 1 or 2). It still follows: write CCX(0;1,2) by CCX-comm
as A′ CX(3→0) A′ CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2). Then Z₃ conjugates
each factor inside one P_w.

Single-K atoms (J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) lie in no P_w as matrices, so the
relations that contain them need this gluing.

## Classical completeness on four wires

`check4.py classical`: the classical rules give index exactly 6435 for
the subgroup P that preserves the partition by wire 3. That is
|A₁₆| / |P|. `check4b.py`: P's own letters do not present P (overflow).
S₈ on three wires is presented (index 5040). Whether the classical rules
present A₁₆ needs a finer chain. A direct enumeration over S₈ has 2.6·10⁸
cosets.

## The decoded relations as they stand

`dec4.py` decodes Table 1 relators at the four cosets with
`../../GeneralN/normalize.decode_dyadic`. On four wires these are long:

| Relation | Gates | HH | P_w-runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1c | about 1100–1340 | 40–48 | about 200 |
| r2f, r5a | about 2500–3000 | 84–96 | about 450 |
| r6a | about 5200–5800 | 178–190 | about 900 |

The routes, classical circuits that place each atom, make them long. A
four-wire proof would decode each atom as a classical conjugate of a few
short canonical atoms (a sign pair, a K pair, J), with classical
completeness absorbing the routes. The decoded relations would then
become short canonical relations among those atoms, one per Table 1
family and equality pattern.

## Scripts

- `r4.py`: R4 holds exactly.
- `fin4.py`: finiteness of generated subgroups.
- `t1fin.py`: finiteness of the group generated by each Table 1
  relation's letters.
- `check4.py`, `check3c.py`: fragment presentations by coset
  enumeration.
- `lat4.py`, `nb4.py`, `vorder.py`, `ss.py`: the lattice E8 ⊕ E8 split by
  wire 3. Its stabiliser V has order |W(E8)|². The V-orbit of HH(2,3)·L
  grows fourfold per level beyond 7637, so that graph is not small
  locally.
