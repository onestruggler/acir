# Four wires: what the base case needs

`../../NaturalP/EveryWidth` reduces completeness at every width to
completeness on at most twelve wires. One, two and three wires are done.
This directory looks at four wires: the first width where the group is
infinite, and so the first base case that no enumeration can certify.

The scripts need only Python 3 (no numpy). Run them under WSL with
`python3 -B` from this directory. `check4.py` also uses `../tc`
(`gcc -O2 -o tc tc.c` in `..`).

## No finite subgroup settles it

`fin4.py` tests which subgroups generated by placed gates are finite (a
finite group has a finite orbit of e₀):

| Generators | Finite? |
| --- | --- |
| gates on wires 0, 1, 2 (W(E8)) | yes, orbit 2160 |
| X, CX, HH, SW on four wires | yes, orbit 4320; order 89 181 388 800, the Barnes–Wall group |
| X, CX, CCX, SW on four wires | yes, A₁₆ |
| W(E8) on wires 0 1 2, and HH on wires 0 3 | no |
| CCX(0; 1, 2), HH on wires 0 3, and the two-wire gates on wires 0 1 2 | no |

So four-wire identities that mix a Toffoli with an HH crossing to the
fourth wire live in an infinite group. Coset enumeration cannot decide
them.

Nor do the rules restricted to a fragment present that fragment.
`check4.py clifford` enumerates the four-wire Clifford group over its
three-wire subgroup with the Clifford-only rules: it overflows 20 million
cosets (the index is 34 560). `check3c.py` shows that this already
happens on three wires (index 2240): the natural rules derive Clifford
facts through the Toffoli. So a proof on four wires cannot treat the
Clifford and classical parts as separate vertex groups with their own
rules.

## The rule R4 is an edge relation

`R4 = (CCX(0;1,2) · HH(0,3))⁴ = CZ(1,2)` holds (`r4.py`). Write
b = HH(0,3) and a = CCX(0;1,2). Then b a b = CCZ(0,1,2) as a matrix,
a signed permutation, and R4 says exactly

    b · a · b  =  CCZ(0,1,2)   and   (a · CCZ)² = CZ(1,2).

The second identity is a signed-permutation identity. The first is an
*edge relation* of the graph of lattices g·ℤ¹⁶ with vertex group V_Z,
the signed permutations in the circuit group. It says how the step b
conjugates an element of the edge group V_Z ∩ b V_Z b.

That edge group is generated by:

- elements supported on at most three wires together with wires 0 and 3:
  X₀, X₃, Z₀, Z₃, the CNOTs between 0 and 3 or into them from 1 or 2,
  and anything on wires 1 2;
- the Toffolis CCX(0;1,2) and CCX(3;1,2).

The first kind conjugate by b inside a three-wire group, so the rules
derive their edge relations. The Toffolis need all four wires: their
edge relation is R4 and its image under the swap of wires 0 and 3,
which fixes b.

Every way of deriving b a b = CCZ from smaller facts needs a wire that is
neither a Toffoli wire nor b's partner. With one (five wires) it goes
through:

1. CCX-comm writes a = A′ · CX(3→0) · A′ · CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2).
2. HH(0,4) passes A′ and turns CX(3→0) into CZ(3,0), on three wires.

On four wires the borrowed wire and b's partner are both wire 3. So R4
is probably not derivable on four wires.

The spin cover cannot decide this (`spin4.py`). Twist each gate's lift
to Spin(16) by a fourth root of unity, so that CCX, whose square lifts
to −1, becomes an involution. Then the structural commutations of two
gates covering all four wires still lift to −1 under every twist:
`X3 CCX0 X3 CCX0`, `CX0 CX2 CX0 CX2`, `SW0 SW2 SW0 SW2` and others.
The relators therefore contain the central −1, and the rules present a
quotient of the circuit group itself, not of its cover. The amalgam argument that
proved CCXH⁴ necessary for CCX+H does not carry over, however. The swap
rules link every placement of a gate, so the rules admit no splitting
into two vertex groups.

## Why four wires is the irreducible base

- The atoms (MM, XX, and J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) need four-wire windows: a
  J on a non-planar quadruple is not in W(E8).
- r5a needs them for every relabelling. Its quadruples abcd and abce
  share three points, so both cannot be affine planes.
- The letters of r3d, r5a and r6a generate infinite groups (`t1fin.py`),
  so these relations do not lie in any finite vertex stabiliser. They are
  genuine 2-cells.
- On four wires the half subgroups (gates acting on one half of wire 3)
  are the charge-00 part of O₈(ℤ[1/2]). That group is infinite, and it
  is not the controlled three-wire circuits: controlling a CCX gives a
  single transposition, which is odd and so not a circuit.

So any proof, by translation and routing or by gluing halves, needs
genuinely four-wire derivations. The ctrl lemma's cores are the other
recurring need: controlled rules on five to nine wires.

## Infinite vertex groups for free: the subgroups P_w

The Hadamard embedding φ_w sends a three-wire CCX+H circuit to four
wires, H_i to HH(i, w), and every other gate to itself on the wires
other than w. Its image P_w is the group of circuits that use wire w
only through HH gates. Each P_w is infinite (≅ the three-wire CCX+H
group), and each is complete given R4.

Every CCX+H rule on three wires is carried by φ_w to a consequence of
the natural rules:

- three-wire completeness on a triple, after HH(i,w) HH(j,w) = HH(i,j);
- the structural rules;
- R4, for the rule CCXH⁴, whose image is R4 itself.

So `../../CCX+H/NaturalP/ThreeWire.complete₃` gives every true identity
between words over the generators of P_w (the gates avoiding w, and
every HH).

For w = 0 this is proved in Agda, with R4 as a module parameter
(`../../NaturalP/FourWire/`). `Embed` defines φ₀ as `ψ`, with H on wire
i going to HH on wires 0 and i + 1. `Transport` carries every CCX+H
derivation on three wires through ψ: the two-wire instances by `Fast`
on three wires, CCXH⁴ to R4, and the rest one wire up or by the swap
calculus. `Partner.partner-complete` then follows from CCX+H's
`complete₃`.

Every four-wire rule of the natural theory lies in some P_w or is
classical:

- The rules on a triple lie in P_w for the fourth wire.
- So do the commutations of an HH with anything.
- The rest are classical: commutations of disjoint gates spanning four
  wires, CCX-comm, and the swap rules across four wires.

So with R4 the rules present at least the amalgam of P₀ … P₃ and A₁₆,
and four-wire completeness is the statement that this amalgam is the
four-wire group.

Two checks by hand go through:

- The decoder's sign-pair identity HH(0,1) · C̄C̄X(0; 2, 3) · HH(0,1) = M₁ M₃
  lies in P₁. It needs R4: it is CCXH⁴'s shape, H on a Toffoli target.
- r1c, with its two quadruples doubled along wire 2, lies in P₂.

[CCX(0;1,2), Z₃] = 1 lies in no P_w (each word for Z₃ passes an HH
through wire 0, 1 or 2). It still follows: write CCX(0;1,2) by CCX-comm
as A′ CX(3→0) A′ CX(3→0), with A′ = CCX(3;1,2). Then Z₃ conjugates
each factor inside one P_w.

Single-K atoms (J = M_a X_bc K_abcd) lie in no P_w as matrices, so the
relations that contain them need this gluing.

## Classical completeness on four wires

**Proved in Agda.** `../../NaturalP/FourWire/Classical/Complete.agda`:
two words in the 34 placed classical letters with one permutation of
the sixteen levels have equal circuits in NaturalP with the schema
(`classical-complete`). The proof is the tower below: the affine
tower `Aff/L0`–`L4` (norm steps for the extras), the 38 extras
(`Extras/E0`–`E37`, derived by `derive16.py`), and the A₁₆ tower
`A16/L0`–`L11` (points 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 3, 5, the pair {6, 7}, then the
colourings of orders 144, 36 and 4), each level a certificate checked
by one evaluation. Times from scratch, one process per level: level 5
(70 500 facts) 21 minutes and 10.8 GB; the others 1.5 to 12 minutes;
`Complete` 2 minutes. The extras' derivations, one definition per fact,
took about 1.5 hours.

**The classical rules present A₁₆ on four wires.** `check4c.py`
enumerates the group they present over the affine subgroup (X, CX, SW).
The index is exactly 32 432 400 = |A₁₆| / |AGL(4,2)|. The run takes
12 minutes and 4 GB with `../tc`. The affine fragment is complete at
every width, unconditionally (`../../NaturalP/AffineComplete`), so the
classical rules are complete on four wires.

A certificate this size cannot go into Agda as a coset table. A proof
there needs a chain with small indices instead. The coarser checks:
`check4.py classical` gives index 6435 for the subgroup P that preserves
the partition by wire 3, and `check4b.py` shows that P's own letters do
not present P (overflow), while S₈ on three wires is presented (index
5040).

### Towards a proof in Agda: a tower of point stabilisers

A coset table of 32 million rows cannot go into Agda, so the plan is
a tower with small indices (`tower4.py`). Fix basis states one at a
time in the order 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 3, 5, 10, 12. At each step the letters
fixing the states so far generate the whole pointwise stabiliser in
A₁₆, down to A₇:

| Level | Fixed states | Letters | Group | Index of the next level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | none | 34 | A₁₆ | 16 |
| 1 | 0 | 30 | A₁₅ | 15 |
| 2–4 | add 1, 2, 4 | 24, 19, 15 | A₁₄, A₁₃, A₁₂ | 14, 13, 12 |
| 5–8 | add 8, 3, 5, 10 | 12, 10, 8, 6 | A₁₁ … A₈, Toffolis only | 11 … 8 |
| 9 | add 12 | 4 | A₇ | over the trivial group, 2520 |

The letters are the 34 placed classical gates. The other placed
letters are defined as swap conjugates of the 15 adjacent ones, so in
NaturalP they are words. Level 0's relators (`tower4.relators_tower`,
125 of them) are:

- the natural classical rules at adjacent placements;
- the structural rules of the adjacent letters;
- every letter an involution;
- the definitions of the other letters;
- one Steinberg relation, [CX(0→1), CX(1→2)] = CX(0→2)
  (`affine4.txt`).

That last one is needed: the classical rules restricted to the affine
letters do not present AGL(4, 2), and enumeration over the translations
overflows. With it, the index is 20 160. (In NaturalP itself the affine
fragment is complete through the HH rules, so the classical subgroup
of the group NaturalP presents is A₁₆; the pure presentation has no HH.)

In Agda (`../../NaturalP/FourWire/Classical/`):

- `Perm`, `Gamma` (generated by `emit_gamma4.py`, every relator sound
  by `refl`), `Pure`, `LevelKit`, `Separation` and `Stab` carry the
  pure tower, adapted from the three-wire one.
- `Circuit` (generated by `emit_circuit4.py`) gives each letter its
  circuit and derives all 125 relators in NaturalP with the schema, on
  four wires, in about 90 s. Relators on three wires go by `Kit.fast₃`,
  involutions and definitions as palindromes, commutations by
  `Circuit.Base`'s comm-gate lemmas, and the rest are the two rules
  CCX-comm and σ-nat₃.

What is left is the relators of levels 1–9. Each level needs relators
among its own letters, derived from level 0. Two attempts so far:

- Reidemeister–Schreier relators from plain labelled enumerations
  (`rs4.py`) are derived automatically. But their labels grow with the
  depth (a median of 44 letters at level 2), and a set short enough to
  enumerate does not present the next level.
- Short extra relators found by search (`ext4.py`), with labels kept
  canonical by the completeness of the level below, as in the
  three-wire tower. These need their own derivations in NaturalP. The
  levels generated by Toffolis alone need extras with words of at least
  five letters.

The extras on the Toffoli-only levels are four-wire Toffoli identities.
Level 7 has 4 of length 5, for instance
[CCX(0; 1 2), CCX(2; 1 3)] = CCX(0; 1 3), a Steinberg relation
controlled by wire 1. Level 8 has 5 of length 6. Level 9 (A₇) has 5 of
length 15–16: with relators of length at most 14 the enumeration
closes at 7560, a triple cover 3·A₇.

They are not local. `local4.py` looks for a small alphabet around an
extra whose own relators present the group it generates, and then
derives the extra by a proof-producing enumeration. It fails for 13
of the 14 extras of levels 7–9, even with the rules at every placement
(`T.relators()`).

Partial enumeration also fails so far. `partial4.py` mirrors
`../w3/partial.py`: a proof-producing enumeration over the three-wire
classical group on wires 0, 1, 2, with labels normalised by
completeness on three wires. But that group has index about 2.6·10⁸ in
A₁₆, and 100 000 cosets do not reach the coincidence.

`conj4.py` starts the conjugation calculus: every letter as a swap
conjugate of one base letter per kind, with S₄ presented by the swap
relators (24 cosets). It would derive the rules at every placement from
level 0.

So a four-wire classical proof in Agda still needs a derivation finder
for four-wire Toffoli identities. The extras are true and short, but
their derivations pass through the affine letters.

**Partial enumeration over the affine group is such a finder.** Affine
completeness now holds at every width with no hypothesis
(`../../NaturalP/AffineComplete`), so labels in AGL(4, 2) can be kept
canonical. `partial4.py --affine` runs a proof-producing enumeration
of A₁₆ over AGL(4, 2), seeded with the target's prefixes and stopped at
a limit, and then traces the target. Results:

- Level 7: all four extras are derived, in about 25 s each. The closed
  derivations have 3 000–8 500 facts, a third of them norm steps.
- Level 8: three of the five extras are derived.
- Level 9: none of the length-15 and -16 extras is derived at 100 000
  cosets.

The bottom of the tower is the obstacle, and the reason is the Schur
multiplier of A₇, which has order 6. In its four Toffoli letters, A₇
needs a relator of length 15 or 16: shorter ones present the triple
cover 3·A₇. With four more double transpositions as letters, short
relators present the double cover 2·A₇ (index 5040). So any short
presentation of the bottom by double transpositions lands in a cover,
and the relation that kills the centre has to be derived through
letters outside the bottom. The affine finder does not reach it at
300 000 cosets.

**The rules derive the short four-wire Clifford relations.** The
four-wire Clifford group is 512 times smaller than the gluing of its
three-wire Clifford subgroups with AGL(4, 2) (above), so it has
genuinely four-wire relations. `cl4derive.py` takes every relator
w₁·w₂⁻¹ with words of at most three Clifford letters, spanning all four
wires and not a plain commutation. There are 378 classes up to
rotation, reversal and relabelling of the wires. All 378 are derived
by the affine finder from the natural rules with R4, at 30 000 cosets
each, in about an hour in all.

The same test with the Toffolis (`g4derive.py 3 20000`, all 40 placed
letters, exact matrices) finds 926 classes. The finder derives 908 of
them at 20 000 cosets. The 18 it misses are classical relations among
Toffolis, or such a relation conjugated by an HH. The classical tower
derives every classical relation, so none of the 18 is evidence against
completeness on four wires with R4.

**A bottom without the triple cover.** Instead of fixing a ninth
point, take the setwise stabiliser of a pair the level-7 letters can
swap. For {6, 7}, the seven level-7 letters preserving the pair
generate the whole stabiliser, S₇ (order 5040, index 36 in A₉). One of
them swaps 6 and 7 and acts on the other seven points as a
transposition, so conjugation by it inverts the centre of 3·A₇.
Relators of length at most 10 present S₇ over the trivial group: the
enumeration closes at 5040 with words of at most 5 letters a side.

So the classical tower can be: the eight point-stabiliser levels
(indices 16 down to 9), then S₇ (index 36), then a chain inside S₇.
Every extra relator is short enough for the affine finder.

S₇ over the trivial group is too big for Agda: its labelled enumeration
makes 4.4 million facts. Inside S₇ the chain goes on by subgroups that
the letters generate and that are the stabilisers of their orbit
colourings, so that the cosets are separated by the colour of each
level's image (`../../NaturalP/FourWire/Classical/StabCol`). A chain by
points (S₆, then S₅) needs relators longer than twelve letters; the
chain through the subgroups of order 144, 36 and 4 (indices 35, 4, 9,
4) closes with three new extras of length 4 to 6
(`tower16.py --extend`, `tower16extra.txt`, 38 extras in all).

**All 38 extras are derived** (`derive16.py`, `derive16.pkl`). A first
pass derives 22 of them over AGL(4, 2) at 100 000 cosets; a second
(`--again`) adds the derived ones as relators and gets the rest at
150 000. At 300 000 cosets the enumerator needs more than 7 GB. The
derivations have 50 to 15 700 facts each.

**Agda.** The affine tower (`Classical/Aff/L0`–`L4`, `emit16.py aff`)
typechecks, so `Aff.L0.complete` gives the norm steps of the extras'
derivations. It first took 430 s at the 7 GB cap for L0 alone, for a
reason worth knowing: a use of a definition that comes from a
parameterised module application (`normE` from `LevelKit ιX ιB
Below.complete`, each level's `complete` from `Checked`) carries the
module's parameters into the term, and since each level's `complete` is
a parameter of the level above, the terms nest through the tower. The
profile showed 0.8 s of typing against 20 s of serialization,
positivity, dead-code and termination passes. Re-exporting through
plain definitions (`normB = normE` in each Base, `complete = complete′`)
brings every level under 20 s and 0.7 GB.

The cost, roughly:

- The tables plus the extras' derivations come to tens of thousands of
  facts.
- The four-wire group would add one canonical decoded relation per
  Table 1 family, charge and overlap with the coset representatives'
  indices 0 and 1: some hundreds. Each fine-grained derivation has
  thousands of facts.

That is too much for Agda unless the derivations are coarse: whole
steps inside one P_w (by `Partner.partner-complete`) or inside the
classical group (by classical completeness), as the three-wire CCX+H
proof used Θ-steps. Every four-wire rule lies in some P_w or is affine,
so a fine derivation is in principle a chain of such steps. But it
cannot be compressed after the fact. A derivation of the affine finder
is a tree of facts with heavy sharing, and replaying it as one sequence
of rewrites (`compress4.py`) runs out of memory on the CCZ identity.
So a finder whose moves are such steps (a contraction over P₀ … P₃ and
the affine group, with normal forms in each) is the next tool to build.

The same finder works with HH (`g4partial.py`). The letters gain the
six placed HH, and the relators gain the HH rules of `../natural3.py`
at adjacent placements. It derives that CCZ(0,1,2) and CCZ(1,2,3)
commute, written through HH(0,3) and HH(0,1), in 14 s. It does not
derive R4 unless R4 is a relator, as expected.

## The lattice graph g·ℤ¹⁶

`gz.py`, `gz2.py`: the vertex group V_Z is the signed permutations in
the circuit group, 2¹⁵ · A₁₆ (order 342 798 989 524 992 000). The step is
h = HH(0,1). The edge group V_Z ∩ h V_Z h has order at least 1.63·10¹⁰,
so ℤ¹⁶ has at most 21 021 000 neighbours: a partition of the levels into
four quadruples, times 8. Sampled two-step lattices h σ h ℤ¹⁶ sit at
relative positions (2-adic elementary divisors)

| Position | Share of samples |
| --- | --- |
| (−2², −1², 0⁸, 1², 2²) | 60% |
| (−2³, 0¹⁰, 2³) | 30% |
| (−2, −1⁴, 0⁶, 1⁴, 2) | 10% |
| (−1⁶, 0⁴, 1⁶) | rare |

The last is at distance one but is not a neighbour: a neighbour sits at
(−1⁴, 0⁸, 1⁴). So the local geometry has more cases than the E8 graph
of CCX+H on three wires.

## Gluing the Clifford group

`cl4glue.py` asks whether the four-wire Clifford group (order
89 181 388 800, the Barnes–Wall group) is the amalgam of the four
embedded three-wire real Clifford groups φ_w(C3) and the affine group.
`cl4glue.py calibrate` completes the three-wire Clifford presentation.
The transcribed rules miss three relators, stored in `c3_extra.txt`, and
with them the index is 2240. `cl4glue.py glue` enumerates over φ₃(C3);
the expected index is 17 280.

**It is not.** With a complete presentation of the affine group (short
relators, checked by the index 20 160 of the translations), the
enumeration closes at index 8 847 360 = 2⁹ · 17 280: the group that the
four φ_w(C3) and AGL(4, 2) present is 512 times larger than Cl4 (about
70 minutes, 4.5 GB). So Cl4 needs four-wire relations beyond its
three-wire Clifford subgroups and the affine group. Within the natural
rules these must come from the Toffoli rules, or from the gluing of
the P_w, which contain the φ_w(C3).

## The decoded relations as they stand

`dec4.py` decodes Table 1 relators at the four cosets with
`../../GeneralN/normalize.decode_dyadic`. On four wires these are long:

| Relation | Gates | HH | P_w-runs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| r1c | about 1100–1340 | 40–48 | about 200 |
| r2f, r5a | about 2500–3000 | 84–96 | about 450 |
| r6a | about 5200–5800 | 178–190 | about 900 |

The routes, classical circuits that place each atom, make them long. A
four-wire proof would decode each atom as a classical conjugate of a few
short canonical atoms (a sign pair, a K pair, J), with classical
completeness absorbing the routes. The decoded relations would then
become short canonical relations among those atoms, one per Table 1
family and equality pattern.

**How many.** The Schreier generators sit at the four states, whose
representatives use only the levels 0 and 1. So the decoded relations
need only be derived up to the classical permutations that fix 0 and 1:
A₁₄ on the other fourteen levels is twelve-transitive, and no relation
has more than eight indices. `patterns4.py` counts the classes: each
index is 0, 1 or another level, and the other levels are relabelled in
order of first occurrence. The 21 families of Table 1 give 109
patterns, so there are 436 canonical decoded relations on four wires,
against about 4.4 million translated equations over 7824 edges.

**What a four-wire proof needs, in this order.**

1. Classical completeness on four wires (above; in Agda now).
2. A decoder equivariant under those permutations. An atom at the
   levels x decodes to φ(R x)⁻¹ · core · φ(R x), where R x is a
   classical word sending x to canonical levels taken from the tower's
   chain (0, 1, 2, 4, …). Then φ(σ) D(atom at x) φ(σ)⁻¹ ≈ D(atom at σx)
   reduces to: the permutation R(σx) σ R(x)⁻¹ fixes the canonical
   levels, so by the tower's normal form it is a word in the letters of
   the matching level, and each such letter commutes with the core.
   That is a few dozen fixed four-wire identities for the finder.
3. The 436 canonical relations and the 12 roundtrip schemas, by the
   finder with R4. The proof of each Table 1 instance is then a case
   split on its equality pattern, the routing, and one canonical
   relation.

R4 itself stays a hypothesis (as for P₀) unless it is derived.

What the 436 are made of (`canon4.py`): an edge is a signed
permutation, from an M or an X, or a signed permutation times one K.
Counting the K-edges in each canonical relation:

| K-edges | Relations | Families |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 172 | r1a, r1b, r2a, r2b, r2d, r3a–r3c |
| 2 | 180 | r1c, r2c, r2e, r3d–r3g, r4a, r4c |
| 3, 4, 8 | 84 | r4b (16), r2f (36), r5a (16), r6a (16) |

The first 172 lie in the signed permutations V_Z (2¹⁵ · A₁₆). They
follow from completeness for V_Z, the classical tower extended by the
signs. The next 180 say that a K squares to one (r1c) or that a signed
letter conjugates a K to another K (the commutations r2c, r2e, r4a and
the relabellings r3d–r3g, r4c). Both follow from a decoder of the
K-edges that is equivariant under V_Z: each K-edge decoded as a
signed permutation conjugate of a few fixed cores, whose stabilisers
commute with them. So the genuinely new four-wire content is 84
relations between two or more K's. Those are r4b (the mixed atom),
r2f (K's on disjoint quadruples commute), r5a and r6a, each with its
equality patterns and four states.

**The kickback, a rule stronger than R4.** Completeness for V_Z needs
the conjugation of each monomial gate (−I, Z, CZ, CCZ) by each
classical letter (`diag4.py`; 615 identities, 170 of them spanning four
wires). The finder derives 134 of the 170, with rules at every placement,
R4, and the short identities inside each P_w and among the classical
letters as relators (`../wn/finder.py`, `--coarse 2`). The other 36 are
all of one kind: a Toffoli kicking back a CCZ through its target,

    K_CZ :  CCX(t;a,b) · CZ(t,c)    · CCX(t;a,b) = CZ(t,c)    · CCZ(a,b,c)
    K_CCZ:  CCX(t;a,b) · CCZ(t,a,c) · CCX(t;a,b) = CCZ(t,a,c) · CCZ(a,b,c)

on four distinct wires. R4 is the same kickback with c = b, where it
gives CZ(a,b). With every other identity as a lemma the finder still
derives none of them. Under ctrl they are the controlled forms of
three-wire kickbacks, but the controlled Toffoli needs a fifth wire.
`../wn/kick.py`, `kick2.py`:

- with K_CCZ as a rule at every placement, all 12 K_CZ are derived;
- with K_CZ as a rule, none of the K_CCZ is;
- **with K_CCZ as a rule and without R4, R4 is derived** (40 s).

So K_CCZ subsumes R4 and K_CZ, and with it as a rule all 36 are
derived (`diag4.py --kick`), so all 170 four-wire monomial identities
are. The natural rules with R4 are probably
not complete on four wires, and the four-wire rule to add is K_CCZ:
a Toffoli and a CCZ that share the target and one control commute up
to the CCZ on the other three wires.

A first test of step 2 (`stabs4.py`): the canonical sign pair
s = M₀M₁ is X1 X2 X3 · HH03 · CCX3;12 · HH03 · X1 X2 X3. The finder
derives its commutation with the 12 affine letters fixing the levels
0 and 1 and with X0, about 8 s each. It derives none of the 12
Toffolis, even at 150 000 cosets: those are commutations of gates with
controls of opposite colours on a shared wire. They come instead from
P₀, which is complete given R4 and contains s (s uses wire 0 only
through HH03). A Toffoli avoiding wire 0, such as CCX3;12, commutes
with s in P₀. One on wire 0 is a commutator of such a Toffoli with a
CNOT, CCX0;12 = [CCX3;12, CX3>0], and the CNOT commutes by the finder.
So the decoder's identities should be found with coarse P_w steps as
well as the finder's fine ones.

**The order of work, as it now stands.**

1. Classical completeness on four wires: the A₁₆ tower, as certificates
   (`emitcert16.py`, `../../../../Presentation/Certificate.agda`).
2. Completeness for V_Z, signed permutations, as A₁₆ and the 15 monomial
   gates. The normal form is a set of monomials times a classical word.
   It needs the 615 monomial identities. With K_CCZ as a rule the
   finder derives the four-wire ones; the others are on three wires or
   in some P_w.
3. A finder for G₄ over V_Z: the partial enumeration of the cosets of
   V_Z (the lattices g·ℤ¹⁶, as in the three-wire CCX+H proof), with
   labels kept canonical by step 2. Its derivations of the K-cores'
   stabiliser commutations, the cores' transitions between states, and
   the 84 relations between several K's become certificates.
4. The equivariant decoder, the case split on patterns, the roundtrip,
   and the assembly with `CircuitTranslation` at width four.

The derivations of steps 2 and 3 cite placed instances of the rules;
each needs its NaturalP proof once (a swap conjugate of an adjacent
instance), and the short P_w identities need `Partner`, which needs R4,
which K_CCZ gives.

**Beyond four wires.** `../../NaturalP/EveryWidth` needs completeness
on up to twelve wires, because affine routing preserves the affine
dimension of a relation's indices and that can reach twelve. Classical
routing does not: A_{2ⁿ} moves any ten levels into the sixteen of a
four-wire window. With classical completeness at every width, the
same equivariant decoder would lower the base to four wires, once the
completeness on at most eight wires that the ctrl lemma uses is
replaced by derivations of the finitely many identities it is applied
to: the controlled rules and the commutation cores. Classical
completeness at every width could come the same way: Clément's
argument for A_N with Carmichael's presentation by the 3-cycles
(0 1 i), whose relations x_i³ = (x_i x_j)² = 1 involve four levels
only, so the windows have at most four wires.

## A finder over V_Z

`g4vz.py` enumerates the cosets of V_Z (the lattices g·ℤ¹⁶) instead of
AGL(4, 2). The subgroup's generators are the classical letters and the
14 monomial gates. A label is kept canonical by its signed permutation:
the algebraic normal form of its signs, then the A₁₆ tower's canonical
word for its permutation (`canonA16.py`, which pickles the tower's
representatives). A norm step is an instance of completeness for V_Z.
It is much stronger on the K content. The merge of a white- and a
black-controlled HH (the controlled-HH gadget of NaturalP/Ctrl) into
HH(0,1), a 35-letter identity, is derived in 67 s (`merge4.py`); the
AGL finder does not derive it.

`g4dec.py` decodes the canonical K relations into circuits. An M- or
X-edge becomes its canonical V_Z word. A K-edge L · K_A · R becomes
T · S⁻¹ · ρ · J₀ · ρ⁻¹ · S, with J₀ = q s q s (q the controlled HH
under a white control, s = M₀M₈; 54 letters). Decoded this way the
canonical r2f relations have about 465 letters, and the finder does
not derive them at 20 000 cosets. So the K relations need
intermediate lemmas about the controlled HH (merges, commutations
with signed letters), or a decoder with shorter cores.
