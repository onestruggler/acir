# Completeness for every width: the plan (2026-10-15)

Source: the joint paper (`scratchpad/joint/clifford-cs-clement-style.pdf`,
sources in its zip, `stability-notes.md`), Sections 5-7: a stability
theorem (complete on n - 1 qubits and the controlled versions of the
widest equations derivable => complete on n) and its five-qubit
instance.  Decision (user): the controlled versions of our three extra
four-wire rules (lf, hyb-sq, hyb-yb) are not reachable by the paper's
tools (box normal forms get stuck on a controlled K next to a box;
de-borrowing needs a two-qubit piece of determinant +-1, and a
controlled K or Toffoli has the wrong determinant on four wires), so
they become five-wire rules next to AX, and the schema of iterated
controlled versions covers all four.  Final theorem:

    our rules (BS23 + cx-ccz, toff-ccz, ck-tct, lf, hyb-sq, hyb-yb)
      + AX + lambda(lf) + lambda(hyb-sq) + lambda(hyb-yb)
      + lambda^k of those four, k >= 1
    is complete on every number of qubits.

## Ingredients

1. **The controlled functor lambda^b_t** (Circuit n -> Circuit (n+1), the
   new wire t on top, letterwise): iI -> S_t, S_v -> CS_tv, K_v -> CK_tv,
   CS_xy -> Sigma(x; t, y | u, A), lambda^0 = X_t lambda^1 X_t.  Sigma is a
   fixed five-wire gadget placed among the wires: Lambda_5(-I; u) .
   [Toff(A; t, y), CS(x, A)], u, A two other domain wires (borrowed: used
   and restored).  Needs a domain of at least four wires.  Semantics:
   lambda^1_t(w) is w controlled by t (one evaluation of Sigma on five
   wires, then the homomorphism).  Independent of Syntactics: can be
   written now.
2. **The five-wire rules** AX, lambda(lf), lambda(hyb-sq), lambda(hyb-yb),
   and the schema lambda^k of them (k >= 1) at every width >= 5 + k.
   Soundness: evaluation on five wires, and the semantics of lambda for
   the schema.  Touches Syntactics: wait until the four-qubit
   verification on the other machine is done.
3. **Transport** (paper, Lemma transport): n-1 |- u = v  =>  n |- lambda^b_t u
   = lambda^b_t v, given the controlled version of every rule (F1) and of
   the structural rules (F2), and the lambda-calculus (F0 borrowed-role
   switch, F4 lambda^0 and lambda^1 commute, F5 g = lambda^0 g lambda^1 g).
   At n = 5 these are the paper's ~825 Python-checked derivations from
   four-wire windows (joint/run_all.py --dump, deb_all.py), plus our
   three new rules taken as axioms; from n = 6 on they are windows (one
   width down) or schema instances.
4. **The decoding for n >= 5**: G_n(V) a fully controlled SU(2) gate
   (G_5(V) = lambda^0_4(G_4(V)), G_4(V) any four-wire circuit with that
   semantics), and a two-level letter V_(p,q) decoded as alpha G_n(V)
   alpha^-1 with alpha an affine circuit (X, CX) sending 0, e_0 to p, q.
   Independence of alpha: the stabiliser of 0, e_0 is generated by CX_{u->v}
   (u != 0) = lambda^1_u(X_v), which commutes with lambda^0_u(..) (F4); for
   v = t at n = 5 the paper's F6.  Our P' letters (pp, px, dx, ki) are
   products of at most three two-level SU(2) matrices, so they decode as
   products of such conjugates; Theorem410 (completeness of P', every
   N >= 16) plays the role of the paper's Theorem 4.4 (RSpres).
5. **Lemma 8.8 at n** (the paper's "Relations"): every rule of P' has at
   most five indices (to check) -> they lie in an affine hyperplane -> an
   affine tau maps it to {x_t = 0} -> the decoded rule is lambda^0_t of an
   (n-1)-wire equation between circuits of equal semantics, derivable by
   completeness at n - 1, and transport carries it.
6. **Lemma 8.7 at n** (the paper's "Encoding"): for a gate g pick t not on
   g and a word w' for g on n - 1 wires; w_g = iota_0(w') iota_1(w')
   decodes to lambda^0_t(g') lambda^1_t(g') = g (F5), so Lemma87.Decodes
   (by-decodes) gives D o E.
7. **Assembly**: CompletenessInduction with completeness one width down;
   complete at every width.

## Checks and refinements (2026-10-15)

- Every rule of P' (Auxiliary/Rules) has at most five indices: ki-ki,
  ki-px and ki-yb have five, the rest four or fewer.  So the decoded
  sides of a rule instance lie in an affine hyperplane from n = 5 on.
- P' is not closed under the affine maps of the indices (K_[a,b] is not
  K_[b,a]), so the hyperplane argument must not map letters to letters.
  It does not need to: decode every P' letter as a fixed product of at
  most three two-level SU(2) gadgets, each alpha G_n(V) alpha^-1
  (pp a b = (iZ)_(a,b); px c a b = (-I)_(c,a) (ZX)_(a,b) when c is not
  a, b; dx a b c d = three factors; ki a b s = (K D)_(a,b) (iZ-type)_(a,s)),
  and conjugate decoded circuits only: tau alpha G alpha^-1 tau^-1 =
  (tau alpha) G (tau alpha)^-1, independence of alpha replaces tau alpha by
  one fixing the hyperplane, and G_n(V) = lambda^0_t(G_{n-1}(V)).
- The paper's F-items at n = 5 rest on four-wire windows and the F1 of
  QCA's rules; our rules contain QCA's and are complete on four qubits,
  so they hold for us unchanged (no Python rerun needed); the F1 of lf,
  hyb-sq, hyb-yb are the new rules.
- What has to be built in Agda first is a five-wire derivation pipeline
  like Four/ (macro letters at width 5 with canonical spellings, the
  window step through complete4, an emitter from joint/derivn.py's
  derivation format), since the F-items, the representation lemma and
  F6 all arrive as five-wire derivations.

## Every width: the transport (2026-10-17, branch clifford-cs, Stable/)

The schema is a layer of its own (Stable.Rules: Five.Rules through
`five`, plus `ctl k p` over the index family `Wide` of the five-wire
rules), so the 250 checked Five modules stay valid.  Stable.Transport
proves transport at every width from completeness on five wires and ten
closed equations (`Hyp`):

* Switch₆ (2, six wires): Σ(1;5,2|3,4) = Σ(1;5,2|0,3) and
  Σ(2;5,3|1,4) = Σ(2;5,3|0,1).  Unavoidable for any λ¹: with four domain
  wires the CS on 1 2 and 2 3 must borrow below, one wire up they borrow
  above, so `↑` and `top` cannot both commute with λ¹ on the nose.
* comm₁-base (2, six wires): the CS on the top two wires of a five-wire
  domain against S / K on wire 0.
* comm₂-base₁ (2, six wires): S / K on the top wire of five against the
  CS on wires 0 1.
* comm₂-base₂ (4, six to nine wires): the CS on the top two wires of a
  domain of 5..8 wires against the CS on wires 0 1 (their images borrow
  the two wires below the top pair, and 2 3: together every wire).

Everything else reduces a width down: rules by `top` (Untop, Coherence),
the schema by definition, commutations with the upper gate below the
top by `top`, with it at the top through `place 1` (S/K on wire 0) or
`place 4` (CS on 0 1; from six wires for scalar/S/K on top, from ten for
CS on top).

Next, to discharge the ten facts:
1. Placement calculus: place i (place j c) = place (j+1) (place i c) for
   i <= j.  The networks N1 = (cyc j)^ . cyc i and N2 = (cyc i)^ .
   cyc (j+1) satisfy N2 = Swap01 . N1 as permutations (checked, n <= 9),
   and Swap01 passes c^^ (low-comm); PermCalc.perm-= turns the
   permutation equation into one of networks.
2. Windows in the new theory: two placed circuits `place j u`, `place j v`
   with equal semantics are equal given completeness one width down
   (place-cong-y and the completeness hypothesis; Place.lemma-5-1's
   argument).
3. From seven wires on: each fact is a chain of windows -- switch one
   borrowed wire at a time (an equation on six wires: x, y, t, u, A and
   the new wire), then commute on five.  E.g. comm2-base2 at width 7:
   re-borrow CS01's image to {4,5} and CS45's to {0,1}; the union misses
   wires 2, 3.
4. Six wires: b6.py's derivations (SW-B via the lemma CZ12-B, Sbox-a),
   over the five-wire boxes of Five.Letters placed at width six.

After that, Theorem S proper: the decoding at width n (G_n = λ⁰(G_{n-1}),
independence of α, the affine-hyperplane argument for Lemma 8.8, Lemma
8.7 by ι0/ι1 halves), i.e. Five's Complete5 generalised to n >= 6.

### The six-wire facts (2026-10-17, Stable/Six/)

All seven six-wire equations of `Stable.HypFrom.Six` are derived from
completeness on five wires (`Stable.Six.All.six`), so transport into at
most N wires needs completeness on five wires and on six to N − 1, and
nothing else (`hyp′`, `transport′`).  The route is b6.py's:

* Engine: gates `gk ρ c` placed by swap networks; a window re-places the
  gates of a segment and of its replacement under a network carrying an
  idle wire to the top and decides the five-wire lines by one
  evaluation; `transport` places a lemma's lines again along a network.
* Pass2/Pass1/Dance: the box with box wire 3, as t Q t Q, passes
  CX(0→3) and CX(3→0) (copies on wire 0 commuted through the two-wire
  pieces of a CCZ and cancelled), hence the swap; renamed letter by
  letter, its box wire is on wire 0.  Su: Σ(1;5,2|3,4) = Σ(1;5,2|0,4).
* Switch (Switch₆), Comm1 (comm₁-base), Comm2 (comm₂-base₁), Comm3
  (comm₂-base₂ at six wires): F0 windows, respelling windows and Su
  transported along wire maps; comm₂-base₂ moves the first S-box to
  borrow {0, 1} and the second {3, 4}, which leaves wire 2 idle.

Cost: a five-wire window with one S-box a side peaks near 6.3 GB (about
85 s), with two a side 7.5 GB (140 s); one module per such window,
checked one process at a time (chkall.sh).
