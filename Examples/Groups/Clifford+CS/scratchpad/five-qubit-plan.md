# Completeness for every width: the plan (2026-10-15)

Source: the joint paper (`scratchpad/joint/clifford-cs-clement-style.pdf`,
sources in its zip, `stability-notes.md`), Sections 5-7: a stability
theorem (complete on n - 1 qubits and the controlled versions of the
widest equations derivable => complete on n) and its five-qubit
instance.  Decision (user): the controlled versions of our three extra
four-wire rules (lf, hyb-sq, hyb-yb) are not reachable by the paper's
tools (box normal forms get stuck on a controlled K next to a box;
de-borrowing needs a two-qubit piece of determinant +-1, and a
controlled K or Toffoli has the wrong determinant on four wires), so
they become five-wire rules next to AX, and the schema of iterated
controlled versions covers all four.  Final theorem:

    our rules (BS23 + cx-ccz, toff-ccz, ck-tct, lf, hyb-sq, hyb-yb)
      + AX + lambda(lf) + lambda(hyb-sq) + lambda(hyb-yb)
      + lambda^k of those four, k >= 1
    is complete on every number of qubits.

## Ingredients

1. **The controlled functor lambda^b_t** (Circuit n -> Circuit (n+1), the
   new wire t on top, letterwise): iI -> S_t, S_v -> CS_tv, K_v -> CK_tv,
   CS_xy -> Sigma(x; t, y | u, A), lambda^0 = X_t lambda^1 X_t.  Sigma is a
   fixed five-wire gadget placed among the wires: Lambda_5(-I; u) .
   [Toff(A; t, y), CS(x, A)], u, A two other domain wires (borrowed: used
   and restored).  Needs a domain of at least four wires.  Semantics:
   lambda^1_t(w) is w controlled by t (one evaluation of Sigma on five
   wires, then the homomorphism).  Independent of Syntactics: can be
   written now.
2. **The five-wire rules** AX, lambda(lf), lambda(hyb-sq), lambda(hyb-yb),
   and the schema lambda^k of them (k >= 1) at every width >= 5 + k.
   Soundness: evaluation on five wires, and the semantics of lambda for
   the schema.  Touches Syntactics: wait until the four-qubit
   verification on the other machine is done.
3. **Transport** (paper, Lemma transport): n-1 |- u = v  =>  n |- lambda^b_t u
   = lambda^b_t v, given the controlled version of every rule (F1) and of
   the structural rules (F2), and the lambda-calculus (F0 borrowed-role
   switch, F4 lambda^0 and lambda^1 commute, F5 g = lambda^0 g lambda^1 g).
   At n = 5 these are the paper's ~825 Python-checked derivations from
   four-wire windows (joint/run_all.py --dump, deb_all.py), plus our
   three new rules taken as axioms; from n = 6 on they are windows (one
   width down) or schema instances.
4. **The decoding for n >= 5**: G_n(V) a fully controlled SU(2) gate
   (G_5(V) = lambda^0_4(G_4(V)), G_4(V) any four-wire circuit with that
   semantics), and a two-level letter V_(p,q) decoded as alpha G_n(V)
   alpha^-1 with alpha an affine circuit (X, CX) sending 0, e_0 to p, q.
   Independence of alpha: the stabiliser of 0, e_0 is generated by CX_{u->v}
   (u != 0) = lambda^1_u(X_v), which commutes with lambda^0_u(..) (F4); for
   v = t at n = 5 the paper's F6.  Our P' letters (pp, px, dx, ki) are
   products of at most three two-level SU(2) matrices, so they decode as
   products of such conjugates; Theorem410 (completeness of P', every
   N >= 16) plays the role of the paper's Theorem 4.4 (RSpres).
5. **Lemma 8.8 at n** (the paper's "Relations"): every rule of P' has at
   most five indices (to check) -> they lie in an affine hyperplane -> an
   affine tau maps it to {x_t = 0} -> the decoded rule is lambda^0_t of an
   (n-1)-wire equation between circuits of equal semantics, derivable by
   completeness at n - 1, and transport carries it.
6. **Lemma 8.7 at n** (the paper's "Encoding"): for a gate g pick t not on
   g and a word w' for g on n - 1 wires; w_g = iota_0(w') iota_1(w')
   decodes to lambda^0_t(g') lambda^1_t(g') = g (F5), so Lemma87.Decodes
   (by-decodes) gives D o E.
7. **Assembly**: CompletenessInduction with completeness one width down;
   complete at every width.
