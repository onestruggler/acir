# Four-bit reversible circuits over X, CX and CCX

*Block Normal Forms and Certified Equations for Four-Bit Reversible
Circuits* (`pldi/four-bit-astra/`), formalised on this repository's `Word`,
`Presentation` and `Circuit` framework. `MainTheorem.agda` is the entry point:

- `sound`, `complete`: the theory T of Figure 1 (rules (1)–(7)) with the
  symmetric monoidal laws is sound and complete for four-wire circuits over
  X, CX and CCX (Theorem 1.1); `complete₁`, `complete₂`, `complete₃` on
  one, two and three wires (Propositions B.1–B.3);
- `presentation₄`: the four-wire circuits modulo T present Alt(16), and
  `presentation₁`–`presentation₃` present Sym(2), Sym(4), Sym(8)
  (Corollary 5.2 and Propositions B.1–B.3), as `IsPresentationOf`;
- `normal-form`, `unique`, `onto`: every four-wire circuit rewrites to the
  block normal form `NF (coords u)`, whose coordinates (E ∈ Alt(8),
  B ∈ Sym(8), S an eight-subset of the sixteen states) are determined by its
  function, and every even permutation of the states is a normal form's: the
  semantic bijection of Proposition 3.3;
- `absorption`: a gate (any circuit) followed by a normal form rewrites to the
  normal form of the updated coordinates, `NormalForm.Absorb` giving them as
  the paper's Theorem 5.1 does: S′ = w⁻¹S, B′ = B A₀, E′ = E B A₁ A₀⁻¹ B⁻¹ for
  the blocks A₀, A₁ of r_S w r_S′⁻¹.

Nothing is postulated, and every module is `--safe`.

## Conventions

Wire 0 is the bottom wire; the paper numbers its wires from the top, so on
four wires its wires 1, 2, 3, 4 are our 3, 2, 1, 0. CX has its control on
wire 1 and target on wire 0, CCX its controls on wires 2, 1 and target on
wire 0; other placements are conjugates by swaps (`Placed`'s placed letters
`Xᵖ t`, `CXᵖ c t`, `CCXᵖ a b t` with a < b). Words are in diagram order:
`w • v` is w then v, so each rule reads as its figure; the paper's algebra is
in composition order. A state is a vector of bits, wire 0 first; the eight
states of three wires are numbered along the reflected Gray code 000, 001,
011, 010, 110, 111, 101, 100 (`Width3.enc`), and the sixteen of four wires by
`Four.enc` (the lower half in that order, then the upper half reflected).

## The proof

The paper's own proof checks three finite certificates of about 380 000
deductions. The formalization proves the same theorem by presentations of
permutation groups instead, so that what is checked by computation is small:

1. **Three wires and below** (`Width1`–`Width3`, `Involutions`, `Kit`):
   the edge Toffolis along the Gray code satisfy the Coxeter relations of
   S₈, so the circuits present Sym(8) and T is complete on three wires
   (`ByCoxeter`, over `Symmetric/CoxeterFrames`).
2. **Placed letters** (`Placed`, `PlacedEngine`, `ToPlaced`, `Narrow3`): a
   gate on any wires is a conjugate by swap networks, and a step on any three
   wires is justified by completeness on three wires. `Equations4` has the
   four-wire equations found by search (`gen/pg_search.py`), among them the
   paper's equation (8).
3. **The top layer** (`EvenRS`, `ByEvenRS`, `Four`, `Faithful`): EvenRS N
   presents Alt(N) by Reidemeister–Schreier from S_N's Coxeter presentation;
   at N = 16 each symbol is a short circuit targeting only two wires, so each
   relation keeps some wire, and completeness reduces to `Faithful ₃`: two
   words whose letters never target wire 3 and that have the same function
   are equal.
4. **The circuits keeping wire 3** (`KFaithful`) are K = C ⋊ P, P the
   circuits of the lower wires and C the controlled even permutations. C is
   generated by the controlled perfect matchings `Xm μ`, frames of G = CX₃₀
   (`CFrame`'s frame lemma `W′`, `CLabels`), which satisfy involution,
   conjugation and Klein relations under decided side conditions (`CRel`,
   with five base commutations: `CBC`, `BaseComm`). Words of labels are
   rewritten by `LabelEngine` certificates, which decide EvenRS 8's
   relations for C (`CCerts`, `CFaithful`); every letter of K is a word of
   lower letters and G (equation (8) relabelled), and a lower letter pushes
   across a label by the frame lemma, so a K-word is labels followed by a
   lower word, whence `Faithful ₃`.
5. **The groups** (`ByPerm`, `Groups`) and **the normal form**
   (`NormalForm`, with `NFLib`, `NFTables`, `EvenWord`).

The normal form's circuits are the formalization's own deterministic
choices: D(B) the circuit of Symmetric's Coxeter word for B on the lower
wires, C(E) the controlled matchings of EvenRS 8's symbols for E, and r_S the
circuit of EvenRS 16's symbols for the frame of S's states followed by the
others, made even by one transposition. The paper's statements hold for any
such fixed choice; its crossing circuits θ_k and the Gray-order matching that
fixes κ_S are not reproduced.

## Typechecking

Check `MainTheorem.agda`, which reaches all 40 modules of the folder (about
13 000 lines without comments): 93 s from scratch given the interfaces of
the libraries it uses (`Symmetric`, `Alternating`, `Independence/Prop`), with
a peak residency of 0.9 GB, so it fits the shared 3 GB heap.

Two traps cost most of the debugging, both about Agda evaluating Symmetric's
word for a permutation (`Surjectivity.go`): comparing two spellings of a
sign at a numeral width (`sign {16}` against `sign {8 + 8}`, two module
applications of `EvenRS`, an implicit left to unification), and comparing a
goal about a circuit with a lemma about its unfolding. So signs and
Symmetric's words are reasoned about at variable widths (`EvenWord`,
`ByPerm`), and the normal form's building blocks and the realising circuits
are `opaque`, with their definitions as equations.
