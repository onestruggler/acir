A set of equations between circuits is \emph{complete} if it derives every
true equation.  Completeness proofs are among the most computation-heavy in
the theory of quantum computing: they rely on normal forms and on long
case analyses showing that every rule application interacts correctly with
every normal form.  Recent completeness proofs come with appendices hundreds
of pages long, produced and checked by hand.  This paper presents an Agda
library that mechanizes the mathematics these proofs rest on: monoid and group
presentations by generators and relations, normal forms, and completeness of
finite relation sets, specialized to wire-indexed circuits.  The library
represents presented monoids as inductively generated congruences on free
monoids of words, represents normal forms as the standard-library function
bundles (injections, right inverses, bijections) out of the word setoid, and
derives completeness from soundness and a \emph{unique normal form} witness by
a single generic lemma.  Normal forms are built by a constructive,
monoid-level version of the Reidemeister--Schreier method: a tower of
verified coset tables adds one digit to the normal form per level, as in a
mixed-radix number system.  The library also provides presentation-level
constructions (direct, semidirect, and amalgamated free products, and group
extensions) with their presentation theorems, and the corresponding semantic
constructions, which are intended for the Agda standard library.  We validate
the design on a catalogue of verified presentations:
symmetric groups as circuits (sound and complete for both an endofunction and
a permutation semantics), cyclic groups, wreath products $\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$
(at $N = 2$, the signed permutations), Pauli groups modulo phases in all prime
dimensions, and, as amalgamated free products with verified alternating normal
forms, the presentations of the single-qubit and single-qutrit Clifford+$T$
monoids and of $U_3(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$.  All headline results are
collected in a single index module; the verified development is safe, axiom-free
Agda.  We compare in detail
with formalizations of group presentations, normal forms, and quantum circuits
in Agda, Lean, Rocq, and Isabelle.
