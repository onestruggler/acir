\section{A Worked Example: Permutation Circuits}\label{sec:permutations}

We begin with the smallest complete case study in the library: circuits
built from a single two-wire gate, the swap $\sigma$, which present the
symmetric groups.  The point of the walkthrough is to exhibit the pipeline
--- gates, relations, cosets, tower, semantics, uniqueness, completeness,
presentation --- that every later case study instantiates, concretely
enough that the general machinery of \S\ref{sec:design} can be read as
``the same thing, with the gates abstracted''.  The statement quoted at
the end appears verbatim in the index module \texttt{MainTheorems.agda}.

\subsection{Gates, generators, and circuits}

A gate set is a family $\aid{Gate} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathsf{Set}$ giving,
for each arity, the gates that act on that many adjacent wires.  For
permutations there is exactly one gate, on two wires:

\begin{code}
data Gate : ℕ → Set where
  σ-gate : Gate 2
\end{code}

The generic circuit layer (\S\ref{sec:circuits}) turns any such family into
wire-indexed \emph{generators}: a gate placed at the bottom of the wires, or
a generator shifted up by one wire.

\begin{code}
data Gen : ℕ → Set where
  gate₁ : Gate 1 → Gen (₁₊ n)     -- a 1-ary gate on the bottom wire
  gate₂ : Gate 2 → Gen (₂₊ n)     -- a 2-ary gate on the bottom two wires
  _↥   : Gen n → Gen (₁₊ n)       -- the same generator, one wire higher
\end{code}
\begin{code}[hide]
Circuit : ℕ → Set
Circuit n = Word (Gen n)

CRel : ℕ → Set₁
CRel n = Rel (Circuit n) 0ℓ

_↑ : Circuit n → Circuit (₁₊ n)
_↑ = wmap _↥

pattern σ-gen = gate₂ σ-gate

σ : Circuit (₂₊ n)
σ = [ σ-gen ]ʷ

infix 4 _SRel,_===_ _VRel,_===_ _≈_
infixr 10 σ•_

private variable
  w v : Circuit n

postulate
  _≈_ : Circuit n → Circuit n → Set
\end{code}

Here $\aid{₁₊}\;n$ and $\aid{₂₊}\;n$ are the library's patterns for
$1{+}n$ and $2{+}n$, and $n$ is an implicit argument, so
$\aid{gate₂}\;\aid{σ-gate}$ is a generator at any width from two upward.
An $n$-wire \emph{circuit} is a word of generators, $\aid{Circuit}\;n =
\aid{Word}\;(\aid{Gen}\;n)$.  Words (\S\ref{sec:words}) form the free
monoid: a circuit is a single generator $[\,g\,]$\aid{ʷ}, the empty circuit
\aid{ε}, or a sequential composition $c \aid{•} c'$.  The empty circuit on
$n$ wires is the identity circuit, $n$ bare wires and no gate; like every
circuit it carries its wire count as a type index.  We write $\sigma :
\aid{Circuit}\;(2{+}n)$ for the one-letter circuit
$[\,\aid{gate₂}\;\aid{σ-gate}\,]$\aid{ʷ} and $c{\uparrow}$ for the circuit
$c$ shifted up one wire ($\aid{{\_}↑} = \aid{wmap}\;\aid{{\_}↥}$ shifts
every generator).

The reading is diagrammatic.  On three wires, the two generators and a
composite correspond to circuit diagrams as follows (wires drawn bottom-up,
composition left to right):

\subsection{Relations: two axioms, structure for free}

The group-specific relations are an inductive family indexed by the wire
count.  Each axiom is stated once, with its gates at the bottom of the
wires and the wire count left open (the implicit $n$), so that a single
constructor covers every width at which the axiom fits; the structural
rule \aid{cong↑} below then moves it to any height:

\begin{code}
data _SRel,_===_ : (n : ℕ) → WRel (Gen n) where
  order       : (₂₊ n) SRel, σ • σ === ε
  yang-baxter : (₃₊ n) SRel, σ • σ ↑ • σ === σ ↑ • σ • σ ↑
\end{code}

(The name \aid{{\_}SRel,{\_}==={\_}} is a \emph{mixfix} identifier: in
Agda, underscores in a name mark argument positions, so the type
$n\;\aid{SRel,}\;w \aid{===} v$ reads ``$w = v$ is an axiom at $n$
wires''.)  These two axioms are the Coxeter presentation of $S_n$ in
circuit clothing: $\sigma^2 = \varepsilon$ and the braid (Yang--Baxter)
relation.

What about the relations every circuit theory needs, whatever its gates?
By a \emph{circuit theory} we mean a gate set together with gate-specific
relations, presenting for each width $n$ a monoid of $n$-wire circuits
under sequential composition.  Such circuits are the fixed-width slices of
a symmetric monoidal category of circuits (a PROP), and the monoidal
structure leaves exactly two traces at fixed width.  The shift $c \mapsto
c{\uparrow}$ is ``tensoring with an identity wire'', so equations must be
preserved by it; and the interchange law says that gates acting on
disjoint wires commute.  These structural rules are what every circuit
theory shares --- they are what a pen-and-paper presentation leaves
implicit in the phrase ``a congruence compatible with composition and
tensor product'' --- and writing them into every gate set would be
repetitive and error-prone.  The circuit layer therefore adds them
generically: \aid{Lift-Relation} (\S\ref{sec:circuits}) extends any
gate-specific family with three structural rules, yielding the full
relation set \aid{{\_}VRel,{\_}==={\_}}:

\begin{code}
data _VRel,_===_ : (n : ℕ) → CRel n where
  srel  : n SRel, w === v → n VRel, w === v
  cong↑ : n VRel, w === v → (₁₊ n) VRel, w ↑ === v ↑
  comm₁ : (h : Gate 1) (g : Gen n) → (₁₊ n) VRel,
    [ g ↥ ]ʷ • [ gate₁ h ]ʷ === [ gate₁ h ]ʷ • [ g ↥ ]ʷ
  comm₂ : (h : Gate 2) (g : Gen n) → (₂₊ n) VRel,
    [ g ↥ ↥ ]ʷ • [ gate₂ h ]ʷ === [ gate₂ h ]ʷ • [ g ↥ ↥ ]ʷ
\end{code}

The far-commutation instance of \aid{comm₂} and the braid relation are the
two diagram equalities of Figure~\ref{fig:relations}.  From here on,
$\approx$ denotes the monoid congruence generated by
$n\;\aid{VRel},{=}{=}{=}$ (\S\ref{sec:presentations}): the least relation
containing the axioms and closed under reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity,
congruence of \aid{•}, associativity, and the unit laws.  Two circuits are
equal in the presented monoid exactly when $\approx$ relates them, and
deciding $\approx$ is the word problem.

Because $\sigma$ is its own inverse and shifts of invertible generators are
invertible, the presented monoid is a group; we witness this by a
\aid{Grouplike} proof (every generator has a left inverse), constructed by
induction on generators.

\subsection{Cosets and the staircase normal form}\label{sec:sn-cosets}

The engine behind the normal form is coset enumeration along the chain of
subgroups
\[
  S_1 \;<\; S_2 \;<\; \dots \;<\; S_n \;<\; S_{n+1},
\]
where $S_n$ sits inside $S_{n+1}$ as the permutations fixing the bottom
wire --- syntactically, the circuits of the form $c{\uparrow}$.  Read as
permutations, two circuits lie in the same right coset of $S_n$ exactly
when they bring the same wire to the bottom position.  There are $n{+}1$
choices, so $S_n$ has index $n{+}1$ in $S_{n+1}$, and a natural
representative of each coset is the \emph{descending staircase} of swaps
that carries that wire down.  On three, two, and one wires the
representatives are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc@{\qquad\qquad}cc@{\qquad\qquad}c}
  \ufig[0.6]{c2-0} & \ufig[0.6]{c2-1} & \ufig[0.6]{c2-2} &
  \ufig[0.6]{c1-0} & \ufig[0.6]{c1-1} & \ufig[0.6]{c0-0}\\[2pt]
  $\varepsilon$ & $\sigma{\bullet}\varepsilon$ &
  $\sigma{\bullet}\sigma{\bullet}\varepsilon$ &
  $\varepsilon$ & $\sigma{\bullet}\varepsilon$ & $\varepsilon$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In Agda a coset is a \emph{descriptor} --- the length of the staircase,
as a unary numeral bounded by the wire count --- and the \emph{section}
$[\,\_\,]^{\mathsf c}$ realizes a descriptor as the staircase circuit:

\begin{code}
data C : ℕ → Set where
  ε   : C n
  σ•_ : C n → C (₁₊ n)

[_]ᶜ : C n → Circuit (₁₊ n)
[ ε ]ᶜ     = ε
[ σ• c ]ᶜ  = σ • ([ c ]ᶜ ↑)
\end{code}

So $\aid{C}\;n$ has exactly $n{+}1$ elements --- $\varepsilon$,
$\sigma{\bullet}\varepsilon$, up to a staircase of $n$ swaps --- matching
the index of $S_n$ in $S_{n+1}$; the table above lists $\aid{C}\;2$,
$\aid{C}\;1$, and $\aid{C}\;0$.

Why this yields a normal form is the classical argument recalled in
\S\ref{sec:preliminaries}.  Every $f \in S_{n+1}$ lies in exactly one
coset, say the one represented by $c_n$; then $f$ factors uniquely as
$f = f' \cdot c_n$ with $f' \in S_n$, and recursing on $f'$ writes $f$ as
a product of one representative per level, $c_1 c_2 \cdots c_n$ with $c_k
\in \aid{C}\;k$.  Counting representatives, $|S_{n+1}| = (n{+}1) \cdot n
\cdots 2$: the tuple $(c_1, \dots, c_n)$ is a numeral in the
\emph{factorial number system}, one digit per level.

\emph{Computing} this factorization is the job of the coset table: a right
action that pushes one generator past a staircase, emitting a residual
circuit on the wires above and a successor coset.  Pushing $\sigma$ into a
staircase can lengthen it, absorb it ($\sigma^2 = \varepsilon$), or pass
it through and shift it up (Yang--Baxter); a generator that does not touch
the bottom wire simply commutes past.  The residual is therefore a
\emph{circuit} rather than a single generator --- either nothing or one
swap, on the wires above --- which is why the table returns a word.  The
four cases are the four pictures of Figure~\ref{fig:pushing}:

\medskip\noindent
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.66\linewidth}
\begingroup\renewcommand{\AgdaCodeStyle}{\footnotesize}

ract : C (₁₊ n) → Gen (₂₊ n) → Circuit (₁₊ n) × C (₁₊ n)

ract (σ• ε)    σ-gen = ε , ε          -- σ² = ε absorbs

ract ε         (g ↥) = [ g ]ʷ , ε     -- disjoint: commute
ract (σ• c)    (g ↥) = proj₁ (ract c g) ↑ , σ• (proj₂ (ract c g))
\end{code}
\endgroup
\end{minipage}\hfill

(\aid{σ-gen} abbreviates $\aid{gate₂}\;\aid{σ-gate}$; two further clauses
dispose of vacuous low-arity cases.)  The last clause is the recursion: a
shifted generator meets a staircase by meeting its tail one wire down.
Each clause is certified by the \emph{soundness law}, proved once per
table by a short equational derivation from the axioms and pictured on
the right --- in Agda,

\begin{code}

The library's stateful word traversal \aid{{\_}ᵗ} (\S\ref{sec:words})
extends \aid{ract} to whole circuits, threading the coset from left to
right and concatenating the residuals.  Running it from the identity coset
maps an $(n{+}2)$-wire circuit to a pair of an $(n{+}1)$-wire circuit and a
coset --- one level of normalization, and, in the vocabulary of
\S\ref{sec:engine}, the normal-form transport that a verified coset table
provides.

\emph{Iterating} this is the coset tower (\S\ref{sec:engine}).  For each
level, the symmetric-group development (module
\texttt{Examples.Groups.Symmetric}) supplies the coset type $\aid{C}\;k$,
the table \aid{ract}, the section $[\,\_\,]^{\mathsf c}$, and the five
hypotheses that make a coset table verified --- \aid{ract-sound} is one of
them; the others are finite case splits closed by evaluation --- packaged
as a single record.  The tower folds these records upward, starting from
the trivial normal form of the one-wire theory, in which every circuit is
empty.  The carrier grows by one digit per level,
\[
  \aid{NF}\;n \;=\; \top \times \aid{C}\;1 \times \aid{C}\;2 \times
  \dots \times \aid{C}\;(n{-}1),
\]
and has exactly $2 \cdot 3 \cdots n = n!$ elements, as it must.  The
normal-form function $\aid{nf} : \aid{Circuit}\;n \to \aid{NF}\;n$ runs
the tables; its \emph{section} $\aid{inv-nf} : \aid{NF}\;n \to
\aid{Circuit}\;n$ rebuilds a circuit from its digits by concatenating the
staircases $[\,c_k\,]^{\mathsf c}$, suitably shifted; and the witness that
this is a normal form (\S\ref{sec:nfproperty}) is the proof that
$\aid{inv-nf}\;(\aid{nf}\;c) \approx c$ for every circuit $c$.
Figure~\ref{fig:staircase} shows the section of one normal form on four
wires: a ``staircase of staircases'', exactly Selinger's normal-form
picture for Clifford circuits restricted to
swaps~\cite{selinger2015clifford}.

\subsection{Two semantics, uniqueness, and completeness}

So far everything was syntax.  The development interprets circuits twice:

\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{loose} semantics
  $\aid{⟦\_⟧} : \aid{Circuit}\;n \to (\mathsf{Fin}\;n \to \mathsf{Fin}\;n)$
  into endofunctions of $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$ under composition ($\sigma$ swaps
  the bottom two elements), a monoid that is \emph{not} a group; and
\item a \emph{tight} semantics into
  $\aid{Permutation′-group}\;n$, the group of permutations of
  $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$ --- the standard library's \aid{Permutation′} bundled
  as a \aid{Group} by our \texttt{ForStdlib} layer
  (\S\ref{sec:forstdlib}).
\end{itemize}

\emph{Soundness} --- $w \approx v$ implies
$\aid{⟦}w\aid{⟧} \doteq \aid{⟦}v\aid{⟧}$ --- is, as always, a small case
analysis over the axioms: the two Coxeter relations hold for the swap
function, and the structural rules hold because shifted circuits act only on
shifted points.  The heart of the matter is the \emph{unique normal form}
property (\S\ref{sec:nfproperty}): normal forms with equal denotations are
equal,
\[
  \aid{⟦}\,\aid{inv-nf}\;u\,\aid{⟧} \doteq \aid{⟦}\,\aid{inv-nf}\;v\,\aid{⟧}
  \;\longrightarrow\; u \equiv v \qquad (u, v \in \aid{NF}\;n),
\]
proved by induction on the tower: two staircase sections that agree as
functions must peel off equal top digits (they send the top point to the
same place), and the rest agree by induction.  Soundness and uniqueness are
exactly the premises of the library's generic lemma
\aid{by-normalization}, which concludes \emph{completeness}:
$\aid{⟦}w\aid{⟧} \doteq \aid{⟦}v\aid{⟧}$ implies $w \approx v$.  The index
module states three theorems of this kind, for every $n$: soundness and
completeness of the loose semantics, and uniqueness of the tower normal
form for the tight semantics.  In \texttt{MainTheorems.agda} they are
named \aid{symmetric-soundness}, \aid{symmetric-completeness}, and
\aid{symmetric-unique-nf}, and their Agda types are the statements above
with the setoids spelled out.

Completeness for the \emph{loose} semantics is the stronger, perhaps
surprising fact: even read as mere endofunctions, circuits that are equal
as functions are provably equal --- the relation set does not need
invertibility of the model.  For the tight semantics, soundness,
completeness, and surjectivity (every permutation is a product of adjacent
transpositions --- realized by \aid{inv-nf} composed with a semantic normal
form) assemble into the \emph{presentation record} of
\S\ref{sec:presentations}, which packages a \aid{Grouplike} witness with a
group isomorphism from the presented group onto the target whose
underlying function is the semantics.  This is the one statement we quote
in Agda, since every presentation theorem in the paper has its shape:

\begin{code}
symmetric-presentation :
  ∀ n → (n VRel,_===_) IsPresentationOf (SymTight.Permutation′-group n)
\end{code}
\begin{code}[hide]
symmetric-presentation n = SymThm.Tight.presentation n
\end{code}

read: the monoid of swap circuits modulo \{\aid{order},
\aid{yang-baxter}\} and the structural rules \emph{is} the group of
permutations of $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$, via a group isomorphism whose
underlying map is the semantics.  (\aid{IsPresentationOf} is the mixfix
record name \aid{{\_}IsPresentationOf{\_}} used infix, between the
relation set and the group.)  Everything later in the paper is this
pipeline again, with richer gates, longer towers, and amalgamations where
the chain branches.
