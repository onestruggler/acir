\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an Agda library for presentations of monoids and groups,
normal forms, and completeness of finite relation sets, organized around
inductively defined circuits and a verified, monoid-level
Reidemeister--Schreier engine.  Completeness is reduced, by one generic
lemma, to soundness and a unique-normal-form witness.  Normal forms are built
by verified coset tables, one mixed-radix digit per level of a subgroup
tower, or by amalgamation where the tower branches.  The catalogue of
verified instances spans the symmetric groups as circuits (complete for
two semantics), cyclic groups, signed permutations and general wreath
products $\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$, Pauli groups modulo phases in all prime
dimensions, and the presentations of the single-qubit and single-qutrit
Clifford+$T$ monoids and of $U_3(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$ as amalgamated
free products.  In the Clifford+$T$ case, the amalgam's alternating normal
form reproduces the syllable structure of the Matsumoto--Amano normal
form.  Every headline theorem is collected, fully
typed, in one index module, and the whole verified development is safe,
axiom-free Agda.

The work continues in three directions.  \emph{Clifford
completeness.}  The multi-qupit Clifford groups sit above the Pauli
groups (modulo phases) formalized here as extensions by the symplectic
groups $Sp(2n,\mathbb{Z}_p)$; with the Pauli presentations and the
extension-presentation theorem verified, the next target is
machine-checked completeness for multi-qubit and multi-qupit
Clifford circuits: formal counterparts of Selinger's qubit
presentation~\cite{selinger2015clifford}, the qutrit rule set
of~\cite{li2025qutrit}, and its generalization to all odd primes (work in
progress).  Their pen-and-paper proofs are long, regular case analyses of
the kind this framework handles.  \emph{More groups.}  The
constructions should extend to further families: the lamplighter groups
$\mathbb{Z}_N \wr \mathbb{Z}$ as a first infinite-index stress test of
the wreath machinery, HNN-style extensions to complement amalgamations,
and the remaining gate groups over rings such as $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i]$
and $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]$~\cite{bian2021un,li2021on}, including
the orthogonal groups over $\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2]$ whose finite
presentation underpins the completeness of the quantum programming
language Hadamard-$\Pi$~\cite{fang2026hadamard}.
\emph{Upstreaming.}  The \texttt{ForStdlib} constructions (semidirect
products, monoid amalgamations, extensions, epimorphisms, the permutation
group) are being prepared for the Agda standard library, and the
presentation framework itself is written to its conventions with that
destination in mind.

More broadly, completeness proofs for circuit
calculi are getting longer (one recent appendix runs to 264
pages~\cite{clement2026realcliffordch}), but their structure
is unchanged: normal forms, towers, and case analyses.
A proof assistant with a strong evaluator handles this structure well.
Our experience with this library suggests that, with
presentations as data, normal forms as function bundles, and coset tables
as certificates, such proofs can be machine-checked and reused at
reasonable cost.
