\subsection{The Bian--Selinger verifications: syntax to syntax}\label{sec:rel-bs}

The direct ancestors of this library are the Agda developments that Bian
and Selinger published alongside their presentations of the $2$-qubit
Clifford+$T$ group~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2} and the $3$-qubit
Clifford+CS group~\cite{bian2023cliffordcs3}, systematized in Bian's
thesis~\cite{bian2023thesis}.  These developments use several methods
that we reuse: monoid-level presentations (no formal inverses),
the Reidemeister--Schreier theorem applied --- to Greylyn's presentation
of $U_4(\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2,i])$~\cite{greylyn2014} in the $2$-qubit
case --- to derive subgroup presentations,
amalgamated products of monoids, and machine-checked simplification of
``thousands of relations'' down to a small set (seventeen, for
Clifford+CS).  The checked results concern \emph{presentations}.  In the
two-qubit case, the translation into Greylyn's ambient presentation
preserves and reflects the presentation congruences; its image is the
index-two Clifford+$T$ subgroup, not the entire ambient group.
The developments also establish presentation isomorphisms.  Their
connection to concrete matrix groups uses mathematical results outside
the formal developments.
Our amalgamation case studies likewise end in presentation isomorphisms.
The \aid{{\_}IsPresentationOf{\_}} records of
Table~\ref{tab:presentations} additionally connect syntax to stdlib
permutations and groups assembled from semantic factors using
\texttt{ForStdlib}.  Some actions and embedding maps are transported
through the factor presentation isomorphisms
(\S\ref{sec:constructions}); the generic \aid{by-normalization} lemma
supplies completeness.  A second delta is reusability: coset tables,
towers, and the product constructions are library modules
with explicit hypothesis records, where the earlier developments were
structured as per-paper proofs.

\subsection{Quantum programs and rewrite soundness in proof assistants}\label{sec:rel-qc}

Several proof-assistant developments verify quantum programs and compilers.
\textsc{Qwire}~\cite{paykin2017qwire,rand2018qwirepractice} embeds a circuit language in Rocq
with a denotational semantics; \textsc{sqir}/\textsc{voqc}
\cite{hietala2021voqc,hietala2021proving} defines a quantum IR in Rocq and
verifies an optimizing compiler: every rewrite the optimizer performs
is proved \emph{sound} against the matrix semantics.
VyZX~\cite{lehmann2025vyzx} inductively represents ZX diagrams in Rocq and
proves the ZX rewrite rules sound.  CoqQ~\cite{zhou2023coqq} builds
Dirac-notation program logics over MathComp;
Qbricks~\cite{chareton2021qbricks} verifies circuit-building programs
with automated first-order reasoning over path-sums; on the Isabelle
side, QHLProver formalizes quantum Hoare logic, including soundness
and completeness for partial correctness~\cite{liu2019quantum}, and
Isabelle-Marries-Dirac verifies textbook algorithms~\cite{bordg2020dirac}.
These results concern program logics, algorithm correctness, or rewrite
soundness.  They do not establish completeness of a finite circuit
equation set, the question studied here.  A further step would be to connect our presented
gate groups to the matrix semantics of \textsc{sqir} or VyZX, and
formulate and prove completeness directly against matrices.

\subsection{Completeness theorems on paper: gate sets and ZX}\label{sec:rel-paper}

The mathematical literature proves such theorems on
paper~\cite{selinger2015clifford,clement2023complete,clement2024minimal,
li2025qutrit,clement2026realcliffordch}.  Further examples include the
CNOT-dihedral fragment~\cite{amy2018cnotdihedral} and its
prime-dimensional generalization~\cite{blake2026phaseaffine}.  Likewise for the graphical
side, where the completeness results for the ZX-calculus, from the qubit
stabilizer fragment through Clifford+$T$ to the odd-prime-dimensional
qupit calculi~\cite{backens2014zx,jeandel2018zx,booth2022qupitzx,
poor2023travaganza}, are presented as mathematical proofs.  Among the
works surveyed here, mechanized completeness results occur in the
Bian--Selinger developments, the Agda developments of the
rig-categorical school (next subsection), and the present work.  The rule sets themselves are
also a moving target: Blake's recent PROP-theoretic treatment uniformly
transfers and simplifies the presentations of six near-Clifford circuit
fragments (among them the two-qubit Clifford+$T$, three-qubit
Clifford+CS, and qutrit Clifford presentations discussed above),
obtaining minimal rule sets for several of
them~\cite{blake2026simpler}.  Each such simplification amounts to the
claim that the dropped rules are derivable from those kept, exactly the
kind of claim this library is designed to verify.

\subsection{Complete equational theories via rig categories}\label{sec:rel-rig}

A parallel school, rooted in programming-language semantics rather than
gate groups, reaches completeness through \emph{categorical} structure.
Choudhury, Karwowski, and Sabry present the reversible language $\Pi$ as
the free symmetric rig groupoid and mechanize the syntax and most proofs
in univalent HoTT-Agda (about 7\,500 lines), using the formalization for
normalization-by-evaluation, verification, and synthesis of reversible
Boolean circuits~\cite{choudhury2022symmetries}.  Their completeness result
establishes full abstraction of $\Pi$'s syntactic groupoid against a
univalent subuniverse of finite types, using higher inductive types and
univalence.  Our library proves completeness of concrete finite relation
sets against permutations and groups assembled from semantic factors,
using coset normal forms in setoid-based Agda.
Their development also encounters the word problem for $S_n$, which our
factorial-carrier normal form decides (\S\ref{sec:permutations}).

On the quantum side of that school, Carette, Heunen, Kaarsgaard, and
Sabry extend rig groupoids with square-root maps and prove the resulting
equational theory sound and complete for arbitrary Clifford circuits,
Clifford+$T$ circuits on at most two qubits, and the computationally
universal Gaussian Clifford+$T$ gate set (their Section~6, which builds
on the presentations of Selinger and of Bian and
Selinger)~\cite{carette2024squareroots}.  This development, too, is
partially mechanized: an Agda library extending agda-categories
formalizes the equational model, all of their \S6.1 (whose completeness
theorem for the two-qubit Clifford fragment imports a result of
Selinger's), and lemmas of two of the three
larger completeness proofs.  The authors report that no errors surfaced
in the paper proofs, that a missing rig-category coherence was found in
agda-categories itself, and that the weak setting of
agda-categories forces explicit unit and associativity bookkeeping,
tedious without the library's combinators.  Our \aid{by-assoc} solver
handles analogous associativity and unit-law obligations
(\S\ref{sec:solvers}).  Two recent papers in this school give
mathematical completeness proofs: Fang, Heunen, and Kaarsgaard's
Hadamard-$\Pi$ adjoins the Hadamard gate to $\Pi$ and derives
completeness from a new finite presentation, with a synthesis algorithm,
of the orthogonal groups over
$\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2]$~\cite{fang2026hadamard} (matrix groups of
the family whose presentations Li, Ross, and Selinger
studied~\cite{li2021on}); and Heunen, Kaarsgaard, and Lemonnier derive
sound and complete axiomatizations of controlled circuits uniformly, for
quantum and Toffoli-style gate sets at once, from coherence for
semisimple rig categories~\cite{heunen2026onerig}.  A possible application
is to verify the presentation of $O_n(\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2])$ used in the
completeness proof for Hadamard-$\Pi$.  This would provide one ingredient
for mechanizing that proof.

\subsection{Group theory in proof assistants: abstract theory versus concrete presentations}\label{sec:rel-gt}

Lean's mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020} defines \aid{FreeGroup} and
\aid{PresentedGroup} --- the quotient of a free group by the normal
closure of a relator set --- and develops Coxeter groups atop
them~\cite{mathlib-doc-presented}, together with a groupoid-based proof
of the Nielsen--Schreier theorem.  It also provides reduced-word normal
forms for free groups and transversal-based normal forms for amalgamated
products of groups.  These are general constructions; our focus is
verified coset tables and normal-form transports for concrete circuit
presentations.  MathComp represents finite groups as finite types, as in
its formalization of the Odd Order theorem~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder}.
It also provides a presentation notation:
$G \mathrel{\backslash\mathtt{isog}}\ \mathtt{Grp}(x_1 \ldots x_n : s_1 =
t_1, \ldots)$ states that $G$ is isomorphic to the largest finite
quotient of the presented group, and MathComp uses it to characterize
the dihedral, semidihedral, and quaternion groups; but the proofs go
through the finite group theory of the target (orders and counting), and
no coset enumeration or normal form is involved.  In Isabelle, the AFP has free
groups with the ping-pong lemma~\cite{breitner2010freegroups} and a
recent combinatorial Nielsen--Schreier proof with a decision procedure
for the conjugacy problem in free groups~\cite{kharim2023freegroups}.
These developments use normal forms to reason about free groups; they do
not provide presentations of concrete target groups.  Our library
verifies coset tables for concrete
presentations of the kind used in computational group
theory~\cite{sims1994computation,holt2005handbook}.  Its certificates
consist of tables, representative maps, and proofs of five equational conditions,
independently of the search procedure used to find the tables.

On the univalent side, cubical Agda~\cite{vezzosi2019cubical} and its
library~\cite{cubicallibrary} provide free groups and set quotients as
higher inductive types, including unique normal forms and decidable
equality for free groups on discrete generators;
agda-unimath develops finite group theory including the symmetric groups
and the sign homomorphism~\cite{agdaunimath,mangel2023sign}.  Normal-form
reconstruction maps and Schreier transversals return concrete words, so we retain
words as the carrier and use a setoid to express their equality.  This
representation also keeps the library compatible with the non-cubical
standard library; \S\ref{sec:setoids} discusses the choice further.

\subsection{Formalized rewriting and completion}\label{sec:rel-rw}

The rewriting community has formalized its own meta-theory: IsaFoR/CeTA
contains Knuth--Bendix orders and completion~\cite{sternagel2013kb} and
abstract completion~\cite{hirokawa2019abstract}, certifying completion
\emph{procedures} that, when they succeed, decide word problems via
convergent rewriting.  Our method uses a verified normal-form function
and proves uniqueness against the semantics; it does not require
orienting the relations or proving confluence.  This sidesteps the
classical obstruction that finitely
presented monoids with decidable word problem need not admit finite
convergent presentations (Squier~\cite{squier1987word}; Squier, Otto,
and Kobayashi~\cite{squier1994finiteness}): a normal-form function is
a weaker artifact
than a convergent rewrite system, and correspondingly more often
available.  The polygraphic school of Guiraud--Malbos and
collaborators~\cite{guiraud2018polygraphs,ara2025polygraphs} studies
presentations and their coherence in higher categories, with Lafont's
algebraic theory of Boolean circuits~\cite{lafont2003boolean} as an earlier
study of circuit presentations.  These cited treatments give mathematical
proofs.

\subsection{Related tools}\label{sec:tools}

Two unverified web tools compute normal forms of the kind this
library is built to prove correct and complete.  A
normalizer to \emph{Lafont normal form} for linear permutation circuits
implements the normalization lemmas of Lafont's algebraic theory of Boolean
circuits~\cite{lafont2003boolean,lafontlp-tool}; and a normalizer for the
symplectic groups $Sp(2n,\mathbb{Z}_p)$ computes Selinger-style staircase
normal forms~\cite{qupit-tool}.
