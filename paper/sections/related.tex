\subsection{The Bian--Selinger verifications: syntax to syntax}\label{sec:rel-bs}

The direct ancestors of this library are the Agda developments that Bian
and Selinger published alongside their presentations of the $2$-qubit
Clifford+$T$ group~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2} and the $3$-qubit
Clifford+CS group~\cite{bian2023cliffordcs3}, systematized in Bian's
thesis~\cite{bian2023thesis}.  They pioneered everything we build on:
monoid-level presentations without formal inverses, the
Reidemeister--Schreier theorem applied to derive subgroup presentations
(from Greylyn's presentation of
$U_4(\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2,i])$~\cite{greylyn2014} in the $2$-qubit case),
amalgamated products of monoids, and machine-checked simplification of
``thousands of relations'' down to seventeen.  Their limitation is that
they work entirely on the \emph{syntactic side}: every theorem relates
two presentations of a group, and no independent semantic object occurs
in any statement, so soundness and completeness in the model-theoretic
sense are not expressible there.  The present library keeps the
syntactic technology --- our amalgamation case studies end in exactly
such isomorphisms --- and adds the semantic half: the theorems of
Table~\ref{tab:presentations} target groups built independently of the
syntax, and \aid{by-normalization} connects the two halves.  A second
delta is reusability: coset tables, towers, and the product constructions
are library modules with explicit hypothesis records, where the earlier
developments were per-paper proofs.

\subsection{Quantum circuits in proof assistants: soundness, not completeness}\label{sec:rel-qc}

A rich ecosystem verifies quantum \emph{programs} and \emph{compilers}.
\textsc{Qwire}~\cite{paykin2017qwire} embeds a circuit language in Rocq
with a denotational semantics; \textsc{sqir}/\textsc{voqc}
\cite{hietala2021voqc,hietala2021proving} defines a quantum IR in Rocq and
verifies an optimizing compiler --- every rewrite the optimizer performs
is proved \emph{sound} against the matrix semantics.
VyZX~\cite{lehmann2025vyzx} inductively represents ZX diagrams in Rocq and
proves the ZX rewrite rules sound.  CoqQ~\cite{zhou2023coqq} builds
Dirac-notation program logics over MathComp;
Qbricks~\cite{chareton2021qbricks} verifies circuit-building programs
with automated first-order reasoning over path-sums; on the Isabelle
side, QHLProver formalizes quantum Hoare logic~\cite{liu2019quantum} and
Isabelle-Marries-Dirac verifies textbook algorithms~\cite{bordg2020dirac}.
None of these address the question this paper is about: whether a
\emph{finite rule set derives all true equations} --- the completeness of
the calculus itself, as opposed to the correctness of particular
transformations in it.  The distinction has practical consequences:
completeness is what justifies normal-form-based decision procedures and
tells an optimizer author that no valid rewrite is missing.  We see the two
ecosystems as complementary: a natural target is to connect our presented
gate groups to the matrix semantics of \textsc{sqir} or VyZX, obtaining
end-to-end completeness against matrices rather than against abstract
product groups.

\subsection{Completeness theorems on paper: gate sets and ZX}\label{sec:rel-paper}

Completeness of a finite rule set for a circuit fragment --- the property
the verification ecosystem leaves open --- is what the mathematical
literature proves on paper, for gate sets --- from the CNOT-dihedral
fragment~\cite{amy2018cnotdihedral} and its prime-dimensional
generalization~\cite{blake2026phaseaffine} through the qubit and qutrit
Clifford groups~\cite{selinger2015clifford,li2025qutrit} to full
quantum circuits~\cite{clement2023complete,clement2024minimal,
clement2026realcliffordch} --- and likewise on the graphical side, where the completeness results for the ZX-calculus, from the qubit
stabilizer fragment through Clifford+$T$ to the odd-prime-dimensional
qupit calculi~\cite{backens2014zx,jeandel2018zx,booth2022qupitzx,
poor2023travaganza}, are all pen-and-paper; mechanizations have so far
been confined to the Bian--Selinger line, the Agda developments of the
rig-categorical school (\S\ref{sec:rel-rig}), and the present work.  The
rule sets themselves are also a moving target: Blake's recent
PROP-theoretic treatment uniformly transfers and simplifies the
presentations of six near-Clifford circuit fragments --- among them the
two-qubit Clifford+$T$, three-qubit Clifford+CS, and qutrit Clifford
presentations of \S\ref{sec:rel-bs} --- obtaining minimal rule sets for
several of them~\cite{blake2026simpler}.  Each such simplification
amounts to the claim that the dropped rules are derivable from those
kept, which is precisely the kind of claim our library is designed to
verify; machine-checking these reduced rule sets is a natural next step.

\subsection{Complete equational theories via rig categories}\label{sec:rel-rig}

A parallel school, rooted in programming-language semantics rather than
in gate groups, reaches completeness through \emph{categorical}
structure.  Choudhury, Karwowski, and Sabry present the reversible
language $\Pi$ as the free symmetric rig groupoid and mechanize the
syntax and most of the proofs in univalent HoTT-Agda (about 7\,500
lines)~\cite{choudhury2022symmetries}.  This is the mechanization
nearest in spirit to ours, and it contrasts with ours on every axis:
their completeness is \emph{coherence} for a free categorical structure
over the classical (permutation) fragment, obtained with higher inductive
types and univalence; ours is completeness of \emph{concrete finite
relation sets} against independently constructed groups, obtained by
coset normal forms in axiom-free, setoid-based, standard-library Agda.
The two meet at one point: their development needs to decide the word
problem for $S_n$, which is what our factorial normal form of
\S\ref{sec:permutations} does.

On the quantum side, Carette, Heunen, Kaarsgaard, and Sabry extend $\Pi$
by an eighth root $\omega$ of the identity and a square root $\mathsf{V}$
of negation to the language $\sqrt{\Pi}$, and prove its equational
theory sound and complete for Clifford circuits, for Clifford+$T$
circuits on at most two qubits, and for the universal Gaussian
Clifford+$T$ gate set --- the unitaries over $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i]$,
the family $U_n(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$ of \S\ref{sec:u3} --- building
on Selinger's Clifford presentation and Bian and Selinger's presentation
of $U_n(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$~\cite{carette2024squareroots}.  The
development is partially mechanized over agda-categories (the model, the
two-qubit Clifford completeness proof, and opening lemmas of the larger
proofs), and the authors report that manual associativity-and-unit
bookkeeping in weak categories was a major cost --- a cost our strict
word syntax and \aid{by-assoc} solver are designed to remove
(\S\ref{sec:solvers}).  Still pen-and-paper, Fang, Heunen, and
Kaarsgaard's Hadamard-$\Pi$ derives completeness from a new finite
presentation of the orthogonal groups over
$\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2]$~\cite{fang2026hadamard}, of the family Li, Ross,
and Selinger studied~\cite{li2021on}, and Heunen, Kaarsgaard, and
Lemonnier axiomatize controlled circuits uniformly from coherence for
semisimple rig categories~\cite{heunen2026onerig}.  A verified
presentation of $O_n(\mathbb{Z}[1/\sqrt2])$ would put the completeness
of Hadamard-$\Pi$ on machine-checked footing.

\subsection{Group theory in proof assistants: abstract theory versus concrete presentations}\label{sec:rel-gt}

Lean's mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020} defines \aid{FreeGroup} and
\aid{PresentedGroup} --- the quotient of a free group by the normal
closure of a relator set --- and develops Coxeter groups atop
them~\cite{mathlib-doc-presented}, together with a groupoid-based proof
of the Nielsen--Schreier theorem.  This is presentation \emph{theory} in
the abstract: what mathlib does not provide is infrastructure for
proving that a \emph{given} finite relation set presents a \emph{given}
concrete group --- no coset enumeration, no verified Schreier
transversals, no normal-form transports; the Coxeter development, for
instance, axiomatizes the presentation rather than verifying completeness
of specific relation sets for specific permutation groups.  The Rocq/MathComp
school, whose deep results --- most famously the Odd Order
theorem~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder} --- concern finite groups
represented as finite types, does have a presentation notation: $G
\mathrel{\backslash\mathtt{isog}}\ \mathtt{Grp}(x_1 \ldots x_n : s_1 =
t_1, \ldots)$ states that $G$ is isomorphic to the largest finite
quotient of the presented group, and MathComp uses it to characterize
the dihedral and quaternion $2$-groups; but the proofs go through the
finite group theory of the target (orders and counting), and no coset
enumeration or normal form is involved.  In Isabelle, the AFP has free
groups with the ping-pong lemma~\cite{breitner2010freegroups} and a
recent combinatorial Nielsen--Schreier proof with a decision procedure
for the conjugacy problem in free groups~\cite{kharim2023freegroups} ---
normal-form reasoning in the free group, adjacent in spirit to our
\aid{NormalFormInjective}, but again without presentations of concrete
target groups.  Against this landscape the library's niche is sharp:
\emph{verified coset enumeration for concrete presentations}, of the kind
computational group theory performs unverified~\cite{sims1994computation,
holt2005handbook}, packaged so that the certificate (tables, sections,
five equations) is separated from the search that found it.

On the univalent side, cubical Agda~\cite{vezzosi2019cubical} and its
library~\cite{cubicallibrary} provide free groups as higher inductive
types and quotients with the right universal properties definitionally,
and agda-unimath develops finite group theory including the symmetric
groups and the sign homomorphism~\cite{agdaunimath,mangel2023sign}.  A
HIT-based presented group would spare us the congruence bookkeeping of
the setoid design, but at the price of the sections into the syntax on
which the design rests; \S\ref{sec:setoids} weighs that trade, and the
library's standard-library target settles it.

\subsection{Formalized rewriting and completion}\label{sec:rel-rw}

The rewriting community has formalized its own meta-theory: IsaFoR/CeTA
contains Knuth--Bendix orders and completion~\cite{sternagel2013kb} and
abstract completion~\cite{hirokawa2019abstract}, certifying completion
\emph{procedures} that, when they succeed, decide word problems via
convergent rewriting.  Our approach is deliberately different: we never
orient the relations or prove confluence --- the normal form is an
arbitrary verified \emph{function}, and uniqueness is proved against the
semantics.  This sidesteps the classical obstruction that finitely
presented monoids with decidable word problem need not admit finite
convergent presentations (Squier~\cite{squier1987word}; Squier, Otto,
and Kobayashi~\cite{squier1994finiteness}) --- a normal-form function is
a weaker artifact
than a convergent rewrite system, and correspondingly more often
available.  The polygraphic school of Guiraud--Malbos and
collaborators~\cite{guiraud2018polygraphs,ara2025polygraphs} studies
presentations and their coherence in higher categories, with Lafont's
algebraic theory of Boolean circuits~\cite{lafont2003boolean} as an early
landmark directly ancestral to circuit presentations like ours; those
developments are pen-and-paper, and mechanizing polygraphic coherence
along the lines of this library is an attractive direction.

\subsection{Related tools}\label{sec:tools}

Two unverified web tools compute normal forms of the kind this library
verifies: one rewrites linear permutation circuits to \emph{Lafont
normal form}~\cite{lafont2003boolean,lafontlp-tool}, the other rewrites
words over the generators of $Sp(2n,\mathbb{Z}_p)$ to Selinger-style
staircase normal forms~\cite{qupit-tool}.
