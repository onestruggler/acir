\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Size and typechecking cost}\label{sec:stats}

The verified development is about 22\,000 lines of Agda in 74 files
(September 2026; \texttt{wc -l}, comments included): half the reusable
core --- words, presentations with their constructions and solvers, the
normal-form zoo and the Reidemeister--Schreier engine, the circuit layer,
the \texttt{ForStdlib} additions, and a support library of modular
arithmetic --- and half the examples of \S\ref{sec:examples}, of which
the qutrit Clifford+$T$ and $U_3$ studies alone are 4\,300 lines.  Of
the seven thousand lines under \texttt{Presentation}, only about a
thousand are the core proper; 4\,300 are the five product constructions
with their two-sided theorems, the amalgamation alone 1\,800, and six
hundred are the solvers.  We expect the figure to shrink: the version of
the library developed since this paper was written has already retired
one of two semidirect-product modules.

Typechecking cost is modest.  Checking the whole development from
scratch with Agda~2.8.0 (61 modules, the standard library's interfaces
cached) takes 1\,min\,42\,s and peaks at 3.5\,GB of memory; a third of
the time is the amalgamated-product construction (31\,s), checked once
and for all, the three amalgamation case studies take 19\,s together,
and every other module is under 4\,s.  Discharging by evaluation the
obligations that reduce to computation has therefore not made checking
slow --- the seventy-two
\aid{refl}-discharged obligations of the nine-coset qutrit level are
among the eight seconds of their module.  The costs to watch are memory,
which grows with the terms the evaluator unfolds, and the edit--check
loop of the largest modules.

\subsection{Setoids versus quotients, revisited}\label{sec:setoids}

The deliberate design decision of \S\ref{sec:presentations} --- presented
monoids as setoids over inductive congruences, not as quotient types ---
deserves a balance sheet now that the case studies are on the table.
What it cost: every function out of words carries a congruence proof, and
records like \aid{Injection} exist precisely to bundle that bookkeeping.
What it bought: (i) normal-form maps, coset tables, and sections are
plain structurally recursive programs, so the hundreds of table
obligations in \S\ref{sec:examples} reduce to \aid{refl} by evaluation
--- in a quotient-based development each would eliminate through a
quotient before computing; (ii) sections --- maps \emph{into} the syntax
choosing representatives, the load-bearing ingredient of
\aid{NormalForm}, Schreier transversals, and amalgamation tails --- are
natural for setoids and awkward for quotients, where they are not in
general definable; (iii) the library remains inside plain Agda and the
standard library's algebra hierarchy, a requirement for upstreaming.  The
univalent alternative
\cite{vezzosi2019cubical,cubicallibrary} would erase cost (i)'s
congruence fields but forfeit (ii) definitionally and (iii) by
foundation.  For this problem domain --- where the whole game is
computing canonical representatives --- we found the setoid side of the
trade clearly favourable; the standard library's function bundles meant
the ``zoo'' of \S\ref{sec:nfproperty} needed no new theory at all.

\subsection{What the standard library lacked}\label{sec:stdlib-gap}

The \texttt{ForStdlib} layer (\S\ref{sec:forstdlib}) is a measure of the
gap between textbook group theory and what a widely used dependently
typed standard library currently provides: no semidirect products, no
amalgamated products or pushouts of monoids, no extensions or exact
sequences, no epimorphism structures, not even the permutations of
$\mathsf{Fin}\;n$ bundled as a group.  None of these are hard --- the
seven modules total 865 lines --- but each was necessary for some
semantic side of a presentation theorem, and we submit them as
independently useful.  The deeper omission is thematic: the standard
library's algebra hierarchy is rich in \emph{structures} but thin in
\emph{constructions}, and presentation-level results force the
constructions into existence.

\subsection{Why Agda, and why not a tactic language}\label{sec:whyagda}

Agda has no tactic language in the sense of Rocq, Lean, or Isabelle, and
one might expect a development of this kind --- hundreds of case-split
obligations, long equational chains --- to miss one.  Our experience is
the opposite, and the reason is the shape of the obligations.  Everything
the library asks its user to prove is a finite case split whose cases are
either closed by evaluation or filled by an equational derivation written
in the standard library's setoid-reasoning style; neither benefits much from
proof search, and both benefit from a strong, predictable evaluator and
from dependent pattern matching that keeps wire indices in step
(\S\ref{sec:circuits}).  The ``solvers'' of \S\ref{sec:solvers} are not
tactics but verified functions --- \aid{by-assoc} normalizes both sides to
lists, \aid{by-equal-nf} to normal forms, and the comparison is
\aid{refl} --- so the development uses no metaprogramming at all, and
nothing in it depends on the behaviour of a search procedure.  Two places
would profit from automation, and both are future work: generating the
hypothesis records of a coset table from the table alone (currently a
mechanical but hand-written case split, one clause per coset and axiom),
for which Agda's reflection mechanism would suffice; and finding the
tables, which we deliberately keep outside the checker
(\S\ref{sec:lessons}).  Conversely, nothing in the design is specific to
Agda: it would transfer to Rocq or Lean with their evaluators, provided
the setoid discipline is kept --- the quotient-based presented groups of
mathlib (\S\ref{sec:related}) would need exactly the sections we build,
and those are what quotients make awkward.

\subsection{Proof engineering lessons}\label{sec:lessons}

Four lessons generalize beyond this library.  \emph{Choose indices for
the unifier.}  The circuit layer's successor-only indexing
(\S\ref{sec:circuits}) looks like a small stylistic choice; it is the
difference between coverage checking succeeding by injectivity of
constructors and stuck arithmetic side conditions contaminating every
table.  \emph{Make certificates first-order.}  The five coset-table
hypotheses quantify over finite inductive types only, so obligations
split into finitely many closed goals --- the precondition for
\aid{refl}-discharge; hypotheses phrased over all words would have
demanded inductions in every instance.  \emph{Let evaluation prove what
it can.}  The most effective single tool in the development is
\aid{by-equal-nf Eq.refl}: once a normal form for a level exists, all
equational facts \emph{about that level} that happen to be true become
free.  This does not remove the case analyses --- the soundness law and
the uniqueness proof of each level are still proved by hand, case by
case, and they are most of the work --- but it shifts the effort to
setting up the next normal form, after which the obligations that are
mere computation cost nothing.  \emph{Separate search from checking.}
Coset tables were found by ordinary computation (and, for the larger
studies, by the companion web tools of \S\ref{sec:tools}); the library
only ever verifies them.  Nothing in the formal development depends on
how a table was produced, which kept the trusted story simple and the
exploration loop fast.

\subsection{Threats to the interpretation}\label{sec:threats}

Two honest caveats.  First, our completeness theorems are stated against
semantic groups built compositionally ($\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$, product
groups, extension groups) or against permutations; for the amalgamation
case studies the final theorems are isomorphisms between two
\emph{presentations}, whose semantic reading as the concrete matrix
monoids (e.g.\ Clifford+$T$ as unitaries over
$\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i]$~\cite{amy2020numbertheoretic,bian2021un})
additionally uses the paper-mathematics identification of those groups
with the amalgamated products --- the group-level amalgamation
presentation theorem of \S\ref{sec:constructions} closes this gap
whenever presentations of the three constituent groups are supplied, but
we have not yet formalized the matrix side itself.  Connecting to a
verified matrix semantics (\S\ref{sec:related}) would discharge this end
to end.

Second, statistics like ``every well-definedness obligation closed by
\aid{refl}'' describe our case studies, not a theorem about the method,
and they rest
on a finiteness assumption: at every level of a tower the cosets form a
finite inductive type, so the five hypotheses are finite case splits.
Three kinds of gate sets escape this.  Gate sets with continuous
parameters --- the phase and rotation gates of the complete equational
theories for full quantum
circuits~\cite{clement2023complete,clement2024minimal}, whose normal
forms carry real angles --- have no finite coset structure at all; their
completeness proofs are genuinely analytic, and the library offers them
its presentation vocabulary but not its engine.  Subgroup steps of
infinite index, such as the step from the base group to the lamplighter
group $\mathbb{Z}_N \wr \mathbb{Z}$ of \S\ref{sec:conclusion}, have
cosets indexed by an infinite type; the coset-table hypotheses are still
statable, but they would have to be proved by induction rather than by
finitely many evaluations, and nothing in our engine yet automates that.
(The amalgamation construction already handles one shape of infinite
index --- alternating words of arbitrary length --- which is why the
Clifford+$T$ monoids, of infinite index over their Clifford subgroups,
are within reach.)  Finally, finite but large tables, of the size that
computer search produces for multi-qubit gate
sets~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2,bian2023cliffordcs3}, remain within the
method in principle but will stress the evaluator; \S\ref{sec:stats}
gives the current costs, and the odd-prime Clifford development in
progress is the first real measurement of how they scale.
