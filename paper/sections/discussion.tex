\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Mechanization statistics}\label{sec:stats}

The repository contains about 22\,000 lines of Agda in 74 files
(November 2026; \texttt{wc -l}, comments included).  The verified index
module reaches 61 of these; the remainder includes auxiliary examples
and unfinished experiments.
The reusable core --- words, presentations, normal forms, the engine, the
constructions, the circuit layer --- is under ten thousand lines, with the
\texttt{ForStdlib} additions and a support library of modular arithmetic
beside it; the verified examples add
about the same again, and the largest single objects are two of the three
amalgamation case studies, the qutrit Clifford+$T$ and $U_3$ studies,
about 4\,260 lines together.  Of
the 7\,000 lines under \texttt{Presentation}, about 1\,000 are the core
proper; 4\,400 are the five product constructions with their normal-form
and presentation theorems, the amalgamation alone 1\,800; 600 are the
solvers; and the rest is the join primitive and the $S_n$ and wreath
presentations over a plain alphabet that the $U_3$ study uses.

A fresh check of the index module's 61-module dependency closure took
2\,min\,33\,s wall time and peaked at 3.54\,GiB resident memory in one
run on 26 November 2026.  We used Agda~2.8.0 and stdlib~2.4 on Ubuntu~26.04
under WSL2, with an Intel Core i5-13400TEF and 26\,GiB of memory visible
to WSL.  The command was \texttt{agda -{}-safe -{}-cubical-compatible
MainTheorems.agda}, measured with \texttt{/usr/bin/time -v}; project
interfaces were absent and the standard library's interfaces were cached.

\subsection{Setoids versus quotients, revisited}\label{sec:setoids}

Every function out of a presented monoid requires a
congruence proof, and records like \aid{Injection} bundle that bookkeeping.
Setoids let the implementation retain explicit word representatives and
use the standard library's algebra and function bundles directly.
Normalizers and reconstruction maps are ordinary recursive programs,
and many closed table obligations reduce to \aid{refl} by evaluation.
Derivations of $\approx$ are also data, on which the congruence-lifting
lemmas of \S\ref{sec:constructions} are inductions.
A quotient implementation would require suitable eliminations and
congruence proofs.  It could still expose computable representatives
once a canonical normalization function had been established, as in the
cubical library's free-group development~\cite{cubicallibrary}.
Our choice avoids that additional quotient interface and retains
compatibility with the standard library.

\subsection{What the standard library lacked}

The \texttt{ForStdlib} layer (\S\ref{sec:forstdlib}) adds constructions
missing from the standard library: semidirect products, monoid
amalgamations, group extensions and exact sequences, epimorphism
structures, and a bundled permutation group.  These seven modules total
865 lines and support the semantic presentation theorems.

\subsection{Proof engineering lessons}

Four design choices were useful in this development.  \emph{Choose indices for
the unifier.}  Successor-only indexing (\S\ref{sec:circuits}) lets the
coverage checker use constructor injectivity, avoiding arithmetic
constraints in coset-table definitions.  \emph{Use generator-level
certificates.}  The five coset-table hypotheses reduce whole-word laws
to conditions on generators, coset labels, and axiom instances.  For
fixed finite tables these can yield closed goals; parameterized
families such as $S_n$ also require induction.  \emph{Let evaluation
prove what it can.}  Once a normal form is available for a level,
\aid{by-equal-nf Eq.refl} proves equations whose two sides compute to
definitionally equal normal forms.  Soundness and uniqueness still
require equational or inductive proofs, but the closed computational
cases need no further equational derivation once the lower-level normal
form is available.
\emph{Separate search from checking.}  Coset tables were found by
ordinary computation outside Agda, and only their verification is
formal.  Agda verifies the coset-table hypotheses independently of the
procedure used to find the table.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:threats}

Two limitations.  First, our completeness theorems are stated against
semantic groups built compositionally ($\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$, product
groups, extension groups) or against permutations; for the amalgamation
case studies the final theorems are isomorphisms between two
\emph{presentations}, whose semantic reading as the concrete matrix
monoids (e.g.\ Clifford+$T$~\cite{giles2013remarks,glaudell2019qutrit},
or the unitaries over
$\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i]$~\cite{amy2020numbertheoretic,bian2021un})
additionally uses the paper-mathematics identification of those groups
with the amalgamated products, which we have not formalized.
Second, the counts of obligations discharged by \aid{refl} describe
particular fixed tables.  The method does not guarantee this behavior:
parameterized families already use induction, and infinite label types
would need proofs beyond finite enumeration.
