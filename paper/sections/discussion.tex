\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Mechanization statistics}\label{sec:stats}

The verified development is about 22\,000 lines of Agda in 74 files
(September 2026; \texttt{wc -l}, comments included), 61 of which the
index module reaches; the rest are examples and auxiliary modules
nothing imports.
The reusable core (words, presentations, normal forms, the engine, the
constructions, the circuit layer) is under ten thousand lines, with the
\texttt{ForStdlib} additions and a support library of modular arithmetic
beside it; the verified examples add
about the same again, and the largest single objects are two of the three
amalgamation case studies, the qutrit Clifford+$T$ and $U_3$ studies,
about 4\,260 lines together.  Of
the 7\,000 lines under \texttt{Presentation}, about 1\,000 are the core
proper; 4\,400 are the five product constructions with their normal-form
and presentation theorems, the amalgamation alone 1\,800; 600 are the
solvers; and the rest is the join primitive and the $S_n$ and wreath
presentations over a plain alphabet that the $U_3$ study uses.

Typechecking cost is modest.  Checking the whole development from
scratch with Agda~2.8.0 (61 modules, the standard library's interfaces
cached) takes 1\,min\,42\,s and peaks at 3.5\,GB of memory; a third of
the time is the amalgamated-product construction (31\,s), checked once
and for all; the three amalgamation case studies take 19\,s together,
and every other module is under 4\,s.

\subsection{Setoids versus quotients, revisited}\label{sec:setoids}

The deliberate design decision of \S\ref{sec:presentations} (presented
monoids as setoids over inductive congruences, not as quotient types)
deserves a balance sheet now that the case studies are on the table.
What it cost: every function out of the presented monoid carries a
congruence proof, and records like \aid{Injection} carry that bookkeeping.
What it bought: (i) normal-form maps, coset tables, and sections are
plain structurally recursive programs, so the hundreds of table
well-definedness obligations in \S\ref{sec:examples} reduce to \aid{refl}
by evaluation;
(ii) sections, maps \emph{into} the syntax
choosing representatives (the load-bearing ingredient of
\aid{NormalForm}, Schreier transversals, and amalgamation tails), are
natural for setoids and awkward for quotients, where they are not in
general definable; (iii) the library remains inside plain Agda and the
standard library's algebra hierarchy, a requirement for upstreaming;
and, secondarily, (iv) derivations of $\approx$ are data, on which the
congruence-lifting lemmas of \S\ref{sec:constructions} are inductions.
The univalent alternative \cite{vezzosi2019cubical,cubicallibrary} would
turn equational reasoning into rewriting, but its eliminator still
demands a congruence proof for every function out of the quotient, and
(ii) and (iii) would be forfeited.  For this problem domain, where
the whole game is computing canonical representatives, we found the
setoid side of the trade clearly favorable; the standard library's
function bundles meant the ``zoo'' of \S\ref{sec:nfproperty} needed no
new theory at all.

\subsection{What the standard library lacked}

The \texttt{ForStdlib} layer (\S\ref{sec:forstdlib}) is a measure of the
gap between textbook group theory and what a widely used dependently
typed standard library currently provides: no semidirect products, no
amalgamated products or pushouts of monoids, no extensions or exact
sequences, no epimorphism structures, not even the permutations of
$\mathsf{Fin}\;n$ bundled as a group.  None of these are hard (the
seven modules total 865 lines), but each was necessary for some
semantic side of a presentation theorem, and we offer them as
independently useful.  The deeper omission is thematic: the standard
library's algebra hierarchy is rich in \emph{structures} but thin in
\emph{constructions}, and presentation-level results force the
constructions into existence.

\subsection{Proof engineering lessons}

Four lessons generalize beyond this library.  \emph{Choose indices for
the unifier.}  The circuit layer's successor-only indexing
(\S\ref{sec:circuits}) looks like a small stylistic choice; it is the
difference between coverage checking succeeding by injectivity of
constructors and stuck arithmetic side conditions contaminating every
table.  \emph{Make certificates finite.}  The five coset-table
hypotheses quantify over generators, cosets, and axiom instances only,
so obligations split into finitely many closed goals, the
precondition for \aid{refl}-discharge; hypotheses phrased over all words
would have demanded inductions in every instance.  \emph{Let evaluation
prove what it can.}  The single most effective tool in the development
is \aid{by-equal-nf Eq.refl}: once a normal form for a level exists, all
equational facts \emph{about that level} that happen to be true become
free.  This does not remove the case analyses (the soundness law and
the uniqueness proof of each level are still proved by hand, case by
case), but it shifts the effort to setting up the next normal form,
after which the obligations that are mere computation cost nothing.
\emph{Separate search from checking.}  Coset tables were found by
ordinary computation outside Agda, and only their verification is
formal.  Nothing in the formal development depends on how a table was
produced, which kept the trusted story simple and the exploration loop
fast.

\subsection{Threats to the interpretation}\label{sec:threats}

Two honest caveats.  First, our completeness theorems are stated against
semantic groups built compositionally ($\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$, product
groups, extension groups) or against permutations; for the amalgamation
case studies the final theorems are isomorphisms between two
\emph{presentations}, whose semantic reading as the concrete matrix
monoids (e.g.\ Clifford+$T$~\cite{giles2013remarks,glaudell2019qutrit},
or the unitaries over
$\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i]$~\cite{amy2020numbertheoretic,bian2021un})
additionally uses the paper-mathematics identification of those groups
with the amalgamated products, which we have not formalized.
Second, statistics like ``every well-definedness obligation closed by
\aid{refl}'' describe our case studies, not a theorem about the method;
gate sets whose coset structure is not finitely enumerable per level
would need genuinely new ideas.  Gate sets with continuous parameters,
such as the phase and rotation gates of the complete equational theories
for full quantum circuits~\cite{clement2023complete,clement2024minimal},
whose normal forms carry real angles, have no finite coset structure at
all; and subgroup steps of infinite index, such as the lamplighter groups
$\mathbb{Z}_N \wr \mathbb{Z}$, have cosets indexed by an infinite type,
so that the coset-table hypotheses are statable but need induction rather
than finitely many evaluations.
