\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Equational reasoning is the daily bread of circuit design.  Whether one
optimizes a quantum circuit, verifies a compiler pass, or normalizes a
diagram, the justification is always the same: a finite set of local rewrite
rules, applied to a syntactic object, preserving a semantic interpretation.
Two questions attach to any such rule set.  \emph{Soundness} --- does every
rule preserve the semantics? --- is typically easy: it is a finite collection
of small algebraic identities.  \emph{Completeness} --- can every true
semantic equation be derived from the rules? --- is a different animal
altogether.

Completeness proofs are gigantic computations.  The standard strategy defines
a \emph{normal form} for circuits, proves that every circuit can be rewritten
to normal form using the rules, and proves that distinct normal forms denote
distinct semantic objects.  The middle step is where the explosion happens:
one must show, for every rule and every way a generator can meet a normal
form, that the combination reduces again --- a case analysis that is
mechanical, unforgiving, and enormous.  The literature wears the scars
openly.  Selinger's presentation of the $n$-qubit Clifford group already
required a substantial rewrite system and normal form
analysis~\cite{selinger2015clifford}.  Its recent qutrit
analogue by Li, Mosca, Ross, van~de~Wetering, and Zhao --- the first
completeness result for a fragment of quantum circuits in an odd prime
dimension --- runs to more than fifty pages of
proofs~\cite{li2025qutrit}.  The first complete equational theory for full
quantum circuits, by Cl\'ement, Heurtel, Mansfield, Perdrix, and
Valiron~\cite{clement2023complete}, and its minimal
refinement~\cite{clement2024minimal}, rest on intricate normal-form
manipulations; and Cl\'ement's complete equational theory for
Real-Clifford+$CH$ circuits consists of a thirteen-page paper attached to an
appendix of \emph{264 pages} of equational
derivations~\cite{clement2026realcliffordch}.  These are not outliers: the
same tens of pages of mechanical calculation stand behind Selinger's
normal-form analysis and its qutrit successor cited above, behind the
rig-categorical completeness theorems for quantum programming
languages~\cite{carette2024squareroots,fang2026hadamard}, and behind the
recent uniform simplification of the rule sets of six near-Clifford circuit
fragments~\cite{blake2026simpler} --- long, hand-written, hand-checked
derivations every one of which deserves a machine's scrutiny.  Proofs of
this shape --- long, regular, and computational --- are precisely the proofs
that should be machine-checked, and precisely the proofs that a proof assistant with a
strong evaluator can check largely \emph{by computation}.

This paper reports on an Agda library built for exactly this purpose: a
general, reusable formal framework for \emph{presentations of monoids and
groups by generators and relations}, for \emph{normal forms}, and for
\emph{completeness} of finite relation sets, with circuits --- words of
wire-indexed gates --- as the motivating instance.  The library is written in
safe, axiom-free Agda~\cite{norell2007thesis,bove2009agda} against the Agda
standard library~\cite{agdastdlib}, is compatible with the
\texttt{--cubical-compatible} discipline, and is being prepared for
submission to the standard library; the semantic constructions it needed and
the standard library lacked (semidirect products, amalgamated free products
of monoids, group extensions, epimorphisms, the permutation group bundled as
a group) are staged in a dedicated \texttt{ForStdlib} hierarchy.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(0)] \textbf{A library for circuit normal forms and complete relation
  sets.}  A layered Agda library in which a relation set is an inductive
  family on words, the presented monoid is the inductively generated
  congruence closure (\S\ref{sec:design}), normal-form witnesses are the
  standard library's function bundles out of the word setoid, and a single
  generic lemma, \aid{by-normalization}, turns soundness plus a \emph{unique
  normal form} into completeness (\S\ref{sec:nfproperty}).  A verified,
  monoid-level Reidemeister--Schreier engine constructs normal forms by coset
  enumeration, one verified coset table per level
  (\S\ref{sec:engine}).

\item[(1)] \textbf{Inductively defined circuits, for easy normalization and
  completeness proofs.}  A generic circuit layer represents $n$-wire circuits
  as words over wire-indexed generators, and extends any gate-specific
  relation set with the structural rules --- shift congruence and
  far-commutation --- shared by every circuit presentation
  (\S\ref{sec:circuits}).  The indexing discipline is chosen so that Agda's
  evaluator, not the human, discharges the case analyses: in our case
  studies, coset-table obligations close by \aid{refl} after computation of
  both sides.

\item[(2)] \textbf{A catalogue of verified presentations}
  (\S\ref{sec:examples}).  Seven nontrivial example families, several of them
  formalizing presentations from the research literature: cyclic groups
  of every positive order (with, at order $0$, the free monoid
  $\mathbb{N}$ presented as a monoid --- the smallest witness that our
  presentations are monoid presentations); symmetric groups $S_n$ presented as circuits,
  proved sound and complete for a loose endofunction semantics and presented
  for the group of permutations of $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$; wreath products
  $\mathbb{Z}_N \wr S_n$ --- the groups of \emph{signed permutations} and
  their generalizations; Pauli groups $(\mathbb{Z}_p \times \mathbb{Z}_p)^n$
  for every odd prime $p$, assembled purely compositionally; and, as
  two- and four-level amalgamated free products with verified alternating
  normal forms, the single-qubit Clifford+$T$ monoid, a single-qutrit
  Clifford+$T$ monoid, and $U_3(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$ from Bian and
  Selinger's presentation of
  $U_n(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$~\cite{bian2021un}.  Every headline theorem is
  restated, with its full type, in a single index module
  \texttt{MainTheorems.agda}.

\item[(3)] \textbf{Monoid-level amalgamation and Reidemeister--Schreier.}
  Following the methodology that Bian and Selinger introduced for their
  syntactic verifications~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2,bian2023cliffordcs3,
  bian2023thesis}, the library generalizes the group-theoretic notions ---
  coset enumeration, the Reidemeister--Schreier subgroup presentation, and
  amalgamated free products --- from groups to monoid presentations, where
  inverses are not primitive.  Beyond the syntactic side, we prove the
  corresponding \emph{semantic} presentation theorems: if $\Gamma$ presents
  $G_1$ and $\Delta$ presents $G_2$, the constructed relation sets present
  the direct product, the semidirect product, the $n$-fold product, the
  amalgamated free product, and (given a realizing short exact sequence) an
  arbitrary group extension (\S\ref{sec:constructions}).

\item[(4)] \textbf{New theorems for the standard library.}  The semantic
  side of the above required algebraic constructions absent from the Agda
  standard library: semidirect products of monoids and groups via a bundled
  \aid{Action} record; amalgamated free products of monoids as a pushout
  presented by words over the disjoint union of carriers; group extensions
  and split extensions as short exact sequences; monoid epimorphisms; the
  fact that a monoid homomorphism between groups is a group homomorphism;
  and the group of permutations of $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$ bundled as a
  \aid{Group} (\S\ref{sec:forstdlib}).
\end{enumerate}

Everything stated as a theorem in this paper typechecks under
\texttt{--safe}: there are no postulates, no unfinished holes, and no
appeals to classical axioms anywhere in the development described here.  The
driving next target --- machine-checked completeness for multi-qudit
Clifford circuits in all odd prime dimensions, the formal counterpart of
the qutrit rule set of~\cite{li2025qutrit} and its odd-prime
generalization --- is discussed as future work (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{A first taste: permutations}\label{sec:taste}

Section~\ref{sec:permutations} develops the smallest interesting instance
end to end, and it is worth previewing here.  Take circuits over a single
two-wire gate $\sigma$ (swap).  The gate-specific relations are the two
classics --- $\sigma^2 = \varepsilon$ and the Yang--Baxter relation $\sigma
\cdot (\sigma{\uparrow}) \cdot \sigma = (\sigma{\uparrow}) \cdot \sigma \cdot
(\sigma{\uparrow})$ --- and the framework adds, once and for all, the
structural rules every circuit theory shares.  The library's coset-tower
engine then produces a normal form whose carrier at $n$ wires has exactly
$n!$ elements --- a factorial number system, with one ``digit'' per level of
the subgroup chain $S_1 < S_2 < \dots < S_n$ --- together with proofs that
normalization is sound and that the normal form is unique for the semantics
of permutations of $\mathsf{Fin}\;n$.  Soundness plus uniqueness yields, by
the generic lemma, the completeness theorem: two circuits denote the same
permutation exactly when the two relations above (plus structural rules)
prove them equal.  Packaged as a record, this is the statement that the
relation set \emph{presents} the permutation group:
\begin{agdacode}
symmetric-presentation :
  ∀ n → (n VRel,_===_) IsPresentationOf (Permutation′-group n)
\end{agdacode}
The same pipeline, run on larger towers, yields the Clifford+$T$ and
$U_3(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12,i])$ case studies.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related-intro}

The closest prior work is the line of Agda verifications accompanying Bian
and Selinger's presentations of quantum gate
groups~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2,bian2023cliffordcs3,bian2023thesis}.  Those
developments verify that two \emph{syntactic} presentations of a group are
equivalent --- an isomorphism of finitely presented monoids obtained by
Reidemeister--Schreier steps --- and never mention a semantics: no
independent model of the group appears, so soundness and completeness with
respect to an interpretation are not stated.  The present library grew out
of that methodology and subsumes it: our amalgamation case studies
(\S\ref{sec:examples}) end in exactly such syntactic isomorphisms, but the
framework additionally relates presentations to independently constructed
semantic groups (permutations, product groups, extension groups), which is
what licenses the words ``sound'' and ``complete''.  The engine is also
reusable: coset tables, towers, and product constructions are library
modules with explicit hypothesis records, not per-paper proofs.

In the proof-assistant landscape more broadly, quantum-circuit developments
--- \textsc{Qwire}~\cite{paykin2017qwire}, \textsc{sqir}/\textsc{voqc}
\cite{hietala2021voqc,hietala2021proving}, VyZX~\cite{lehmann2025vyzx},
CoqQ~\cite{zhou2023coqq}, Qbricks~\cite{chareton2021qbricks}, and the
Isabelle libraries~\cite{liu2019quantum,bordg2020dirac} --- verify
\emph{programs}, \emph{compilers}, or the \emph{soundness} of rewrite rules
against a fixed semantics.  Completeness of a rule set --- the meta-theoretic
statement that the rules derive every semantic equality --- is not addressed
by any of them; to our knowledge the only mechanized completeness results in
the vicinity are the Bian--Selinger line above and, in categorical form, the
rig-groupoid school's: Choudhury, Karwowski, and Sabry's univalent
presentation of the classical reversible language $\Pi$ as a free rig
groupoid~\cite{choudhury2022symmetries}, and the partial Agda formalization
--- complete for the two-qubit Clifford fragment --- accompanying its
quantum square-root extension~\cite{carette2024squareroots}.  The most
recent rig-categorical completeness theorems for quantum circuit languages
\cite{fang2026hadamard,heunen2026onerig} remain pen-and-paper.  On the algebra side, Lean's mathlib defines presented groups as
quotients of free groups and builds Coxeter groups on top of
them~\cite{mathlib2020,mathlib-doc-presented}, Isabelle's Archive of Formal
Proofs contains free groups and the Nielsen--Schreier
theorem~\cite{breitner2010freegroups,kharim2023freegroups}, and the
Coq/MathComp tradition culminating in the Odd Order
theorem~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder} develops deep finite group theory ---
but none of these provide coset-enumeration infrastructure for proving that
a \emph{concrete} relation set presents a \emph{concrete} group, which is
the bread and butter of this library.  Section~\ref{sec:related} makes these
comparisons in detail, including the univalent
alternatives~\cite{vezzosi2019cubical,cubicallibrary,agdaunimath,
mangel2023sign} and formalized rewriting~\cite{sternagel2013kb,
hirokawa2019abstract}.

\subsubsection{Related tools}\label{sec:tools}

Two web tools implement the mathematics this library mechanizes.  A
normalizer to \emph{Lafont normal form} for linear permutation circuits
implements the normalization lemmas of Lafont's algebraic theory of Boolean
circuits~\cite{lafont2003boolean,lafontlp-tool}; and a normalizer for the
symplectic groups $Sp(2n,\mathbb{Z}_p)$ computes Selinger-style staircase
normal forms~\cite{qupit-tool}.  Both tools compute normal forms of
exactly the kind this library is built to prove correct and complete; the formalized proofs are on-going and close to finish.
