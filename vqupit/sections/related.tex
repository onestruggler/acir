\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Verified circuit transformations and optimization.}
\textsc{sqir}/\textsc{voqc}~\cite{hietala2021voqc} verifies quantum
circuit transformations against complex-matrix semantics.
\textsc{Qwire}~\cite{paykin2017qwire}, CoqQ~\cite{zhou2023coqq}, and
Qbricks~\cite{chareton2021qbricks} provide languages and infrastructure
for reasoning about quantum programs.
Quartz~\cite{xu2022quartz} generates candidate transformations by
enumerating small circuits, verifies them with an automated theorem
prover, and applies them in a cost-based search.
Our presentation theorems address whether a fixed family of equations
captures all equalities in the algebraic semantics. The certificate
client demonstrates one use of that contract; we do not evaluate an
optimizer integration or inherit these systems' matrix interpretation.

\paragraph{The qupit presentation and its predecessors.}
The closest work is the qupit presentation of
Bian et al.~\cite{bian2026qudit}. Version~2 reports Agda verification of
the scalar-sensitive rules and refers to a companion manuscript for the
formalization. Table~\ref{tab:provenance} distinguishes that shared
development from the generic library~\cite{bian2026framework} and the
proof architecture presented here. The present contribution concerns how the library's hypotheses are
discharged: semantic separation and smaller-width completeness certify
the recursive tables, and explicit compatibility proofs connect the
layers to structural semantics. The mathematical rule set and the
composition theorems themselves belong to the companion work.

The normal-form method descends from Selinger's qubit Clifford
presentation~\cite{selinger2015clifford} and its qutrit
generalization~\cite{li2025qutrit}.  Other gate-group presentations
include CNOT-dihedral~\cite{amy2018cnotdihedral}, real
stabilizer~\cite{makary2021realstabilizer}, two-qubit
Clifford+$T$~\cite{bian2023cliffordt2}, three-qubit
Clifford+$CS$~\cite{bian2023cliffordcs3}, and
Toffoli--Hadamard circuits~\cite{amy2024toffolihadamard}.
Bian and Selinger's Agda developments check equivalences between
syntactic presentations.  Here those translations are one part of a
chain that also establishes a presentation of independently constructed
projective and scalar semantics.

\paragraph{Complete circuit and graphical calculi.}
Cl\'ement et al. give complete equational theories for arbitrary
quantum circuits~\cite{clement2023complete}, with extensions for
ancillae and discarding~\cite{clement2024extensions}.
Blake's polycontrolled PROPs give a finite schematic theory for exact
qudit circuits in arbitrary finite dimension~\cite{blake2026polycontrolled};
his phase-affine calculi obtain completeness through normal
forms~\cite{blake2026phaseaffine}.
These languages and rule sets have different primitives and scope from
the Clifford gate language considered here.
The ZX-calculus has completeness results for stabilizer fragments and
Clifford+$T$~\cite{backens2014zx,jeandel2018zx,booth2022qupitzx,poor2023travaganza}.
A graphical completeness proof can use intermediate diagrams outside
the unitary circuit language; obtaining derivations entirely within a
fixed circuit gate set requires an additional argument.
VyZX~\cite{lehmann2026vyzx} supplies formal infrastructure for graphical
quantum reasoning.

\paragraph{Mechanized algebra and reversible languages.}
Choudhury, Karwowski, and Sabry~\cite{choudhury2022symmetries} mechanize
the reversible language $\Pi$ as a free symmetric rig groupoid.
Carette et al.~\cite{carette2024squareroots} extend this setting toward
quantum computation, including a mechanized two-qubit Clifford
completeness result.  Their categorical semantics and our fixed-width
group presentations expose different interfaces for equational reasoning.
General-purpose algebra libraries provide a further foundation:
mathlib has presented groups~\cite{mathlib2020,mathlib-doc-presented},
Isabelle developments formalize free groups and
Nielsen--Schreier~\cite{breitner2010freegroups,kharim2023freegroups},
and MathComp supports large finite-group proofs~\cite{gonthier2013oddorder}.
Our use of coset tables specializes classical computational group
theory~\cite{sims1994computation,holt2005handbook} to a supplied circuit
relation set, with every table entry justified in its generated
congruence and the semantic uniqueness proof supplied separately.
