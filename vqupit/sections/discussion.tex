\section{Validation, Proof Engineering, and Limits}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the checked statements establish}

\texttt{MainTheorems.agda} restates the main results with full signatures
and imports their implementations, including the structural scalar
isomorphism, the phase-repair client, the symmetric-group example, and
structural-rule independence. The implementation checks with Agda~2.8.0
and standard library~2.4 under \texttt{--safe}, without postulates or
holes. The trust base includes Agda's implementation and the checked
standard library. No external algebra system supplies unchecked results.
Most literate excerpts are only scope-checked; the new structural
presentation and client excerpts are also fully typechecked aliases.

All four semantic endpoints in Table~\ref{tab:targets} are now built
from finite-field data and symplectic transformations. The exact
proof still uses word groups internally, but the checked map $\chi$
removes them from the target. Its definition fixes how scalars and
projective gates are read; it is not just an existence claim about
isomorphic groups.

The remaining semantic obligation is a commuting interpretation into
complex matrices: each generator must map to the intended matrix with
its specified phase. The representation argument in
Appendix~\ref{sec:matrix-background} provides external mathematical context, not this
checked correspondence. In particular, the structural bridge does not
by itself certify a compiler whose specification is matrix equality.
The multiplier rules also take a primitive root and a proof that its
powers cover the nonzero field; the artifact does not construct that
witness for every prime.

The multiplier change illustrates the separation concretely.
Replacing the $R$-spelling by the original $S$-spelling requires a
Pauli-prefixed multiplier identity and checking the affected axioms
(\S\ref{sec:plumbing}); it does not alter the coset tables or their
uniqueness proof. Conversely, changing the symplectic normal boxes
would require new local certificates and a new head-injectivity
argument. Decomposition does not make those obligations disappear.

The scalar layer also exposes a limit of generic reuse.
The general extension theorem expects bijective normal forms for its
factors. The projective presentation here is not exported in that form,
so the exact proof establishes completeness by splitting scalar and
gate words instead. The structural bridge subsequently uses the two
factor isomorphisms and cocycle agreement, without introducing another
normalizer.

\subsection{Execution checks and reproducibility}

The symplectic normalizer is executable. The module
\texttt{Symplectic.Normalization.Computations} checks nine closed
equalities by reduction alone: normalization of $S^p$ and $H^4$ at
width one, and of $CZ^p$ at width two, for each $p=3,5,7$, agrees with
normalization of the empty circuit. Each proof is reflexivity after
evaluation, not an application of the corresponding presentation axiom.
These are small execution checks, not a circuit benchmark suite.

The two-qutrit client (\S\ref{sec:client-example}) additionally checks
a replacement involving both wires and multiple entangling gates,
including its semantic premise and scalar correction. Its compact
evaluator is proved correct for every word over the five two-wire
gate positions; the equality check used here enumerates phase-space
vectors. We report no scalable checker, optimizer integration, or
bound on generated derivation size. The example demonstrates the
certificate contract; it does not measure general optimization quality.

\paragraph{Managing definitional computation.}
Unfolding semantic definitions through presentation translations can
expand large proof terms during conversion checking. The phase
evaluator therefore exposes defining equations, and the structural
bridge uses cocycle agreement. Explicit endpoints for extensional
equality prevent unification from reconstructing symplectic records.
These choices affect type checking, not the trust boundary.

\subsection{AI Use}

Versions of Claude assisted the Agda formalization, including composition
of box relations for \aid{ract-sound}, the symplectic-group definition,
and equivalence with the original rules modulo scalars. Claude also
produced the initial \aid{presentation-exact} proof and the Figure~1
layer with $-1$. These contributions were checked and corrected by the
authors. Claude assisted the earlier manuscript draft. OpenAI Codex
assisted the PLDI revision, source inspection, and validation, and
implemented the structural scalar bridge, its sign extension, the
phase-repair client, the closed execution checks, and the two-qutrit
certificate example with its discovery script added in this revision. Agda checks the resulting proof terms; it does not check that
their statements match the intended mathematics or that the prose
accurately describes their scope.
