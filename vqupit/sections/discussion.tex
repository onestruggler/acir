\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is verified, and what is not}

Everything stated as a theorem in \S\S\ref{sec:background}--%
\ref{sec:composing} is checked by Agda~2.8 with the standard library
2.4, under \texttt{--safe}, with no postulates and no holes; the
projective chain is \texttt{--cubical-compatible} as well.  The
headline theorems --- \aid{unique-nf}, \aid{simplified-presentation},
\aid{v1-presentation}, \aid{clifford-presentation} --- are restated
with full types in the index module \texttt{MainTheorems.agda}, which
reaches, through them, everything those proofs rest on; the exact
presentation \aid{presentation-exact} is checked from its own root.
Every theorem is parametric in the odd prime $p$, the width $n$, and a
primitive root $g$.

Three things are \emph{not} formalised, and they delimit what ``a
machine-checked proof of the qupit paper's theorem'' means here.
\emph{The glue facts.}  Our semantic groups are constructed: $\Sp$ is
the group of linear symplectic bijections of $\Zp^{2n}$, $\PPauli$ is
$\Zp^{2n}$ under addition, \aid{Pauli⋊Sp} is their semidirect product,
and the Clifford group with scalars is a central extension of that by a
cocycle.  That these are isomorphic to the groups of unitary matrices
generated by the paper's $H$, $S$, $CZ$ modulo (or not modulo) phases is
classical --- the paper's Proposition~2.25 and Theorem~2.26, citing
Weil --- and is not formalised.  No complex number, matrix or unitary
group appears in the qupit development; a verified matrix semantics
would close this gap (\S\ref{sec:conclusion}).  \emph{The
identification of the two central extensions.}  The structural Clifford
group (Weyl cocycle on the natural semidirect product) and the presented
one have quotients that are provably isomorphic and cocycles that agree
by construction; that the two \emph{total} groups are isomorphic is not
stated as a theorem.  \emph{The primitive root.}  Every module takes a
generator $g$ of $\Zp^{*}$ and the witness \aid{g-gen} as parameters,
mirroring the paper, whose Figure~1 is stated for a chosen $g$; the
existence of a primitive root modulo every prime is not in the library.

\subsection{Proof-engineering lessons}

\emph{Prove well-definedness by semantics.}  The single most
consequential decision in the symplectic development is that the coset
table's respect for the axioms --- the paper's ``the dirty gates get
removed in the same way whichever rewrite one applies'' --- is proved
not by analysing the table but by uniqueness one level down: two
congruent inputs produce cosets with the same bottom-wire action, so by
head injectivity the same coset; the residuals then agree by
cancellation, given injectivity of $\uparrow$, which is faithfulness
one wire below.  This turns the coset tower into a well-founded
induction on the width and removes any need to reason about rewrite
paths.  \emph{Transport, do not re-prove.}  Every rule set above the
semidirect level is connected to the one below by an isomorphism of
presentations, and theorems are carried across by congruence lemmas
rather than re-derived; calculi shared by several rule sets are stated
once over an abstract relation with the differing facts as hypotheses.
\emph{Make the semantics compute.}  Modelling $\Sp$ by transformations
rather than matrices, with extensional equality of the action, made
associativity and units definitional, let the ring solver discharge
every generator obligation, and made the semidirect action definable
from the same phase space the Pauli presentation targets, which is what
allowed the two well-definedness conditions of the semidirect theorem
to be proved by soundness and completeness rather than by hand.

\subsection{On the use of a language model}

A language model was used to help write the Agda code and to draft this
paper from an outline written by the authors; the formalisation was
designed, checked and corrected by the authors, and the paper was
checked and revised by them.  We record this for transparency, and
because a verified development is one whose correctness does not depend
on who, or what, wrote it.
