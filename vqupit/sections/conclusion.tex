\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Completeness at a smaller width can certify the next normalization
step when a semantic observation separates its representatives.
The qupit development realizes this dependency in a certified coset
tower, then restores Pauli and scalar information by composition.
Its structural interpretation also supports a checked client: a
nontrivial circuit replacement receives an exact equational certificate
and its required phase correction. The proof architecture separates
local derivations, semantic uniqueness, and compatibility obligations.
A gate-preserving complex-matrix interpretation and a general search
procedure remain outside the checked result.
