\section{Circuit Equations and the Proof Interface}\label{sec:framework}

The framework~\cite{bian2026framework} represents a circuit language by
a family of gates indexed by arity.  A generator is a gate on the bottom
wires or a generator shifted up one wire; a circuit of width $n$ is a
word over these generators.  Words have constructors $[g]^{\mathsf w}$,
$\varepsilon$, and $u\bullet v$ for a gate, identity, and composition.
Wire counts in the types rule out out-of-bounds placements.  Words are
drawn left to right as written, with the right factor acting first.
The operation $c\,{\uparrow}$ shifts every gate up one wire.

\paragraph{From equations to derivations.}
A rule set is an inductive family $\Gamma_n(u,v)$, written
$n\;\aid{SRel,}\;u\;\aid{===}\;v$ in the code.  It contains the
gate-specific equations.  The library adds structural rules for shifting
equations and commuting gates on disjoint wires.  A zero-wire scalar
also has a rule identifying it with its shift.  The resulting raw
relation generates a monoid congruence $\approx$, closed under
reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, composition, associativity, and
the unit laws.  A proof of $u\approx v$ is therefore an explicit
equational derivation, not a test of semantic equality.  The relation
$\aid{===}$ and its congruence $\approx$ remain distinct throughout.

\paragraph{The presentation contract.}
For a group $G$ with equality $\doteq$, a theorem
$\Gamma\;\aid{IsPresentationOf}\;G$ supplies an interpretation
$\sem{\_}$, inverses for the presented generators, and a group
isomorphism.  In particular, it establishes
\[
  u\approx v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \sem{u}\doteq\sem{v},
  \qquad
  \forall x\in G.\;\exists u.\;\sem{u}\doteq x.
\]
The forward implication is soundness, the reverse is completeness, and
the last property is expressiveness.  The semantic target matters:
defining $G$ to be the same word quotient would not establish a connection
to an independently specified semantics.

The last two interpretations pass through presented extensions
internally; Theorem~\ref{thm:structural} removes their word-quotient
components from the semantic endpoint. No row asserts equality of
complex matrices. That further correspondence must preserve the
interpretation of each named gate, not just the abstract group.

\paragraph{Normal forms through coset tables.}
One way to discharge this contract is to compute a normal form
$\mathit{nf}:\mathsf{Circuit}\;n\to\NF_n$ and a circuit
$[u]$ for each normal form, with $c\approx[\mathit{nf}(c)]$.
If equal denotations imply equal normal forms, soundness then gives
completeness.  The library constructs such normalizers one wire at a
time.  A coset table takes a representative $c$ and a generator $g$ and
returns a residual word $b'$ on fewer wires and a new representative
$c'$, together with a derivation
\[
  [c]\bullet[g]^{\mathsf w}\approx b'\,{\uparrow}\bullet[c'].
\]
Iterating the table traverses a word; iterating the construction over
the width builds a coset tower.  The table must also respect the axioms:
equivalent inputs give equal representative labels and congruent
residuals.  This well-definedness obligation is the key proof problem
in \S\ref{sec:pushing}.

Appendix~\ref{sec:permutations} illustrates this interface for swaps.
In the symplectic case the representatives encode the action on a
two-dimensional wire space.

\paragraph{Composition and translations.}
The other library interface combines presentations by adding interaction
laws. In a semidirect product, these move an acting generator past a
normal-subgroup generator by conjugating it. In a central extension,
each quotient relator additionally receives a scalar correction.
A change of presentation supplies word-valued maps in both directions,
preservation of axioms, and inverse laws modulo congruence.
Appendix~\ref{sec:constructions} gives the generic signatures; these
constructions are dependencies rather than contributions of this paper.
