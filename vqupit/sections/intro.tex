\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A complete equational theory turns semantic equality into a derivation:
if two programs have the same meaning, the theory's equations connect
them. For a circuit language, this gives a precise contract for a
certifying transformation tool. A client can establish equality in a
semantic model and obtain a proof using the language's own equations.
Completeness does not choose a cheap circuit or an efficient search
strategy; it justifies the passage from semantic reasoning back to syntax.

We study the mechanization of this contract for Clifford circuits on
\emph{qupits}, quantum systems of odd prime dimension $p$. The gates are
$H$, $S$, and $CZ$, with scalar gates when global phase matters.
Bian et al.~\cite{bian2026qudit} give sixteen rule schemes characterizing
these circuits at every width and odd prime. Their argument uses a
symplectic normal form, then restores Pauli and scalar corrections.
Checking the primitive equations establishes soundness. Completeness
also requires a normalization procedure, a derivation connecting each
input to its normal form, and a proof that different normal forms have
different meanings. Residual gates created in one normalization step
must be absorbed by later steps, while every forgotten correction must
be recovered at the appropriate semantic layer.

\paragraph{The proof problem and its solution.}
Our central proof-engineering question is how to certify normalization
without duplicating its case analysis in a separate proof that it
respects the equations. We implement the symplectic normalizer as a
tower of coset tables. Each entry returns a representative, a residual
word on fewer wires, and an equational certificate. Its well-definedness
is proved \emph{semantically}: the action on the bottom wire determines
the representative, and completeness at the smaller width identifies
the residuals. Completeness at width $n$ is thus an input to certifying the
normalizer at width $n+1$. The well-founded dependency is the key
proof-engineering result: semantic separation replaces the global
comparison of table executions, while local equational certificates
remain explicit.

Only this symplectic layer needs the large normalizer. The projective
semantics is the semidirect product
$\Zp^{2n}\rtimes\Sp$: a Pauli correction and a symplectic transformation.
We assemble its presentation from the two factors, checking their
interaction at the conjugation interface. A Weyl cocycle restores the
scalar phase, and a direct product supplies the sign in the original
rules. The resulting proof separates normalization, semantic uniqueness,
interaction laws, and changes of presentation. The expensive local
circuit derivations remain necessary; composition changes where their
results are used.

\subsection{Contribution and overlap}\label{sec:contributions}

This paper is the account of the formalization announced by the qupit
presentation~\cite{bian2026qudit}, whose version~2 already reports Agda
verification of the scalar-sensitive rules and refers to a companion
formalization manuscript. We do not claim a new mathematical rule set
or a verification independent of that shared development. The generic
library is also a separate contribution~\cite{bian2026framework}.

The methodological contribution is this division of obligations: local
syntax certifies each step, semantic observations identify its head,
and smaller-width completeness certifies the residual. The qupit case
establishes that the division survives both a large recursive table
and the recovery of information erased by quotient semantics.
The technical results are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A certified symplectic normalizer.}
  A doubly inductive normal form and certified coset tables give
  normalization. Head injectivity and completeness one width below
  establish well-definedness (Lemma~\ref{lem:semantic-wd});
  uniqueness and expressiveness give a presentation of $\Sp$
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:unique} and~\ref{thm:sp}).
\item \textbf{Projective completeness by composition.}
  The Pauli and symplectic presentations are combined and translated
  to the original fifteen non-scalar rules
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). The normalizer never carries Pauli corrections.
\item \textbf{Scalar-sensitive algebraic completeness.}
  Checked cocycle corrections and presentation translations recover
  the original sixteen rules. The scalar extension is connected to
  the structural Weyl model, yielding a certificate interface
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:structural} and~\ref{thm:figure1}). A checked
  two-qutrit client replaces twelve gates by six, including a necessary
  scalar correction, from a computed semantic equality.
\end{enumerate}

The presentation implementations check in safe Agda without postulates
or holes, uniformly in width and odd prime; the multiplier rules take a
primitive root and its generation proof as parameters. The semantic
targets are algebraic groups. Their identification with the specified
complex gate matrices, including the exact phase convention, remains
outside the checked development. Table~\ref{tab:targets} names the
targets, and \S\ref{sec:discussion} states the computational and
verification limits.
