Proving that a circuit normalizer respects an equational theory can
duplicate its case analysis. We show how semantic separation avoids
this duplication in the Agda formalization accompanying a recent
presentation of Clifford circuits in odd prime dimension. The normalizer is a tower
of certified coset tables. A semantic observation identifies each
representative; completeness one width below identifies its residual.
This turns smaller-instance completeness into a method for certifying
the next normalization step. A verified semidirect-product construction
adds Pauli corrections without extending the symplectic normalizer,
and a central extension restores scalar phases. Checked translations
recover the original circuit rules, with an interpretation into an
independently defined structural Weyl group. A certificate client uses
this interpretation to repair projectively valid replacements. A fully
checked two-qutrit example reduces twelve gates to six and three
entangling gates to one, retaining a nonzero scalar correction.
The presentation proofs check in safe Agda without postulates or holes,
uniformly in width and odd prime given a primitive-root witness.
The contribution is the proof architecture and its algebraic
certificate interface; the mathematical rule set and generic
composition library are companion work. Correspondence to the specified
complex gate matrices remains outside the checked development.
